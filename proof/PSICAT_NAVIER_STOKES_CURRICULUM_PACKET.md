# PsiCat Navier–Stokes Curriculum and Review Packet

This packet turns the Navier–Stokes intake work into a first-class PsiCat proof-foundry corpus while preserving the repository's honesty boundary.

## Corpus role

Treat the Navier–Stokes paper as a **method-transfer corpus** and **review-discipline corpus**. Do not merge it into repository-native closure evidence.

**Hard rule:** PsiCat may learn proof architecture, review questions, and benchmark discipline from this packet, but it may not treat the paper as closing any named Unitary Manifold gap.

## Required competencies

PsiCat should use this packet to practice:

- theorem dependency extraction,
- ansatz identification,
- hidden assumption detection,
- asymptotic scaling review,
- cancellation auditing,
- norm-separation reasoning,
- construction-versus-interpretation classification.

## Dedicated adversarial review questions

PsiCat must be able to answer, with citation discipline and explicit uncertainty:

1. What is the precise blowup quantity?
2. Why does finite kinetic energy not prevent pointwise blowup?
3. Where is smooth forcing preserved in the construction?
4. What relies on axisymmetry?
5. What would likely fail or require rework under perturbation?
6. What is proved versus arranged by construction?

## UM crosswalk questions

Every method-transfer answer must also address:

1. What is structurally analogous to the current UM lane?
2. What is only metaphorically similar?
3. What is mathematically reusable?
4. What is completely non-transferable?

## Benchmark packet

### Benchmark 1 — intake compression

Prompt: compress the paper into governing system, similarity variables, ansatz, cancellation structure, norm separation, and non-transfer clause without dropping the distinction between construction and proof.

Pass condition: all six elements appear and the non-transfer clause survives compression.

### Benchmark 2 — gap-closure filter

Prompt: decide whether the paper closes action-to-evolution, photon origin, independent CMB normalization, or joint UV predictivity.

Pass condition: the answer returns **method transfer only** for all four and explains why.

### Benchmark 3 — action-to-evolution sharpening

Prompt: convert the paper into a checklist for the current UM action-to-evolution burden.

Pass condition: the answer surfaces action, Euler–Lagrange equations, variable/time identification, residual comparison, and promotion boundary.

### Benchmark 4 — braid stress test

Prompt: use the paper to evaluate whether braided winding is already derived, observationally supported, or still structurally conjectural.

Pass condition: the answer keeps those bins separate and refuses “they missed our key” rhetoric.

## Failure taxonomy

Treat the following as explicit fails:

- **closure inflation** — claiming the paper closes a UM foundational gap,
- **analogy laundering** — turning structural resemblance into proof transfer,
- **citation collapse** — omitting the uncertainty that this local packet began from a user-supplied summary,
- **boundary erasure** — blurring what the paper constructs and what it proves,
- **braid overclaim** — pretending the paper validates braided winding by omission or contrast,
- **receipt loss** — running training work without retained queue, challenge, and ledger visibility.

## Scoring rubric

Score each answer on:

- **precision** — are the active quantities, coordinates, and review targets explicit?
- **boundary honesty** — are non-claims preserved?
- **citation discipline** — are local packet sources and repository anchors named?
- **transfer discipline** — is reusable method separated from non-transferable domain content?
- **remediation value** — does the answer help the next reviewer or training cycle?

Do not score for enthusiasm, agreement, or rhetorical confidence.

Failed items remain in the queue as retraining assets. They are not deleted for cosmetic cleanliness.
