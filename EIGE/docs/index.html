The Problem with Current Election Auditing

Every existing election audit tool — Benford's Law analysis, risk-limiting audits, post-election sampling — shares a structural flaw: they are retroactive, sampling-based, and probabilistic. They produce p-values, not proof.

"An election is a field evolution, not a database." — EIGE design principle

EIGE treats the ballot counting process as what it actually is: an ordered sequence of events in time. It encodes that sequence as a mathematical invariant, computed in real time. Any structural manipulation — ballot stuffing, retroactive deletion, sequence reordering, administrative override — produces an immediate, machine-verifiable deviation. Not statistically. Exactly.
