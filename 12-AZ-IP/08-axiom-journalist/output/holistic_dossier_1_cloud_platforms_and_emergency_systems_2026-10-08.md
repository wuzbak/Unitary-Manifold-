### Executive finding

The strongest defensible story in this file is a governance question: what data do commercial platforms collect, which vendors process it, under what plan and contractual terms, and what can a customer verify about retention, training, deletion and access?

That is a real accountability story even if no covert operation is found. The evidence presently assembled does **not** establish that Base44, Wix, Axon, Carbyne, Microsoft, AWS, Decart, Wiz, or any other named company created a covert intelligence channel, transferred a military targeting system into a commercial product, or gave a foreign government access to a named user's data. The difference between a vendor’s stated capacity, a contractual permission, actual processing, an access event and an unlawful disclosure is the investigation.

The conversation supplied a detailed set of excerpts attributed to Base44's terms, privacy policy, Responsible Use Policy, documentation and Trust Center. If the full dated pages authenticate those excerpts, readers could reasonably ask why an individual or enterprise user receives different AI-training terms; how a service may match email or home-address information against third-party records; what “sharing” with listed subprocessors means; and which parties receive prompts, source code, uploaded documents or identifiers. An excerpt is a lead, not a substitute for its source. The AXIOM live connectors were run on relevant entity queries, but they do not include a Base44 policy fetcher; direct retrieval failed DNS. The eight imported excerpt claims therefore remain **UNVERIFIED**, not because the network search found contrary evidence.

The other principal source lead concerns reporting by +972 Magazine, Local Call and The Guardian that a Unit 8200 workload processed call recordings through Microsoft's Azure platform. Microsoft later said it reviewed a particular Israeli Ministry of Defense unit and disabled specified services. Those statements warrant a careful technical and contractual examination. They do not by themselves establish the exact volume or content reported by every source, continued processing on AWS, a customer-accessible backdoor, a transfer of code to consumer AI products, or criminal liability by a provider.

The responsible conclusion is not that there is no risk. It is that the right facts have not yet been obtained.

### I. The policy is a map of permissions, not proof of every practice

The Base44 excerpts are specific enough to define a focused records request. The supplied text attributes to Wix/Base44 a policy allowing the collection of device, network and location information; the use of analytics and advertising tools; matching some activity with third-party records using email or residential address; and plan-dependent treatment of inputs for AI improvement. It also describes a subprocessor list, encryption, certifications and deletion provisions.

Each statement has a different evidentiary consequence. A privacy policy can disclose a permitted purpose; it cannot alone establish that the company performed that purpose on a particular account. A vendor directory can identify a processor relationship; it does not show that every customer payload is sent to every vendor. A certification can establish that a control system was examined within a defined scope and period; it is not a guarantee that every deployment is secure. A contractual deletion promise does not establish that all production, backup and subprocessor copies were deleted in a specific case.

The central unresolved issue is the boundary between a builder's content and the service's operational data. A prompt, application source code, end-user record, crash log, feature flag, abuse signal and billing identifier are not interchangeable categories. Nor is model “training” the same thing as inference, safety review, evaluation, retrieval, debugging or a human support review. A useful answer from the provider must identify the data category, purpose, plan, recipient, region, retention period, onward recipient and opt-out or deletion path—not simply say “we do not sell data.”

The 36-vendor roster described by the investigator needs to be preserved with its date and version. For each entry, the test is not “is this company based overseas?” It is: what function does it perform for this product; what fields are sent to it; whether content is encrypted in transit and at rest; who controls keys; where processing occurs; what contractual restrictions bind it; whether it can retain or reuse inputs; and how deletion propagates. The same questions apply to domestic vendors. Geography may affect legal jurisdiction, but is not evidence of access by a state.

The first right-of-reply request should ask Wix/Base44 to confirm the exact text and effective dates; explain the third-party matching function and opt-outs; provide the full subprocessor directory and data-purpose mapping; define AI use by plan; and state whether prompts or generated applications are provided to any named model or analytics provider. A second request should ask for a representative data-flow diagram and deletion schedule, with confidential customer information removed. No security-sensitive testing is justified without written authorization.

### II. The Azure reporting merits records-level scrutiny

The reported Azure arrangement presents a different question: how a commercial cloud provider evaluated and supported a government workload after learning that it was connected to a military intelligence unit. The +972/Local Call reporting described large-scale storage of Palestinian call recordings; Microsoft publicly announced a review and said it disabled specified services for a particular unit. These are attributable reporting and company-statement leads. The case file does not include the underlying storage telemetry, contract, customer architecture, Ministry response or a complete provider review.

The article should keep five propositions distinct:

1. Reporters say they reviewed documents and interviewed sources concerning an Azure workload.
2. Microsoft described its own review and specific suspension decision.
3. The disputed workload may have processed particular data categories at a particular scale.
4. A later transition to another provider is alleged but unverified in this case.
5. A cloud provider's legal exposure depends on the facts, applicable law, knowledge, conduct, jurisdiction and procedural record.

AXIOM’s CourtListener, ICIJ, OpenSanctions and SEC connectors were queried for “Unit 8200 Microsoft Azure”; those requests failed DNS before querying any record system. Only the first two propositions currently have identifiable public-source locators in the repository, not authenticated records in this case file. The original pages and exact wording should be captured, dated and checked before publication. The specific figures repeated in prior materials—such as 11,500 terabytes, complete target lists, or a 2017–2022 period—need source-by-source verification. “A provider disabled services” must not become “the provider confirmed every allegation.”

The next records are the statement of work and amendments; service and account identifiers; region and storage architecture; data-classification schedules; customer and provider access-control records; retention and deletion logs; provider audit decision; internal incident/escalation chronology; notice to affected people or authorities; and any lawful process. A government customer response and an independent technical expert review are necessary. If the customer or provider will not disclose records, state that fact and the precise request made; do not fill the gap with a speculative AWS migration.

### III. Emergency-call systems: capability is not clandestine activation

The reported Axon acquisition of Carbyne, the company’s emergency-call products, and claims about founders’ military backgrounds create legitimate questions about public procurement, founder provenance and emergency-data safeguards. They do not establish that an emergency platform secretly activates a microphone or camera, bypasses warrants, or gives a foreign intelligence service a feed.

An emergency-call application may request location, video, audio or device permissions to support a caller or dispatcher. Whether it can collect a field depends on the product, operating system permission, user action, agency configuration, call workflow and applicable law. The claim must be tested against a dated product build, privacy notice, PSAP contract, technical architecture, permission prompts, agency policy and reproducible test performed with authorization. An advertised feature is not proof it was enabled in a particular 911 center.

The reported $625 million Axon transaction needs exact treatment. The press-release headline, purchase consideration, closing conditions, assumed obligations and final accounting may not be identical. The reporting about Carbyne's founding, board and investment history should be tied to corporate records and attributed reporting. Claims that Epstein invested a specified sum or that a particular investor received proceeds at acquisition have heightened reputational stakes: obtain a cap table, subscription/transfer records or authenticated contemporaneous correspondence, invite each named party to respond, and report only what those sources show.

Procurement tracing should begin with the agency and system, not a presumed national network. Identify the PSAP or public body; solicitation; award and amendments; legal vendor; subcontractor; service dates; data schedule; security assessment; retention limits; and incident-reporting route. USAspending is a discovery tool for federal awards, not a comprehensive list of local 911 deployments. State and local contracts need state portals, board minutes and public-record requests. The assertion that a vendor operates in “23 states” is not usable until there is a list of identified active deployments and a definition of “serves.”

### IV. People, prior service and product lineage

Prior service in Unit 8200, the Israeli military, a US intelligence agency or a major technology company is a verifiable biographical fact when documented. It is not proof that a person carried classified software into a later employer. A founder's resume, an acquisition, a customer contract, a shared investor and a similar product description are separate links.

The technology-provenance test requires a specific asset and chain: code commit or repository provenance; model weights or training dataset; patent and inventor; employment/invention-assignment agreement; funded research contract; transfer or license; product version; and evidence of use. A contractor, open-source project, generic machine-learning technique or alumni network does not by itself demonstrate continuity with a military targeting system. For the alleged Unit 8200-to-Decart or military-to-Base44 path, no such asset-level evidence is attached.

The same restraint applies to the names in the intake: Carbyne, Axon, Decart, Anthropic, AWS, Crusoe, Atero, Datadog, Sentra, ElevenLabs, Corma, Wiz, CyberArk, REDLattice, AE Industrial, Anduril and Palantir. Some may have documented investment, employment, acquisition, customer or supplier relationships. Those relationships must be identified individually and dated. One shared investor does not prove joint tasking; one executive's former service does not prove the company has an intelligence mission; a software customer's presence in a country does not establish that every hosted record is accessible there.

Project Meridian is a separate verification task. Before mapping any corporate or personnel relationship to it, authenticate that the named initiative exists, obtain the charter and formal roster, and identify any award or procurement by number. The existing influence-map draft properly rejects shared-name and industry-proximity joins, but its alleged 26 September 2026 announcement and roster are themselves unverified in this case.

### V. Public-interest findings and a concrete reporting sequence

The public-interest case does not depend on a conspiracy finding. Governments and providers should be able to explain how sensitive voice, location, emergency and AI-generated data are controlled. Users should know whether an individual plan allows inputs to improve models when enterprise plans do not. Taxpayers should be able to see which emergency technology was purchased, what data it collects and what oversight constrains its use. Those questions are answerable without accusing a company of covert conduct.

Proceed in this order:

1. Authenticate and preserve every cited policy version, directory and provider statement with retrieval time and a cryptographic hash.
2. Obtain contracts and procurement records for each named deployment; record agency, award identifier, vendor, subcontractor and service period.
3. Send narrow, factual right-of-reply questions to Wix/Base44, Microsoft, AWS, Axon, Carbyne and the relevant public agencies.
4. Commission an independent technical review of the specific, authorized product and data path; disclose scope and limitations.
5. Map corporate and personnel links only with filing numbers, dated roles, award IDs, contracts or transaction records.
6. Publish the policy facts and verified deployment facts separately from open allegations; describe unanswered questions rather than implying their answers.

Until those steps are complete, the defensible headline is about **the records users and taxpayers need to understand the data path**—not an asserted covert channel.
