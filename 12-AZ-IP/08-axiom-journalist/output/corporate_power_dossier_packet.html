Corporate Power Concentration — Public-Record Accountability Ledger

Status: HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED

Legal risk: CONTROLLED

Lead

Public institutional reporting across competition, wealth, labor, lobbying, climate, and digital infrastructure shows that concentrated corporate power now behaves as a governance problem rather than only an economic one.

Claims

  1. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — Corporate concentration now functions as a democratic-governance problem because market power migrates into lawmaking, standards, and public administration. [1] [2]
  2. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — Extreme wealth concentration creates command power rather than mere consumption inequality by buying access, endurance, and rule-shaping capacity. [4] [5] [6]
  3. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — Federal lobbying and procurement dependence narrow public bargaining power even when formal legal sovereignty remains intact. [7] [1]
  4. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — AI deployment is intensifying existing concentration because model access, cloud infrastructure, and digital gatekeeping are already controlled by a small number of firms. [3] [12]
  5. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — Private-equity rollups in essential sectors can transfer risk downward into care quality, housing security, and local civic resilience. [13] [14]
  6. CORROBORATED (tier 4/5) — Climate accountability is concentrated enough that producer-side corporate responsibility remains visible even when harms are socialized onto the public. [8] [9]
