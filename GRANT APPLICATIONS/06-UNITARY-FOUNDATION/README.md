# Unitary Foundation Quantum Microgrants

**Status:** funder FAQ says applicants may be from any country and need no credentials; projects should leverage or support quantum technology for human benefit. It prefers projects completable in 3–6 months. A $4,000 microgrant amount is reported by a third-party listing; verify the current official amount, application window, and terms before relying on it.

The official FAQ says 50% of an awarded grant is sent when the project starts and the remainder after a midpoint check-in. It does not publish an award-processing timeline or a specific grant amount. Do not treat the reported $4,000 or the payment schedule as a promise of fast funding.

## Proposed project angle

**Working title:** *Reproducible Evaluation of an Open Quantum-Ansatz Software Component.*

Build a narrowly scoped, open and reproducible benchmark around the existing early-stage quantum software lane (for example, a quantum ansatz tool). Compare against a well-defined baseline using simulators and, if available, approved quantum hardware access. Publish methods, code, and null/negative results. The proposal must explain a specific quantum-technology contribution; the Unitary Manifold theory alone is not sufficient.

## Draft project summary

This project will improve [specific open quantum tool] by implementing and evaluating [one concrete feature] against [baseline] on [defined problem set]. In 3–6 months, we will produce a documented package, reproducible test and benchmark suite, and a short report describing where the approach works and where it does not. We will not claim quantum advantage or hardware performance without suitable experimental evidence.

## Application checks

- Confirm current call, award size, eligible payee, payment method, and IP/open-publication expectations.
- Keep scope deliverable within the funder's preferred 3–6 months.
- Define a baseline, data set, compute/hardware budget, and independent review before requesting funds.
- Verify the exact code license and whether generated contributions require disclosure.
