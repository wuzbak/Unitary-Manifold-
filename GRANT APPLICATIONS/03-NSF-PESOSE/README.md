# NSF PESOSE — Pathways to Enable Secure Open-Source Ecosystems

**Solicitation:** NSF 26-506. **Status:** active program; the past September 2026 cycle is over. The next full-proposal deadline is **March 2, 2027** (first Tuesday in March, annually thereafter); reconfirm the date, track-specific ceilings, and instructions in the official solicitation before scheduling.

## Why consider it

PESOSE supports planning, building, and securing sustainable open-source ecosystems. The themes match AxiomZero's open-science and responsible-AI purpose. This program is not simply a large software-development grant: reviewers need a credible ecosystem with users, contributors, governance, sustainability, and security needs.

## Proposed project angle

**Working title:** *A Sustainable, Secure Open-Source Governance and Scientific-Software Ecosystem.*

Start with one existing public product, likely EIGE or the scientific software interface, and choose a PESOSE track based on the current solicitation. A defensible project would establish external maintainer/user governance, secure development and release practices, contributor onboarding, documented interfaces, reproducible tests, and a sustainability model. Do not treat the repository's internal test volume as an ecosystem or assert that software is widely adopted without evidence.

## Track and budget gate

NSF 26-506 sets maximum budgets of **$300,000 for up to one year** for Track 1 and **$1.5 million for up to two years** for Tracks 2 and 3. The solicitation requires **three to five letters of collaboration** from third-party users and/or contributors of the open-source product; the letters must come from current users or contributors who are not directly related to the proposing team. The repository does not establish whether AxiomZero can obtain qualifying letters. Treat that as a go/no-go gate, not as evidence that users or contributors do or do not exist. No budget or match assumptions have been made.

## Draft concept summary

We propose to [plan/build/secure] an open-source ecosystem around [specific project] that addresses [demonstrated public or national need]. The work will organize an external community of [identified user/contributor groups], define transparent governance and maintenance responsibilities, and implement a security and sustainability plan. Milestones will be measured by [external adoption/contribution metrics], [security/release metrics], and [maintenance response metrics]. The work will publish findings, including limitations and unresolved risks.

## Gating evidence

- Inventory verifiable current users/contributors for the selected open-source product; the solicitation-required letters must be from current third-party users or contributors, not merely prospective contacts.
- If three to five eligible letter writers cannot be confirmed and agree to participate, do not submit; do not represent prospective interest as current use or contribution.
- Create a baseline for external contributors, issues, releases, downloads/deployments, and current governance.
- Establish a security threat model, vulnerability-reporting process, dependency inventory, release controls, and remediation plan.
- Confirm that the proposed open-source license and funder terms are compatible.
- Ask NSF PESOSE program staff whether an SPC is eligible as lead and confirm the next due date.
