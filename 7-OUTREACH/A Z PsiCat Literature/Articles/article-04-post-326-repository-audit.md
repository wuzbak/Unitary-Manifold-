Post 326 read like a broad-spectrum inventory: code volume, test surfaces, formal assets, API routes, product lanes, and replacement-value framing.

The useful part is not the magnitude of the numbers. The useful part is that the audit ties claims to method and source surfaces. Counts are only meaningful when readers can reproduce where they came from and what they include.

For expert audiences, this is the key distinction:

- **Scale claims without method** are marketing.
- **Scale claims with method and scope limits** are infrastructure.

The article mostly landed on the second side, and that is worth preserving in this rewrite.

It also carried an important caveat: static inventories and declared ledgers do not collapse into one-to-one semantics automatically. A token scan and a governance ledger can both be correct while measuring different objects. Good audits say that out loud. Great audits make it impossible to miss.

In short, post 326 is strongest when read as a transparency exercise, not a triumph narrative.
