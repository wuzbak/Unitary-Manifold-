The AI safety conversation usually operates at one of two levels. At the policy level, it concerns regulations, deployment restrictions, and institutional guidelines. At the technical level, it concerns alignment methods, interpretability research, and capability thresholds. Both levels are real and important.

There is a third level — the architectural level — that receives less attention. This is the level of system design: how are human judgment, AI capability, and real-world action connected, and what happens to the system when those connections degrade? This is the level the Unitary Pentad addresses.

Current AI systems are trusted or distrusted based on policy, reputation, and intuition. When a user or institution trusts an AI system to perform a task, that trust is expressed as: a permission is granted, and the AI acts.
