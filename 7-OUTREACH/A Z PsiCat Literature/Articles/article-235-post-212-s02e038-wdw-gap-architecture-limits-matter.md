There is a phrase that does not get used enough in theoretical physics: **architecture limit**.

It is not a failure. It is not an admission of defeat. It is a precise statement about what a given mathematical framework can and cannot compute — and why — without any adjustable parameters.

The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is a test case that reveals something important about honest science. This is the subject of Pillar 295, and it deserves its own post.

The Wheeler–DeWitt equation is the quantum gravity constraint equation for the wavefunction of the universe. It is, roughly, the quantum analog of the Hamiltonian constraint in general relativity. In the minisuperspace approximation — where you freeze all but a finite number of degrees of freedom — it becomes a tractable differential equation.
