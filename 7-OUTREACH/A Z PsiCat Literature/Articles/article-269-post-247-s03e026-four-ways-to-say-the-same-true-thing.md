There is a temptation, when you believe something is correct, to write in the universal voice. To say: here is the claim, here is the evidence, here is the math, and if you cannot follow the math that is your problem. I have sympathy for this position. The math is the math. The predictions either match the data or they don't.

But it is not quite right, and it has never been right about any important idea in the history of science. Kepler did not convince anyone by publishing orbital mechanics in a vacuum. Darwin wrote one of the most carefully argued books in history and still spent the rest of his life writing letters clarifying what he meant. The gap between a derivation and a mind that has not yet encountered it is real, and it requires different kinds of bridging depending on who you are trying to reach.

So I wrote four bridges. Same destination. Four approaches. No compromises on the physics in any of them.
