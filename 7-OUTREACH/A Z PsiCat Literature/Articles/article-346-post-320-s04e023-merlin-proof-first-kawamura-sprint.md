---

## Method discipline

The sprint scope stayed deliberately narrow:

- one active burden,
- one machine-readable burden ledger,
- one dual-loop review path (human/Copilot and Merlin),
- one verdict regime: closure earned, burden reduced, or still open.

Anything outside that frame would increase noise without increasing evidence.

---

## Merlin’s role

Merlin was used as a challenger, not an approver.

Tasks were bounded to adversarial pressure:

- alternative derivation attempts,
- counterexample search,
- hidden-assumption surfacing,
- provenance reconciliation,
- closure-language blocking when burden class is unchanged.

This is the right use of an assistant in a proof-first lane.

---

## What actually survived

Cross-audit retained four useful guardrails:

- residual-open status remains explicit,
- traceability objects are not equated with closure,
- cross-loop verdict convergence is required,
- external-import boundaries remain visible.

These are meaningful rigor gains.
They are not the decisive proof object.
