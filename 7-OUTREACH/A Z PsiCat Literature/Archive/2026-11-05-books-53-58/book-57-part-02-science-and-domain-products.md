## A5 — Research, Reflection, and the Craft of Making Films

### Product 08: AxiomZero Journalist AI

AxiomZero Journalist AI—often styled AXIOM in its product guide—is an investigative research workbench built around a newsroom principle: the journalist judges; the tool organizes the work that makes judgment possible. It is not described as an article writer or a publish button. A user begins with an investigative lead, opens a local case, maps people and organizations, records sources, attaches claims to evidence, and generates a brief that remains explicitly for human review.

The strongest part of that flow is the claim ledger. Sources are classified by tier, while a claim can be marked confirmed, corroborated, alleged, or unverified according to the sources attached to it. “Confirmed” in this software vocabulary is not an external certification; it is a product rule applied to the evidence entries in a case. Legal-risk flags—privacy, source protection, libel exposure, national-security concerns—are reminders for review, not legal advice. A source excerpt and reference can be inspected with the claim, contradictions can be logged, open questions remain visible, and the report can carry an uncertainty-aware account of its evidence path.

For example, a reporter receives a tip about a contract. They create a case, add entities and the allegation, log a primary filing and an on-record statement, attach the documents to claims, and record a contradictory statement or missing record. AXIOM can help produce an investigative brief and a governed dossier packet that summarizes confidence, source tiers, and risk markers. Its source-scan facility can produce a manifest for a catalog of public-record systems and has adapters for a limited subset. The README differentiates `COMPLETED`, `PARTIAL`, and `NOT_CONFIGURED`; a completed adapter with no returned hit does not demonstrate that no relevant record exists. That is a crucial negative-evidence safeguard.

The current product describes local SQLite case storage, a transparent research trail, and structured source-led handoffs. Some public-record scanning uses network access; the older claim that all data stays local would be too broad for the current README. The platform is not a general web crawler, a complete fact-checker, or an author of publishable copy. It does not replace source relationships, editor judgment, legal counsel, or the reporter's sense of context. Its more novel development lies in translating evidence practices into a traceable workflow and exporting training/publication packets without intentionally dropping uncertainty. Such a packet is not proof that its recipient read or obeyed the source ledger.

An appropriate use is internal organization of a case and preparation for editor review, with staff verifying every document, quote, calculation, and identity. An inappropriate use is to publish a generated confidence label as if two records automatically made a claim true, to construe an incomplete search as exoneration or guilt, or to let a model generate an accusation and treat its fluency as sourcing. Input: lead, records, sources, entities, claims, notes. Processing: local storage, linkage, heuristic scoring, configured source lookup, and report assembly. Output: case records and a review packet. The person remains accountable for the investigative conclusion.

The source-tier weights and claim categories are best understood as queueing and editorial prompts: they help make uneven records visible and can keep a case from presenting every lead as equally supported. A Tier-1 filing is still relevant only to the proposition it actually states, and two sources may not be independent if one simply copied the other. The source relation is therefore more important than a number generated by a rubric. A solid newsroom workflow would verify the original document, record its date and context, separate a source's assertion from an independently checked fact, and preserve the exact excerpt and link. AXIOM can help maintain that ledger; it cannot decide whether a source is authentic or whether the excerpt was quoted fairly without people doing that checking.

### Product 09: OmegaHolon Engine

OmegaHolon takes a different form of evidence: not documents about a public event, but a person's own structured reflection. It is a personal life-coherence tool with five domains—body and health, mind and emotion, work and purpose, relationships and trust, resources and environment. Users assign each domain an epistemic status, describe conditions and constraints, list open gaps, record a trust or authenticity estimate, and define a falsifiable commitment about what would change their mind. A daily pulse and a decision form add time-series and comparison features. Local SQLite storage supports profiles, audits, decisions, reports, and history.

The decision workflow accepts a question and up to three options, with user-entered impacts on each life domain. The product then weights those impacts according to the chosen domain statuses and trust parameters and ranks the options. A person might compare moving for a job with staying near family, encoding the effects they currently anticipate and then examining the report for the tradeoffs it makes visible. The “output” is a structured reflection prompt or ranking, not a recommendation from an empirically validated mental-health model. It should provoke a conversation with oneself or a trusted adviser, not overrule personal agency.

The central caution is that the mathematics is a borrowed scoring structure. Equations like `omega_score = stability_floor × average_resonance` give a reproducible procedure, but the result is only as meaningful as the inputs, weights, and measurement model. A numerical “crisis” or “unified” grade does not diagnose a person, establish a clinical condition, or measure an objective physical state. The product README states the tool does not require the physics to be correct: it borrows epistemic statuses, thresholds, coupling structures, and the practice of writing falsifiable commitments. That separation is healthy. It also means the physics framework does not validate the life score.

The product's novel practical contribution is a structured alternative to a simple habit tracker. It asks what a person believes is well-founded, what they do not know, what constrains action, and what observation could test a plan. Longitudinal records can make a change in self-assessment visible, but they cannot by themselves tell us whether the scale is reliable or whether a rise in score corresponds to improved well-being. Treat the dashboard as a journal with arithmetic, not an authority on a person's life. For sensitive information, consider that local storage is still data storage: protect the device and its backups, and do not enter details that a user cannot safely retain.

One implemented helper makes the scoring mechanics especially clear. It gathers numeric metrics, chooses a scale of 10 if any number exceeds 1 (otherwise 1), clamps normalized values into [0,1], computes their mean and population variance, then combines mean with a balance term: `0.7 × mean + 0.3 × [1/(1 + variance × φ × 4)]`. For illustrative inputs `[8, 6, 8]`, the numbers become normalized values `[0.8, 0.6, 0.8]`; the implementation returns a bounded score based on both their average and spread. That is a reproducible scoring rule. It is not a validated psychometric scale, and its behavior changes if the caller mixes units, passes missing values, or records self-ratings inconsistently. Because the formula rewards balance as well as mean, it may assign a higher value to a uniformly mediocre profile than the user expects. A responsible product conversation should expose the formula and invite the user to question it, rather than hide it behind a single Ω badge.

### Product 10: Filmer's Companion

Filmer's Companion is a dual-platform production assistant, with a Python desktop service and an Android application. Its scope is unusually broad but practical: script import/export, scene parsing, revision metadata, department breakdowns, scheduling, call sheets, cinematography, location tracking, finance, accounting, marketing, post-production, and delivery. A user can bring in a screenplay in text, Fountain, or Final Draft XML form; the software can organize characters and scenes into prep materials; the production team can attach locations, budgets, tasks, approvals, assets, and review notes; and a producer-facing dashboard can surface blockers.

Imagine an independent production preparing a ten-day shoot. A script enters Script Studio; scene and character parsing seed department breakdowns; the schedule arranges strips and tracks company moves; cast-linked elements build a day-out-of-days view; the AD module drafts call sheets and checks overnight turnaround; finance tracks budget categories and burn rate; locations surfaces permits or unconfirmed locations. During post, assets and timecoded review notes can be tracked, and exports can hand a timeline to another editing tool. The input is production data; processing combines deterministic parsers, calculators, database state, and optionally an AI agent; the output is a collection of working production artifacts, warnings, and interchange files.

The product's own architecture shows a resolver chain: remote model, then local Ollama, then a static knowledge base. That means “assistant” is not synonymous with a continuously available live model; offline use may depend on the deterministic/static path, while model-specific output varies with configuration. The README reports a large set of tests but does not make that count a substitute for testing a real production's workflow, labor agreements, insurance, location permits, or accounting obligations. The guild-aware knowledge base is a convenience; union minima and workplace rules change and must be checked against authoritative current sources.

Filmer's Companion's strongest novel development is operational breadth. It tries to connect script, people, schedule, money, locations, and delivery rather than offering one isolated calculator. That breadth makes integration quality and migration more important: teams should test the exact imports, exports, permissions, backup, database lifecycle, and failure recovery they plan to use. It does not guarantee a film can be produced safely, legally, on budget, or on time. Lighting calculators can demonstrate an inverse-square calculation, but they do not measure a particular fixture on a particular set; a schedule warning can flag a short turnaround, but a human production manager still determines compliant action. Use it as a coordination surface, not as the production's accountable decision-maker.

## A6 — Soil, Rock, and the Many Meanings of Prediction

### Product 11: Terra OS

Terra OS is documented as a soil-and-water expert system: a FastAPI application with text retrieval, specialist routing, APIs for analysis and advice, and SQLite/FTS5 storage. TerraBot retrieves from soil and water documentation; TerraGovernor routes among soil analysis, water chemistry, agronomy advice, ecology, and remediation roles. The system can accept a question, soil analysis request, or water sample; retrieve relevant knowledge; and return an informational answer or structured API result. The README also describes USDA soil lookup helpers, GeoJSON export, and a φ-field ecology-coupling proxy.

A practical workflow might begin with a field observation and a soil profile: submit the available texture, nutrient, drainage, or water-chemistry details; ask the relevant endpoint for analysis or guidance; then inspect the sources and follow up with laboratory tests or a qualified local specialist. A GeoJSON export can make observations easier to move into mapping software. This can lower the friction of organizing environmental information, especially when users have multiple profiles or samples. The processing is retrieval plus application logic; it is not an instrument that independently measures soil or water.

The source description supports a useful expert-system interface, but not a claim that every response is scientifically validated or site-specific enough for high-consequence remediation. Data quality, region, sensor calibration, lab methods, contaminant identity, and local regulations matter. An answer from a WaterChemist route is not a certified potable-water determination. A remediation suggestion is not an approved cleanup plan. The product's own named roles are a routing taxonomy, not five independent credentialed experts. Similarly, a coupling proxy tied to a Unitary Manifold pillar is an adjacent software construct; it does not prove that the physical interpretation is correct or that the algorithm's output is an observed ecological quantity.

The novel work is domain-specific routing and storage around a compact environmental knowledge workflow, with open-data helper paths that can be used to connect information sources. The app can aid triage, recordkeeping, and exploration; users should document provenance and validate consequential measurements. It should not be used by itself to decide that drinking water is safe, that land is remediated, or that a contaminant is absent. Those decisions require appropriate sampling, specialist review, and jurisdictional standards. Treat an output as a lead to evidence, not the evidence itself.

There is a concrete implementation detail an operator should know before treating a coordinate result as a map reading. The USDA helper makes a bounded two-second request and normalizes a response, but if the request or parse fails its fallback derives broad soil labels and pH/organic-matter estimates from latitude and longitude using a few thresholds and sinusoidal functions. The result explicitly appends that a fallback pedology estimate was used. This deterministic fallback helps a demo or an offline user see the response shape; it is not a soil survey observation and should not be used as if USDA returned it. The remediation helper is also a rules table: named contaminants map to predefined steps and confidence constants, then the soil-texture keyword selects a first handling note. That is an explainable prototype rule path, not a site-specific transport model. A good test case should verify both provenance and fallback labeling, not merely assert that the endpoint returns a non-empty object.

### Product 12: Lithos OS

Lithos OS pairs naturally with Terra OS in the repository's Earth-science storyline, but the current scope is narrower than its “crust diagnostic loop” metaphor. It offers mineral identification by text description against a local catalog, formation context from a knowledge base, gemstone guidance through retrieval or optional language-model agents, and a measured-data adapter for public earthquake catalog entries from USGS ComCat. Its README expressly says image-based identification remains an offline stub and no trained image model ships. It also withdraws earlier language about occurrence mapping from a geological model and says the product does not provide geothermal resource assessment, earthquake prediction, or seismic-hazard estimates.

In the documented live-data path, a user supplies coordinates and query bounds; Lithos validates them, fetches GeoJSON from the named USGS catalogue, checks the response schema, caches a valid result, and returns event records with source and retrieval provenance. If the network fails, a cached or stale result can be shown with its real status, or the product reports that data is unavailable. The adapter's constraints on coordinates, query duration, response size, and timeout protect the workflow from malformed or overlarge responses. The tests use injected fixtures and do not test live connectivity. This is a useful distinction: the data may be measured in the sense that it records catalogued events, while the adapter itself still requires correct interpretation and real-world context.

The second workflow is a local mineral lookup. A person describes a sample, searches catalog terms, and receives candidate matches or formation context. That is not equivalent to a lab-grade mineral identification; image classification is not currently present, and gemstone grading is advisory rather than an automated assessment of color, clarity, cut, or carat. Candidate rankings should lead to confirmatory methods such as spectroscopy or expert inspection, not to an unsupported valuation or resource decision.

Lithos and Terra share retrieval ideas, and their product guides describe an Android companion relationship. But a “suite” label does not mean every service or device is deployed as one integrated, tested system. The novel practical layer is in bounded environmental data retrieval, explicit fresh/cached/stale/unavailable provenance, local catalog search, and an API that can feed another tool. The geology analogy to the Unitary Manifold is a conceptual view, not an empirical connection established by the module. This product is a field-information aid, not a geological oracle.

The USGS query path returns more than a bare number: a validated event record is associated with its source, query URL, source-generated time, retrieval time, age, cache flag, and adapter version. Query limits for radius, number of days, magnitude, event count, and response size are checked before the fetch; a stale result is distinguishable from a fresh one. For a field researcher, that provenance makes a map layer auditable: “these catalogued events were returned for this bounded place and interval at this time,” not “the site is safe” or “the next event is predicted.” Automatic solutions may later be revised, and catalogue completeness varies regionally. Even the term “measured” refers to events in the public catalogue, not Lithos physically measuring seismic motion.

The specific weakness is important because neighboring modules can look more authoritative when placed together. Mineral-catalog matches and seismicity are not linked to the same geologic inference in the current README; site events do not establish the identity or economic value of a rock specimen. Terra's coordinate-derived fallback and Lithos' live event adapter also have different provenance and evidentiary status. A combined map would need to retain those distinctions in its layer legends and exported records. Combining two sources is a technical feature; it is not automatically a stronger conclusion.

### Product 13: Delphi

Delphi is a five-channel hypothesis-exploration application. Its channels include Tarot, Runes, Astrology, Chinese Zodiac, and a hypothesis explorer for falsifiable Unitary Manifold claims. The juxtaposition is unusual, so its README's epistemic disclaimer deserves to be read before the feature list: the product says symbolic channels are not facts, outputs carry explicit statuses, open tests and documented gaps remain visible, and human judgment stays in the loop. It is an exploration surface rather than an authority.

The workflow depends on which channel the user selects. In a symbolic channel, a question or selected prompt is passed through a reading or interpretive template and returned as reflective framing. That can help a person articulate assumptions, make alternatives visible, or generate a narrative prompt. It does not produce a scientifically validated prediction about the person's future. In the hypothesis channel, the user searches or browses a registry of claims and status labels, then follows a claim toward the stated falsifier or evidence. The first is a symbolic prompt; the second is a repository-navigation feature. They are not evidentially interchangeable.

The visible API exposes reading generation, horoscopes, zodiac information, and search, with a database and tests documented in the product guide. That establishes a working software surface, not that any divinatory claim is empirically supported. A safe use is entertainment, journaling, or a structured conversation starter, with clear awareness of the symbolic status. A useful research use is to locate a documented hypothesis and inspect its formal status and evidence path. A poor use is to make medical, financial, legal, or other high-stakes choices based on an astrological or tarot reading, or to present a generated reading as evidence for a physical theory.

The novel element is the coexistence of multiple prompt styles with an epistemic channel that can route a reader toward falsifiable claims. It offers different modes of thinking, not a unified predictive instrument. The scientific challenge remains separate: if a claim is in Delphi's hypothesis registry, it needs to be checked against its source module, data, and the current truth layer. The software's presence does not upgrade the claim. As with all products here, a passing test confirms behavior of the tested code paths, not the truth of the propositions those paths display.

The hypothesis-scoring helper illustrates why the distinction is operational, not merely philosophical. `explore_hypothesis` selects one of a fixed set of channels, searches the query text for terms such as “falsify,” “contradict,” “outside,” “fail,” or “tension,” and can switch a status to OPEN when such a token is present. It returns the supplied text, channel identifier, fixed supporting and contradicting pillar lists, a confidence-interval string, and a status. This can act as a rapid exploration scaffold; it is not a statistical calculation of a confidence interval from sample data. A sentence that uses “outside” in a harmless way may trigger the same heuristic, and a counterexample phrased without the watched words may not. The label `confidence_interval` should therefore not be quoted as a measured probability. A reader can input a claim, inspect the returned registry links, then verify the source and status independently; the tool helps navigate but does not reason from new evidence as a Bayesian estimator would.

A future improvement could separate symbolic output from repository evidence in the type system, attach dated source provenance, and expose the status rule. Those are design directions, not present features; for now, treat the output as a navigation prompt rather than a verdict.

## A7 — Message Over Sound; Sensors on a Phone

### Product 14: SDAM

The canonical registry calls Product 14 “SDAM” and gives its descriptor as a Spatial Decision Awareness Module, while the product README is titled “Software-Defined Acoustic Modem.” Those two names point in different directions. The implementation and current detailed README describe an Android acoustic messaging application, so that is the scope described here; the catalog wording is a metadata ambiguity to resolve in a later editorial pass, not a reason to silently invent a second working product.

The documented current path sends text over a speaker/microphone acoustic link. The Android app uses AudioRecord and AudioTrack around a ggwave v0.4.2/JNI codec; messages are encrypted with AES-GCM, then framed with an HMAC-SHA256 authenticator and sequence/counter information. The README describes fragmentation and reassembly limits, a separate legacy handshake format, and replay rejection whose high-water mark exists only in memory. In a single-device or compatible test setup, the input text is divided into bounded pieces, each encrypted and wrapped into an authenticated frame, acoustically encoded, received, authenticated, and reassembled before a success report. The sender's “queued” status is not a delivery receipt; the receiver can report success only after full authenticated reassembly.

The README does not hide the deployment gaps. The native library may not be packaged; if it is unavailable, the app must report the audio path as unavailable instead of claiming a successful transmission. JVM tests exercise deterministic frame encoding, encryption round-trips, tamper rejection, HMAC verification, replay behavior within a process, and payload limits, but they do not emulate audio hardware. Speaker/microphone loopback, range, spectrum, cross-device key provisioning, and device-specific response are unvalidated. A shared key is necessary for two devices to communicate securely, but the product does not yet provide a working peer-key provisioning flow. A frequency label such as “ultrasound” is not proof that the emitted signal is inaudible, contained, or safe.

The useful near-term case is controlled development or demonstration of authenticated encrypted text framing over a local acoustic channel, with devices and keys intentionally provisioned for a test. It is not an air-gap security product ready for a sensitive facility. Acoustic transport does not make a phone RF-invisible, prevent sound transmission through walls, or substitute for facility approval. The README explicitly labels data-diode, industrial telemetry, and mesh use as design targets or unimplemented functions. Its novel development is a real Android crypto/framing path integrated with audio APIs, while the signal behavior, packaging, radio exposure, and operational security remain to be tested. The Unitary Manifold connection is stated as conceptual context; no validated derivation links its equations to ggwave's codec or error correction.

Product 15's browser companion has a simplified acoustic link, not Product 14's native ggwave codec; the two implementations and their tests remain distinct.

At the protocol level, the product makes a useful layered separation: message framing is not the same as acoustic modulation, and authentication is not the same as encryption. The documented SDAM1 path places encrypted content into message fragments, adds framing metadata and an HMAC for authenticated frame handling, then hands the data to the codec. The receiver decodes a frame, checks authentication and sequence state, reassembles the message, and only then decrypts and reports completion. A random grouping ID separates concurrent or newly observed message sets; the counter helps reject replay during a running process, but the README says that replay high-water state resets on restart. That limitation changes the threat model. A test proving that a captured frame is rejected twice in one process does not prove that it remains rejected after app restart or on another receiver.

For researchers evaluating the prototype, a meaningful next test matrix would vary message length, fragment loss and reordering, corrupted tags, duplicate counters, restart boundaries, codec unavailability, device model, room acoustics, and key mismatch. The current JVM tests cover selected crypto/frame cases; the physical and cross-device dimensions remain separate evidence requirements. This describes a rigorous next step, not an assertion that the matrix has been run.

### Product 15: Unitary Pentacorder

Pentacorder is an Android sensor dashboard and research-visualization project, marked in its current guide as in development, not validated, and not release-ready. It reads selected device inputs where Android, hardware, and permission allow; several screens transform these into app-defined values or labels associated with concepts in the Unitary Manifold. The guide is explicit that an accelerometer does not thereby measure a metric perturbation, a gyroscope does not measure a Levi-Civita connection, a magnetometer does not measure a Kaluza–Klein gauge field, and a phone's ambient light sensor does not measure the cosmic microwave background. Those are display analogies, not sensor claims.

The app's tabs combine a dashboard, medical-themed information, transmit controls, sensor visualization, translation, and experimental labs. The input can be a supported sensor stream, a manually entered vital, an available camera or location API, or demonstration data; processing includes app-defined transformations, informational calculators such as NEWS2, optional assistant retrieval, and experimental image/sensor features. The output is a screen, chart, notification, or assistant response. Since permissions and hardware vary, the same app may display different available data on different devices. A “5D” state vector or coherence score can be a visual synthesis of those inputs without becoming a physical measurement.

The medically named screens require especially careful interpretation. The README calls the health features non-diagnostic and says the application is not a medical device or substitute for clinical care. Some camera-based pulse and skin-screen features are experimental and depend on camera, light, device, and user conditions. NEWS2 calculations can be useful educationally, but clinical context, correct vital measurements, and trained interpretation matter. SOS-style controls are not a verified emergency service, and a dashboard alert is not a validated monitoring or decision-support system. Do not rely on it for diagnosis, triage, or emergency response.

There is a second, distinct field-scanner companion in `public-site/az-apps/15-pentacorder.html`. It attempts to use browser APIs for motion, geolocation, microphone, camera, and other hardware where supported. Some features report unavailable because browsers do not expose the corresponding sensor. Its simplified single-tone-per-nibble acoustic demo is not the native ggwave Android codec. Generated field reports package readings; they do not certify measurement accuracy. The repository README warns that behavior has not been validated across devices.

Pentacorder's originality lies in combining multiple device APIs, visualizations, and an assistant interface into one exploratory field dashboard. What it is not is an instrument that verifies a theory, a clinical tool, a reliable emergency beacon, or a finished native acoustic modem. A responsible workflow is to check the hardware and permission status, record the raw input and device, label transformations, compare any meaningful measurement with a calibrated independent instrument, and treat the visualization as a hypothesis or interface demonstration. Its physics-inspired labels help tell a story; they do not constitute a measurement program.

## A8 — Connections Without Conflation

The fifteen products tell a portfolio story, but the story is not a single pipeline. Its clearest connective tissue is a family of operations: record and retrieve information, route a task, compute a transformation, preserve status, and hand off a report or artifact. EIGE's audit bundles, UM-SOS's registries, AXIOM's case ledger, Terra and Lithos' environmental records, Filmer's production database, and Pentacorder's field displays each make information more structured. This is a real software pattern. The domain-specific validity of each item is still a separate question.

Product 01's memory and task routing may help a user inspect repository material; Product 20's Navigator, discussed here only as an interface connection, exposes local tools and its own persona/security logic. Its source includes a serious-versus-storyteller mode, status labels, and rules against presenting missing context as certainty. Product 08 describes governed training/publication packets, and Product 10 has configurable remote, local, and static knowledge routes. These are possible seams for future interoperability. They are not proof that a session in this book ran a live Product 20 inference, that an external council exists, or that a chain of software handoffs is deployed end to end. In this writing, local RAG was built and queried directly from repository source; no online PsiCat endpoint or Base44 infrastructure was contacted.

The shared library is one integration seam; adapters and exported files are others. A useful integration test should state the producer, consumer, schema/version, failure behavior, provenance fields, and whether the test is offline or live. A JSON artifact that validates against a schema proves its shape, not the truth of its contents. A cached USGS record needs freshness and source labels. A preregistration digest detects changes to bytes, but does not establish a trusted timestamp. A case confidence score is a software rule, not an editor's independent finding. An AI handoff retains only the evidence that the interface preserves; it cannot guarantee downstream attention.

The shared package offers more than a common folder name. `AZIPConfig` collects environment-based settings for the AxiomZero and UM-SOS URLs, JWT configuration, logging, storage, and optional observability. `EngineResult` standardizes fields such as engine name/version, `ok`, elapsed time, cache-hit flag, HILS approval, epistemic label, and a short result hash. The abstract `Engine.run` refuses an explicitly unapproved task, caches results by a hash of the JSON-serialized inputs, and optionally records Prometheus counters and latency. `AuthenticatedHTTPClient` supplies bearer headers and a bounded exponential-backoff retry loop when `httpx` is available. `pentad_classify` posts a task summary to a governance endpoint and returns `UNCLASSIFIED` if the request fails. Those are reusable contracts that can make multiple small engines more consistent.

They also show why shared plumbing needs explicit failure tests. A cache key that hashes raw keyword arguments does not know whether two semantically equivalent requests should share a result; an in-memory cache does not persist across processes; an HTTP retry can repeat an operation unless the endpoint is idempotent; and a governance timeout falls back to `UNCLASSIFIED`, not an affirmative approval. Optional imports mean the same library can degrade when dependencies are absent. Those semantics are reasonable for a local support library, but each caller must preserve them instead of flattening “returned result” into “approved.” The code is a shared implementation mechanism, not a portfolio-wide governance guarantee.

The boundary connections are enough: Product 16's Oracle later occupies a synthesis role; Products 17–19 are visualization, education, and falsification surfaces; Product 20 is Navigator; Products 21–22 extend monitoring and security governance; Products 23–27 extend PsiCat-facing assistants, browser, simulator, and workbench; Products 28–40 are focused research and infrastructure tools; Products 41–45 extend media, awareness, geometric-model, integration, and desktop surfaces. Two representative compositions show how the parts could work together without pretending the plumbing is already automatic. **A field study, human-mediated:** a researcher defines a soil question and records an analysis plan through UM-SOS; they use Terra to retrieve or normalize available soil data and preserve whether a response came from a live source or its coordinate-based fallback; they use Lithos to query catalogued seismic events for a bounded interval and preserve the freshness and source metadata; they then compare the evidence to the plan and write a result in the study record. Any transfer between these products in this example is an operator-controlled export/import or transcription unless a specific interface test demonstrates otherwise. Omega could be used separately to calculate a framework report for comparison, but adding that report cannot change the measured soil or seismic data. The useful outcome is a provenance-bearing dossier whose layers remain distinguishable.

**A newsroom claim review, human-mediated:** AXIOM holds the investigation lead, source entries, claims, contradictions, and open questions; the reporter exports the exact records and asks a subject-matter editor to inspect them; the editor checks original public filings and records which claim is supported by which passage; any publication packet retains legal-risk flags and uncertainty. If a separate public audit dataset comes from EIGE, that verifier follows its own election bundle, signature, manifest, and paper-audit workflow. It should not be imported as generic corroboration for a journalism claim merely because both products use “evidence” language. The integrations are institutional and procedural unless an explicit software bridge is present.

These are not synthetic benchmarks with claimed results. They are worked designs that help a reader ask the right engineering questions: where does the record originate; which transformation happened; what data were dropped; how can the recipient verify the provenance; what happens on timeout or a missing dependency; who decides whether an output is actionable? If a future portfolio team implements a handoff, those questions can become contract tests. A small contract might require a schema version, source URI, `generated_at` and `retrieved_at` fields, freshness status, and a way to carry the raw source alongside the summary. Another might require that an empty search result distinguish “zero hits” from “provider unavailable.” Those details turn a narrative of connection into a tested interface.

These statements may refer to different run modes, but a combined local installation must choose and document distinct bindings and configure callers to match. A safe smoke test would start the two services independently, query each health/status endpoint, verify that the intended client reaches the right process, and stop them cleanly. I did not launch either service here, so this is a source-derived configuration question, not a measured collision.

There are also reasons not to flatten every product into a single “AI” category. EIGE is a deterministic audit-support stack with narrowly defined cryptographic and statistical checks. UM-SOS is a research-record and status interface. UOS and the two mobile tools are prototypes with varying degrees of hardware dependence. A model or score in one cannot silently lend scientific credibility to another. The right comparison is by input, method, and output—not by which product sounds most futuristic.

Product-specific README test totals are likewise documentation claims unless an exact validation command was run in this task; no full 15-product test campaign is claimed. I leave the reader with a practical method: start from the registry to find the canonical folder; read the product README's current-state and limitation sections; inspect the implementation path for the claimed transformation; inspect tests for the behavior they actually cover; check the current status record if the prose makes a scientific claim; and ask what input could falsify the output. In product development terms, the portfolio's real novelty may emerge at these handoffs. An individual parser or scoring function is rarely the hardest part; the hard work is preserving context while changing representation. A CVR becomes an internal election record; a soil API response becomes a normalized field; a screenplay becomes scenes and breakdown elements; phone sensor samples become a dashboard visualization. Each conversion can introduce loss, ambiguity, unit changes, or hidden defaults. A mature integration would preserve both raw and normalized forms, attach the transformation version and units, expose validation errors, and let the next tool distinguish a missing value from an observed zero. The examined product guides show several such ideas—source provenance in Lithos, digest verification in UM-SOS, explicit statuses in AXIOM, parser and interchange support in Filmer's Companion—but they do not prove one uniform portfolio-wide provenance contract.

There is a strength here worth naming without exaggeration: many of the products explicitly label open, unavailable, stale, partial, stubbed, or unvalidated states. That is not a scientific achievement, but it is good product engineering because it lets users avoid converting system silence into a confident answer. There are also concrete weaknesses visible in the reviewed source: some fallbacks synthesize approximate values; some scores depend on arbitrary or self-declared inputs; some test suites exercise helper logic rather than hardware or deployment; some product metadata use older language than the current status record; some integrations are described as targets rather than functioning paths. These observations are local to the cited materials and code. They are not a full security audit or an exhaustive validation of all fifteen products.
