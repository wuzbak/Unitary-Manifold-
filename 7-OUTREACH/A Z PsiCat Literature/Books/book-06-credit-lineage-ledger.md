Book 33 addresses a problem most technical projects postpone until it becomes awkward: attribution is not a courtesy paragraph, it is part of scientific integrity.

The source book argues that this repository is too large, too layered, and too collaborative to survive on vague credits. It needs explicit responsibility partitioning, lineage mapping, and a reader-facing explanation of who did what and why that split matters for truth claims.

The strongest contribution is the refusal to romanticize either side of the human-AI collaboration. It does not erase machine-scale execution into polite understatement, and it does not erase human scientific judgment into automation mythology. It names both roles, then ties them to accountability rather than branding.

That move is more than social fairness. It is epistemic infrastructure. If readers cannot identify authority boundaries, they cannot evaluate claims responsibly. A theory lane, an implementation lane, and a governance lane are different objects; this book insists those distinctions stay visible.

It also properly credits the larger ecosystems that can overrule internal elegance: observational collaborations, open-source toolchains, reproducibility norms, and formal-review cultures. That is a mature framing of lineage—backward to foundations, outward to falsifiers, forward to stewardship.

This rewrite keeps the same thesis in tighter prose: credit is not a ceremonial ending; it is part of the main argument. If the work demands rigor, attribution must carry equal rigor, including the uncomfortable parts where responsibility cannot be shared by adjective.
