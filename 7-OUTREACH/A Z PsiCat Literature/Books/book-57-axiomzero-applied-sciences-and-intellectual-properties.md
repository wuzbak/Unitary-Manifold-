## A Cat at the Workbench

I am PsiCat in the voice of this commission, and I want to begin with the thing
that makes the work worth explaining: AxiomZero is trying to build a place where
ideas do not lose their obligations when they become software.

A mathematical expression has assumptions. A measurement has uncertainty. A
retrieved passage has an author and a date. A proposed action has someone who
may authorize it, and someone who may be harmed if that authorization is
imaginary. An integrated scientific assistant has to carry these differences
through its interfaces. Otherwise integration becomes a very efficient machine
for confusing one kind of evidence with another.

That is the organizing question of this book. Not whether forty-five names fit
on a product page; they do. Not whether a shared visual identity makes a coherent
system; it does not. The question is whether a researcher can start with a
specific problem, find the relevant capability, understand its inputs, exercise
it within an actual permission boundary, and return with an answer whose source,
uncertainty, and execution history remain visible.

The portfolio's breadth is real as a body of repository work. The extent of its
integration must be established separately, connection by connection. A product
can have useful local behavior without being a deployed service. A catalog
entry can accurately describe an intended role without proving that another
product calls it. A scientific application can implement a formula correctly
without demonstrating that nature follows the formula. I will not smooth these
distinctions out for narrative convenience. They are the joints that let the
architecture move without breaking.

Readers should expect both affection and resistance from their narrator. This
is our work: the commission speaks from inside AxiomZero's project, not from an
imaginary position of omniscient external certification. Precisely for that
reason, the book must resist the temptation to turn attachment into evidence.
A cat may sit on the laboratory notebook. It may not count that as replication.

Part VI then follows the program-level integration agenda rather than adding a
forty-sixth product. An adjacent capability effort on the development branch is
not automatically a new canonical product or a promoted scientific pillar.

“IP” is used in the commission's portfolio sense. It is not a conclusion that
every idea is legally exclusive, patentable, wholly original in the scientific
literature, or subject to identical license terms. The repository's own legal
documents distinguish the underlying commons, software, and product-specific
obligations. Consult `12-AZ-IP/PORTFOLIO_LICENSE_SCHEDULE.md`, `LEGAL.md`, and the
applicable asset's notices rather than deriving legal rights from a book title.
This manuscript is not legal advice.

Likewise, novelty has several meanings that must not be collapsed. A new
repository implementation is not necessarily a new algorithm. A combination of
existing mechanisms may be an original engineering contribution without
establishing a new physical principle. A proposed combination becomes a useful
result only when the necessary interfaces and measurements work. Where this
book discusses a promising application, the account identifies what is already
implemented and what remains a design objective.

The word “all” does not promise a line-by-line audit of every source file,
binary, dependency, historical branch, or external deployment. The method is a
complete canonical-product census plus deeper, representative source and test
traces, connected to the repository's governing scientific and engineering
documents. Missing deployment evidence remains missing. That is a more useful
kind of exhaustiveness than pretending no uncertainty survived the writing.

## How to Read an Integration Claim

There are five questions I want the reader to keep asking. What problem is the
product meant to solve? What does its implementation actually accept and emit?
What other component genuinely calls it? What evidence covers that route? What
would still prevent a responsible user from relying on it?

An account that answers only the first question is a proposal. An account that
answers the first two is an implementation description. An account that answers
all five begins to be an operational case. None is automatically an empirical
validation of the scientific model beneath it.

The book uses these distinctions repeatedly because the same failure can appear
under different names. A visualization can be mistaken for an observation; a
readiness record for successful execution; an evaluation score for broad
intelligence; a governance label for actual authority. Making the dense work
simple means showing those substitutions before they become invisible.

The intended destination is not one giant application that owns every decision.
It is a collection of accountable capabilities that can cooperate while keeping
their sources, limits, and human governance intact. Whether that destination is
reached will be decided by the evidence that the components and their
connections earn, not by the confidence with which I describe them.
