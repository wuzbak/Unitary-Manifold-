## A Cat at the Workbench

I am PsiCat in the voice of this commission, and I want to begin with the thing
that makes the work worth explaining: AxiomZero is trying to build a place where
ideas do not lose their obligations when they become software.

A mathematical expression has assumptions. A measurement has uncertainty. A
retrieved passage has an author and a date. A proposed action has someone who
may authorize it, and someone who may be harmed if that authorization is
imaginary. An integrated scientific assistant has to carry these differences
through its interfaces. Otherwise integration becomes a very efficient machine
for confusing one kind of evidence with another.

That is the organizing question of this book. Not whether forty-five names fit
on a product page; they do. Not whether a shared visual identity makes a coherent
system; it does not. The question is whether a researcher can start with a
specific problem, find the relevant capability, understand its inputs, exercise
it within an actual permission boundary, and return with an answer whose source,
uncertainty, and execution history remain visible.

The portfolio's breadth is real as a body of repository work. The extent of its
integration must be established separately, connection by connection. A product
can have useful local behavior without being a deployed service. A catalog
entry can accurately describe an intended role without proving that another
product calls it. A scientific application can implement a formula correctly
without demonstrating that nature follows the formula. I will not smooth these
distinctions out for narrative convenience. They are the joints that let the
architecture move without breaking.

Readers should expect both affection and resistance from their narrator. This
is our work: the commission speaks from inside AxiomZero's project, not from an
imaginary position of omniscient external certification. Precisely for that
reason, the book must resist the temptation to turn attachment into evidence.
A cat may sit on the laboratory notebook. It may not count that as replication.

## How to Read an Integration Claim

There are five questions I want the reader to keep asking. What problem is the
product meant to solve? What does its implementation actually accept and emit?
What other component genuinely calls it? What evidence covers that route? What
would still prevent a responsible user from relying on it?

An account that answers only the first question is a proposal. An account that
answers the first two is an implementation description. An account that answers
all five begins to be an operational case. None is automatically an empirical
validation of the scientific model beneath it.

The book uses these distinctions repeatedly because the same failure can appear
under different names. A visualization can be mistaken for an observation; a
readiness record for successful execution; an evaluation score for broad
intelligence; a governance label for actual authority. Making the dense work
simple means showing those substitutions before they become invisible.

The intended destination is not one giant application that owns every decision.
It is a collection of accountable capabilities that can cooperate while keeping
their sources, limits, and human governance intact. Whether that destination is
reached will be decided by the evidence that the components and their
connections earn, not by the confidence with which I describe them.

### A1 — A Product Portfolio Is Not a Proof

I have been asked to describe the first fifteen canonical products in AxiomZero's software portfolio as products: what a person can give them, what they do with it, what comes back, and where the boundary lies between a useful program and a claim about the world. That boundary matters because this catalog sits beside a physics framework. A product can calculate a number derived from a formula, stage a workflow around an experiment, or display a field label inspired by geometry without validating the formula, experiment, or geometry. Software faithfully executing a premise is evidence that the program implements that premise; it is not, on its own, evidence that nature obeys it.

The registry is the starting map, not an independent field survey. It calls Products 01–15 the Axiom OS Core Suite, AZ-KERNEL Rust Kernel, EIGE Governance Engine, UM-SOS Scientific OS, UOS Kernel Prototype, Omega Synthesis Engine, Holon Zero Engine, AxiomZero Journalist AI, OmegaHolon Engine, Filmer's Companion, Terra OS, Lithos OS, Delphi, SDAM, and Pentacorder. It also reports versions, technology-readiness levels (TRLs), test counts, ports, and brief descriptions. Those are documentation assertions copied from the registry; I have not rerun every package or independently assessed readiness. A test count says how many tests a registry author associated with a product, not that I reproduced the count, that the tests establish deployment fitness, or that a scientific prediction has been confirmed.

The products are not a single monolithic executable. They include local Python engines, web services, an operating-system kernel prototype, governance applications, Android software, and planned or incomplete components. Some implementations are consolidations of earlier repositories; others are standalone packages. The portfolio schedule identifies the canonical product folders and says something essential about ownership and licensing: the schedule is an inventory of scope, not a new license or an amendment to AGPL. Asset-specific notices, upstream terms, third-party components, and prior grants continue to matter. A folder's presence in one portfolio does not rewrite the legal history of every file in it.

I will use a small operational lens throughout. **Input** is what a user or another program supplies. **Processing** is the actual implemented transformation—parsing, scoring, retrieval, simulation, or display. **Output** is what the system returns or stores. **Use case** is the practical situation in which that transformation may help. **State** distinguishes what the checked-in product does now from a roadmap or ambition. The method is deliberately plain. If a reader cannot trace an output back through those steps, the description has become sales copy.

The first seven products form a loose foundation-and-modeling cluster. Product 01 coordinates tasks and memory over an existing host operating system. Product 02 is a bare-metal Rust kernel, a very different layer. EIGE and UM-SOS organize evidence and research records. UOS is an exploratory geometric-kernel prototype. Omega and Holon Zero turn framework assumptions into structured computational reports and interpretive models. Their names invite one to read them as one ascending architecture; the source tree does not warrant that simplification. Their connections are practical and editorial: shared constants, data, governance vocabulary, and interfaces. They are not a demonstrated vertical chain in which every upstream scientific assertion has been verified by every downstream product.

Current epistemic control is especially important when reading older product language. `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md` is the current reassessment, and takes precedence over historical descriptions of closure. For example, its current sections keep full functional proof and several physical-time obligations open, while noting that certain narrower identities or constrained results have been earned within explicit perimeters. ### A2 — Host Intelligence and a Kernel That Boots

#### Product 01: Axiom OS Core Suite

Axiom OS is most accurately introduced by the negative definition in its own README: a persistent cognitive layer intended to run on top of an existing operating system, not a replacement kernel or bare-metal OS. That distinction is not cosmetic. The product coordinates software services on a host that still owns process scheduling, drivers, memory protection, and the ordinary boundary between applications and hardware. Its “OS” is an orchestration and user-facing metaphor for an AI-enabled software environment, not a claim that the project has displaced Linux, macOS, or Windows.

The product's documented pipeline begins with a bootstrap that detects a host and writes configuration or registers a service. The intended cognitive core is described as seven managers with narrower sub-agents: geometry, field equations, symbolic math, test orchestration, repository retrieval, web research, and executive synthesis. A model router is meant to send tasks to configured models under a VRAM constraint. A second layer exposes file, execution, and browser tools through MCP servers; memory combines vector retrieval, a SQLite task journal, and a session log; interfaces include an API, dashboard, and Android/Termux client. These components make a legible product concept: gather a task, route it, retrieve context, request bounded work, persist results, and surface approvals or status.

In concrete use, an operator might provide a repository question. The orchestrator is the entry point; a retrieval manager can locate relevant material, an executive manager can compose an answer, and the local state database can retain a task record. For a state-changing operation, the intended handoff is not “AI says go, therefore go”: the README describes governance routing for changes touching claims, tests, or pillar definitions. The output may be an answer, a task result, or an approval request. In many environments, the precise outcome depends on installed dependencies, model configuration, provider availability, and which integrations are actually running.

The implementation itself makes the current state more precise than the bootstrap story. `12-AZ-IP/01-axiom-os/agent_core.py` is a small compatibility-facing module that imports `AxiomZeroOrchestrator`, task and state types from `core.agent_core`, and optionally exposes the legacy `AgentCore`. The richer README explicitly warns that startup recovery reruns queued or interrupted manager pipelines from the beginning, is not a mid-graph resume or exactly-once transaction, and assumes one service process. A manager that acted before a crash can act again; task input and results are plaintext JSON in the local SQLite database. The README further says the restart tests stub managers and exercise database/task lifecycle, not provider, MCP, model-server, or deployed integration. Those caveats are not footnotes to be waved away; they define how to operate the system safely and what the tests actually support.

Use Axiom OS where a person needs an integrated local research or task-workbench with explicit state and human governance, and where they can configure and supervise its components. Do not treat it as an autonomous operating system, a self-verifying scientist, or a turnkey inference appliance just because the product vocabulary says “cognitive core.” The interesting development is the combination of routing, task recovery, memory, and controlled tool surfaces in a host-side architecture. It is not a demonstrated general intelligence, a proof engine whose verdict is self-authenticating, or a substitute for an operator checking an action before its side effects occur.

#### Product 02: AZ-KERNEL Rust Kernel

AZ-KERNEL occupies the lower software layer that Product 01 expressly does not replace. It is a bare-metal Rust kernel consolidated from a fuller `az-kernel` tree and an earlier prototype. The README says the merge retained drivers, filesystem, framebuffer, panic handling, build scripts, and assets, and added a legacy KK-channel IPC primitive. Its documented targets are a UEFI x86-64 boot image, QEMU/OVMF integration, and an ARM64 cross-build path. The code is therefore about boot-time and kernel primitives, not an AI agent installed on a desktop.

The canonical build route is `cargo check` followed by the product's build script. A useful development loop is to change a hardware-independent kernel library component, run the host-targeted library tests, then build the UEFI image and exercise it under the intended emulator. The source separates some allocator, memory bookkeeping, φ-debt RAM filesystem, and IPC logic into a library crate so a normal host Rust test harness can exercise these pieces without booting firmware. One specific engineering concern is that UEFI boot services stop being available after `exit_boot_services`; the allocator work described in the README replaces ongoing allocation through those services with kernel-owned static memory. That is a recognizable systems problem with a concrete implementation surface.

The deliverable is still bounded by target and verification. The host unit tests are not a hardware-compatibility test. A successful `cargo check` is not proof that the full stack boots on every device. QEMU/OVMF testing is not production deployment, and an ARM64 cross-build script does not mean every ARM board is supported. Nor does an IPC channel name establish a physical Kaluza–Klein mechanism in the kernel: geometric nomenclature may organize the software design, but it does not turn a data structure into a new physical law.

Input is a Rust source tree and build configuration; processing is compilation, linking, and execution of kernel code in a supported boot or emulated environment; output is a boot image, compiler diagnostics, or test result. The practical audience is systems developers exploring a research kernel and testing memory, IPC, or boot lifecycle behavior. The risk profile differs from a notebook calculator: a kernel failure can prevent startup or compromise a machine. One should build and test in a disposable virtualized environment first, rather than confusing a development artifact with a safe replacement for a general-purpose OS.

AZ-KERNEL and Axiom OS are adjacent by portfolio intent, not interchangeable. One is designed to work above a host OS; the other targets bare-metal boot. The bridge between those layers would need explicit, tested contracts. Product 35, outside this book's dossier scope, is later described as a host-side JSON adapter that does not connect to a live kernel. That boundary is more honest than implying a working integration simply because the products share a brand and vocabulary. The novel work in AZ-KERNEL is systems integration and the kernel-owned resource primitives; it is not a validated physical realization of the framework's geometry.

### A3 — Evidence Records, Scientific Workflows, and a Prototype OS

#### Product 03: EIGE Governance Engine

EIGE is the most tightly bounded civic application in this opening group. Its current README calls it an audit-support and public-transparency tool for election officials. It does not count votes, replace certified tabulators, replace paper ballots, or certify an election by assertion. The core workflow is not a new vote-casting machine; it is a verifiable record-and-check process around election artifacts.

The registry lists Product 03 at v21, while its product README describes v22. That version drift is a concrete reason to cite the implementation and current product guide for scope while treating registry metadata as a catalog assertion, not a release receipt.

An official supplies election definitions and ballot manifests, then imports cast-vote records (CVRs). The current implementation supports NIST SP 1500-103 JSON and JSONL, plus EIGE's documented CSV format; vendor-specific exports need a jurisdiction-specific conversion first. Product code validates and logs records, reconciles counts and reporting units, records chain-of-custody events, and can prepare an audit sample and audit calculations. A county workflow can publish signed log heads and gather witness cosignatures; a verifier reads a bundle and checks signatures, inclusion/consistency proofs, manifests, tallies, samples, and audit results. At state level, the verifier checks county bundle composition and roll-up arithmetic. The useful output is a structured report with blocking, review, or pass findings—information that officials and independent reviewers can inspect alongside the paper process.

This workflow becomes tangible in the code. `eige/cvr_import.py` converts one NIST CVR object into EIGE's internal structure, including mapping selection identifiers and preserving marks according to the CVR's allocation flags. Its report reader streams records rather than loading an arbitrarily large full report into memory. `eige/verify.py` selects supported audit formats, checks that audited draws belong to the published sample, runs comparison or ballot-polling calculations, and reports when the risk limit was not met so the audit must escalate. The public risk-limit language matters: failure to reach a limit is not itself proof of fraud. A statistical signal is a reason for follow-up, not a verdict about intent.

The README's v22 “What changed” section also retracts security claims found unsupported by review. A rolling hash is retained only as a non-security sequence fingerprint; a former metric-closure detector and “zero-knowledge proof” claim were retired. Merkle structures, signatures, and witness records serve specific integrity and transparency purposes, but they do not prove that the underlying voter, official, scanner, or certified tabulator was honest. EIGE says it cannot detect ballot manipulation before scanning or logging; it cannot infer a signer's honesty from a signature; and it cannot resolve all ballot-secrecy or compromised-firmware concerns. Jurisdictions still need law, paper evidence, operational safeguards, and independent audit.

The practical use case is to make records, mismatches, and audit steps easier to reproduce and review—not to delegate the election outcome to a program. Input: manifest, CVR data, results, custody records, and audit parameters. Processing: validation, durable logging, reconciliation, sampling, risk-limiting calculations, and bundle verification. Output: tamper-evident records and a report that may identify discrepancies or require escalation. A realistic adoption plan starts with synthetic or historical data, tests the local jurisdiction's conversion and chain of custody, and independently checks the audit math and paper workflow before any operational pilot. The TRL and test count in the registry are not independent certification, and the listed software checks do not constitute a legal or election-security approval.

#### Product 04: UM-SOS Scientific OS

UM-SOS is a software interface around research records, not an operating system in the kernel sense. The README describes seven layers: prediction access; experimental-monitor endpoints; derivation graph export and UI; preregistration registry; frontend explorer; governance-integrated AI query; and the deployment bridge. Its value proposition is to make a research program navigable: a user can ask what prediction is recorded, inspect a graph of dependencies, view a gap or status, and create a local preregistration entry with its content digest.

Take a scientist preparing an analysis. The researcher can request all prediction records, inspect the current status for an experiment, browse pillar metadata, and look at an exported derivation DAG. If planning a new test, the local preregistration endpoint stores a record and computes a SHA-256 digest; the interface has no edit/delete route for those local entries. The content hash can help detect a changed record, but—as the README plainly notes—it is not a trusted timestamp, author signature, or identity check. Those distinctions are precisely why a research OS needs labels and source provenance rather than a dashboard that looks authoritative.

The documented current package includes API routes, frontend/graph assets, preregistration data, and build scripts for the registry and DAG. The README gives a local run command and notes the default loopback binding. A human can use it to inspect a structured repository view; an AI query can be routed with epistemic labels and governance lane classification. A well-designed use is to record a hypothesis before data collection, then compare the record with a later analysis. A poorly designed use is to present a current PASS/TENSION/FALSIFIED label as an independent external scientific adjudication without checking where the label came from and what evidence it represents.

The architecture is strongest as recordkeeping, graphing, and navigation. Its limits include the quality and freshness of underlying registry entries, local author declarations, and what each status actually measures. A graph can reveal dependencies without proving each edge; a preregistration can preserve declared intent without proving the declared author; an AI answer can summarize labels without validating a physical model. This product is complementary to EIGE: EIGE focuses on public election records and audit workflow, while UM-SOS organizes a scientific framework's predictions, gaps, and derivation relationships. Both use a workflow interface to make claims traceable. Neither makes a source true because it has been neatly indexed.

The store's digest process shows the distinction at code level. It serializes a payload as canonical JSON—sorted keys, compact separators, UTF-8—then hashes those bytes with SHA-256. Its response contains a generated record ID, UTC timestamp, the payload, the digest, and `identity_status: self-declared`. When a record is read back, the code reserializes the payload and compares the digest. A changed record becomes an integrity mismatch. This is a useful tamper-detection contract for a local append-only log. It is not a signature, and its timestamp is generated by the host clock; neither field is independently notarized. A worked input such as a study title, hypothesis, outcome variables, and analysis plan becomes one stable JSON record whose digest can be checked later. The recorded object can be compared with the analysis plan used after data arrive, but no code can determine whether a researcher actually followed that plan without additional evidence.

#### Product 05: UOS Kernel Prototype

The Unitary Operating System prototype is more ambitious in its conceptual language than its current engineering status. The README proposes a 5D “manifold layer” with a hypervisor, geodesic scheduler, unitary memory, geometric isolation, holographic filesystem, and driver wrapper, plus a monitor mode that shadows host resource metrics. In a demo, the user boots a simulated manifold state, advances ticks, attaches CPU or memory load, schedules process-like objects, shares memory, checks its invariant, and asks for a resource report. The output is a software model—a set of states, scores, and diagnostic values.

The idea is to express resource management in a different vocabulary: a scheduler selects a process using an affinity score; memory objects use φ-address references; a filesystem encodes data into shards; and an intent vector is projected to a four-dimensional hardware signal. These are concrete functions to inspect and test, and they make the prototype a useful place to explore how different abstractions might be represented. However, the README's phrases such as “no fragmentation,” “data loss is physically impossible,” and “no context-switch latency” are theoretical claims about the prototype's model, not benchmark results or guarantees about hardware. A model can return zero fragmentation by construction while a real allocator still needs measurements under workloads, adversarial inputs, concurrency, and failure.

It does not replace the kernel in Product 02 or the host-side agent suite in Product 01. The schedule identifies it as a kernel prototype with a Python-library surface and a large listed test count, but those metadata are not a measure of operating-system readiness. The current task is to clarify the abstraction and test it against ordinary OS semantics, not to turn metaphors into guarantees. #### The Reusable Boundary: Shared Library, Source Authority, and Reader Practice

The common library `12-AZ-IP/lib/az_ip_common/` is described as shared infrastructure for cross-product imports. Shared code can reduce duplicated validation, format conversion, provenance fields, or interface helpers, but it does not make every product a single application. A dependency is not a deployment. Two folders importing the same helper do not establish that they run together in a user-facing workflow, share databases, or have compatible releases. A reader should look for an actual caller, a declared interface, and a test of the interaction before drawing the larger-architecture box around them.

The same standard applies to a cited source. The registry gives the portfolio's names and self-reported metadata. Each product README explains intended behavior and current limitations. Implementation code tells us which transformations exist. Tests show particular exercised cases. The current truth layer governs scientific status where historical claims conflict. None of those sources is a substitute for all the others. A product README can state an intention that code has not reached; implementation can contain a feature without a test of its hardest cases; tests can pass while answering a narrower question than the surrounding narrative implies.

This is why “input → processing → output” is not a mere documentation format. It is the simplest defense against category errors. For Product 06, a field-theory parameter may go in, an equation or lookup is evaluated, and a predicted number comes out; that tells us what the engine calculated. It does not yet tell us whether the number was freely predicted, whether an external data set independently agrees, or whether the physics is true. For Product 07, a defined set of links may be traversed to a “self-reference” result; that describes a program structure and a philosophy, not a new experiment.

### A5 — Research, Reflection, and the Craft of Making Films

#### Product 08: AxiomZero Journalist AI

AxiomZero Journalist AI—often styled AXIOM in its product guide—is an investigative research workbench built around a newsroom principle: the journalist judges; the tool organizes the work that makes judgment possible. It is not described as an article writer or a publish button. A user begins with an investigative lead, opens a local case, maps people and organizations, records sources, attaches claims to evidence, and generates a brief that remains explicitly for human review.

The strongest part of that flow is the claim ledger. Sources are classified by tier, while a claim can be marked confirmed, corroborated, alleged, or unverified according to the sources attached to it. “Confirmed” in this software vocabulary is not an external certification; it is a product rule applied to the evidence entries in a case. Legal-risk flags—privacy, source protection, libel exposure, national-security concerns—are reminders for review, not legal advice. A source excerpt and reference can be inspected with the claim, contradictions can be logged, open questions remain visible, and the report can carry an uncertainty-aware account of its evidence path.

For example, a reporter receives a tip about a contract. They create a case, add entities and the allegation, log a primary filing and an on-record statement, attach the documents to claims, and record a contradictory statement or missing record. AXIOM can help produce an investigative brief and a governed dossier packet that summarizes confidence, source tiers, and risk markers. Its source-scan facility can produce a manifest for a catalog of public-record systems and has adapters for a limited subset. The README differentiates `COMPLETED`, `PARTIAL`, and `NOT_CONFIGURED`; a completed adapter with no returned hit does not demonstrate that no relevant record exists. That is a crucial negative-evidence safeguard.

The current product describes local SQLite case storage, a transparent research trail, and structured source-led handoffs. Some public-record scanning uses network access; the older claim that all data stays local would be too broad for the current README. The platform is not a general web crawler, a complete fact-checker, or an author of publishable copy. It does not replace source relationships, editor judgment, legal counsel, or the reporter's sense of context. Its more novel development lies in translating evidence practices into a traceable workflow and exporting training/publication packets without intentionally dropping uncertainty. Such a packet is not proof that its recipient read or obeyed the source ledger.

An appropriate use is internal organization of a case and preparation for editor review, with staff verifying every document, quote, calculation, and identity. An inappropriate use is to publish a generated confidence label as if two records automatically made a claim true, to construe an incomplete search as exoneration or guilt, or to let a model generate an accusation and treat its fluency as sourcing. Input: lead, records, sources, entities, claims, notes. Processing: local storage, linkage, heuristic scoring, configured source lookup, and report assembly. Output: case records and a review packet. The person remains accountable for the investigative conclusion.

The source-tier weights and claim categories are best understood as queueing and editorial prompts: they help make uneven records visible and can keep a case from presenting every lead as equally supported. A Tier-1 filing is still relevant only to the proposition it actually states, and two sources may not be independent if one simply copied the other. The source relation is therefore more important than a number generated by a rubric. A solid newsroom workflow would verify the original document, record its date and context, separate a source's assertion from an independently checked fact, and preserve the exact excerpt and link. AXIOM can help maintain that ledger; it cannot decide whether a source is authentic or whether the excerpt was quoted fairly without people doing that checking.

#### Product 09: OmegaHolon Engine

OmegaHolon takes a different form of evidence: not documents about a public event, but a person's own structured reflection. It is a personal life-coherence tool with five domains—body and health, mind and emotion, work and purpose, relationships and trust, resources and environment. Users assign each domain an epistemic status, describe conditions and constraints, list open gaps, record a trust or authenticity estimate, and define a falsifiable commitment about what would change their mind. A daily pulse and a decision form add time-series and comparison features. Local SQLite storage supports profiles, audits, decisions, reports, and history.

The decision workflow accepts a question and up to three options, with user-entered impacts on each life domain. The product then weights those impacts according to the chosen domain statuses and trust parameters and ranks the options. A person might compare moving for a job with staying near family, encoding the effects they currently anticipate and then examining the report for the tradeoffs it makes visible. The “output” is a structured reflection prompt or ranking, not a recommendation from an empirically validated mental-health model. It should provoke a conversation with oneself or a trusted adviser, not overrule personal agency.

The central caution is that the mathematics is a borrowed scoring structure. Equations like `omega_score = stability_floor × average_resonance` give a reproducible procedure, but the result is only as meaningful as the inputs, weights, and measurement model. A numerical “crisis” or “unified” grade does not diagnose a person, establish a clinical condition, or measure an objective physical state. The product README states the tool does not require the physics to be correct: it borrows epistemic statuses, thresholds, coupling structures, and the practice of writing falsifiable commitments. That separation is healthy. It also means the physics framework does not validate the life score.

The product's novel practical contribution is a structured alternative to a simple habit tracker. It asks what a person believes is well-founded, what they do not know, what constrains action, and what observation could test a plan. Longitudinal records can make a change in self-assessment visible, but they cannot by themselves tell us whether the scale is reliable or whether a rise in score corresponds to improved well-being. Treat the dashboard as a journal with arithmetic, not an authority on a person's life. For sensitive information, consider that local storage is still data storage: protect the device and its backups, and do not enter details that a user cannot safely retain.

One implemented helper makes the scoring mechanics especially clear. It gathers numeric metrics, chooses a scale of 10 if any number exceeds 1 (otherwise 1), clamps normalized values into [0,1], computes their mean and population variance, then combines mean with a balance term: `0.7 × mean + 0.3 × [1/(1 + variance × φ × 4)]`. For illustrative inputs `[8, 6, 8]`, the numbers become normalized values `[0.8, 0.6, 0.8]`; the implementation returns a bounded score based on both their average and spread. That is a reproducible scoring rule. It is not a validated psychometric scale, and its behavior changes if the caller mixes units, passes missing values, or records self-ratings inconsistently. Because the formula rewards balance as well as mean, it may assign a higher value to a uniformly mediocre profile than the user expects. A responsible product conversation should expose the formula and invite the user to question it, rather than hide it behind a single Ω badge.

### A6 — Soil, Rock, and the Many Meanings of Prediction

#### Product 11: Terra OS

Even the term “measured” refers to events in the public catalogue, not Lithos physically measuring seismic motion.

The specific weakness is important because neighboring modules can look more authoritative when placed together. Mineral-catalog matches and seismicity are not linked to the same geologic inference in the current README; site events do not establish the identity or economic value of a rock specimen. Terra's coordinate-derived fallback and Lithos' live event adapter also have different provenance and evidentiary status. A combined map would need to retain those distinctions in its layer legends and exported records. Combining two sources is a technical feature; it is not automatically a stronger conclusion.

#### Product 13: Delphi

Delphi is a five-channel hypothesis-exploration application. Its channels include Tarot, Runes, Astrology, Chinese Zodiac, and a hypothesis explorer for falsifiable Unitary Manifold claims. The juxtaposition is unusual, so its README's epistemic disclaimer deserves to be read before the feature list: the product says symbolic channels are not facts, outputs carry explicit statuses, open tests and documented gaps remain visible, and human judgment stays in the loop. It is an exploration surface rather than an authority.

The workflow depends on which channel the user selects. In a symbolic channel, a question or selected prompt is passed through a reading or interpretive template and returned as reflective framing. That can help a person articulate assumptions, make alternatives visible, or generate a narrative prompt. It does not produce a scientifically validated prediction about the person's future. In the hypothesis channel, the user searches or browses a registry of claims and status labels, then follows a claim toward the stated falsifier or evidence. The first is a symbolic prompt; the second is a repository-navigation feature. They are not evidentially interchangeable.

The visible API exposes reading generation, horoscopes, zodiac information, and search, with a database and tests documented in the product guide. That establishes a working software surface, not that any divinatory claim is empirically supported. A safe use is entertainment, journaling, or a structured conversation starter, with clear awareness of the symbolic status. A useful research use is to locate a documented hypothesis and inspect its formal status and evidence path. A poor use is to make medical, financial, legal, or other high-stakes choices based on an astrological or tarot reading, or to present a generated reading as evidence for a physical theory.

The novel element is the coexistence of multiple prompt styles with an epistemic channel that can route a reader toward falsifiable claims. It offers different modes of thinking, not a unified predictive instrument. The scientific challenge remains separate: if a claim is in Delphi's hypothesis registry, it needs to be checked against its source module, data, and the current truth layer. The software's presence does not upgrade the claim. As with all products here, a passing test confirms behavior of the tested code paths, not the truth of the propositions those paths display.

The hypothesis-scoring helper illustrates why the distinction is operational, not merely philosophical. `explore_hypothesis` selects one of a fixed set of channels, searches the query text for terms such as “falsify,” “contradict,” “outside,” “fail,” or “tension,” and can switch a status to OPEN when such a token is present. It returns the supplied text, channel identifier, fixed supporting and contradicting pillar lists, a confidence-interval string, and a status. This can act as a rapid exploration scaffold; it is not a statistical calculation of a confidence interval from sample data. A sentence that uses “outside” in a harmless way may trigger the same heuristic, and a counterexample phrased without the watched words may not. The label `confidence_interval` should therefore not be quoted as a measured probability. A reader can input a claim, inspect the returned registry links, then verify the source and status independently; the tool helps navigate but does not reason from new evidence as a Bayesian estimator would.

A future improvement could separate symbolic output from repository evidence in the type system, attach dated source provenance, and expose the status rule. Those are design directions, not present features; for now, treat the output as a navigation prompt rather than a verdict.

### A7 — Message Over Sound; Sensors on a Phone

#### Product 14: SDAM

The canonical registry calls Product 14 “SDAM” and gives its descriptor as a Spatial Decision Awareness Module, while the product README is titled “Software-Defined Acoustic Modem.” Those two names point in different directions. The implementation and current detailed README describe an Android acoustic messaging application, so that is the scope described here; the catalog wording is a metadata ambiguity to resolve in a later editorial pass, not a reason to silently invent a second working product.

The documented current path sends text over a speaker/microphone acoustic link. The Android app uses AudioRecord and AudioTrack around a ggwave v0.4.2/JNI codec; messages are encrypted with AES-GCM, then framed with an HMAC-SHA256 authenticator and sequence/counter information. The README describes fragmentation and reassembly limits, a separate legacy handshake format, and replay rejection whose high-water mark exists only in memory. In a single-device or compatible test setup, the input text is divided into bounded pieces, each encrypted and wrapped into an authenticated frame, acoustically encoded, received, authenticated, and reassembled before a success report. The sender's “queued” status is not a delivery receipt; the receiver can report success only after full authenticated reassembly.

The README does not hide the deployment gaps. The native library may not be packaged; if it is unavailable, the app must report the audio path as unavailable instead of claiming a successful transmission. JVM tests exercise deterministic frame encoding, encryption round-trips, tamper rejection, HMAC verification, replay behavior within a process, and payload limits, but they do not emulate audio hardware. Speaker/microphone loopback, range, spectrum, cross-device key provisioning, and device-specific response are unvalidated. A shared key is necessary for two devices to communicate securely, but the product does not yet provide a working peer-key provisioning flow. A frequency label such as “ultrasound” is not proof that the emitted signal is inaudible, contained, or safe.

The useful near-term case is controlled development or demonstration of authenticated encrypted text framing over a local acoustic channel, with devices and keys intentionally provisioned for a test. It is not an air-gap security product ready for a sensitive facility. Acoustic transport does not make a phone RF-invisible, prevent sound transmission through walls, or substitute for facility approval. The README explicitly labels data-diode, industrial telemetry, and mesh use as design targets or unimplemented functions. Its novel development is a real Android crypto/framing path integrated with audio APIs, while the signal behavior, packaging, radio exposure, and operational security remain to be tested. The Unitary Manifold connection is stated as conceptual context; no validated derivation links its equations to ggwave's codec or error correction.

Product 15's browser companion has a simplified acoustic link, not Product 14's native ggwave codec; the two implementations and their tests remain distinct.

At the protocol level, the product makes a useful layered separation: message framing is not the same as acoustic modulation, and authentication is not the same as encryption. The documented SDAM1 path places encrypted content into message fragments, adds framing metadata and an HMAC for authenticated frame handling, then hands the data to the codec. The receiver decodes a frame, checks authentication and sequence state, reassembles the message, and only then decrypts and reports completion. A random grouping ID separates concurrent or newly observed message sets; the counter helps reject replay during a running process, but the README says that replay high-water state resets on restart. That limitation changes the threat model. A test proving that a captured frame is rejected twice in one process does not prove that it remains rejected after app restart or on another receiver.

For researchers evaluating the prototype, a meaningful next test matrix would vary message length, fragment loss and reordering, corrupted tags, duplicate counters, restart boundaries, codec unavailability, device model, room acoustics, and key mismatch. The current JVM tests cover selected crypto/frame cases; the physical and cross-device dimensions remain separate evidence requirements. This describes a rigorous next step, not an assertion that the matrix has been run.

### A8 — Connections Without Conflation

The fifteen products tell a portfolio story, but the story is not a single pipeline. Its clearest connective tissue is a family of operations: record and retrieve information, route a task, compute a transformation, preserve status, and hand off a report or artifact. EIGE's audit bundles, UM-SOS's registries, AXIOM's case ledger, Terra and Lithos' environmental records, Filmer's production database, and Pentacorder's field displays each make information more structured. In this writing, local RAG was built and queried directly from repository source; no online PsiCat endpoint or Base44 infrastructure was contacted.

The shared library is one integration seam; adapters and exported files are others. A useful integration test should state the producer, consumer, schema/version, failure behavior, provenance fields, and whether the test is offline or live. A JSON artifact that validates against a schema proves its shape, not the truth of its contents. A cached USGS record needs freshness and source labels. A preregistration digest detects changes to bytes, but does not establish a trusted timestamp. A case confidence score is a software rule, not an editor's independent finding. An AI handoff retains only the evidence that the interface preserves; it cannot guarantee downstream attention.

The shared package offers more than a common folder name. `AZIPConfig` collects environment-based settings for the AxiomZero and UM-SOS URLs, JWT configuration, logging, storage, and optional observability. `EngineResult` standardizes fields such as engine name/version, `ok`, elapsed time, cache-hit flag, HILS approval, epistemic label, and a short result hash. The abstract `Engine.run` refuses an explicitly unapproved task, caches results by a hash of the JSON-serialized inputs, and optionally records Prometheus counters and latency. `AuthenticatedHTTPClient` supplies bearer headers and a bounded exponential-backoff retry loop when `httpx` is available. `pentad_classify` posts a task summary to a governance endpoint and returns `UNCLASSIFIED` if the request fails. Those are reusable contracts that can make multiple small engines more consistent.

They also show why shared plumbing needs explicit failure tests. A cache key that hashes raw keyword arguments does not know whether two semantically equivalent requests should share a result; an in-memory cache does not persist across processes; an HTTP retry can repeat an operation unless the endpoint is idempotent; and a governance timeout falls back to `UNCLASSIFIED`, not an affirmative approval. Optional imports mean the same library can degrade when dependencies are absent. Those semantics are reasonable for a local support library, but each caller must preserve them instead of flattening “returned result” into “approved.” The code is a shared implementation mechanism, not a portfolio-wide governance guarantee.

### B1 — The Ω Oracle and the Image That Knows Its Frame

Let us begin with a small discipline: distinguish a model from its conclusion. A model takes chosen inputs and follows declared rules. A conclusion may be as narrow as “the inputs satisfy this threshold,” or as broad as “the world is thus.” The first can be checked against code. The second requires evidence that code alone cannot supply. This distinction is the hinge connecting Product 16, **AxiomZero Ω Oracle**, and Product 17, **UM Physics Image Generator**. One translates declarations into a score-shaped synthesis; the other translates selected parameters into a picture. Neither picture nor score gets to vote itself into being truth.

The Oracle’s interface begins with a system name and type, then accepts a five-body Pentad model with epistemic statuses and φ-trust values. Optional inputs add seven governance-dimension scores, a decision question and options, or falsifiable commitments. Outputs include stability floor, Ω score, braid coherence, governance integrity and chain-of-custody measures, and a synthesis report; decision resonance and commitments appear only when supplied. This is a more useful description than “intelligence engine,” because it tells us what it actually consumes and returns. Its central mathematics combines aligned-body count and average resonance, with status weights `SOLID=1.00`, `CONSTRAINED=0.75`, `ESTIMATED=0.40`, and `OPEN=0.00`. An open claim does not vanish from the model; it contributes zero under the declared weighting.

Here is the first teaching exercise: submit the same five bodies twice, changing only one body’s status from `ESTIMATED` to `OPEN`. Ask which output fields change, and why. Then change only the alignment count and see how the stability floor moves. The exercise teaches sensitivity, not ontology. It shows what this implementation means by “coherence,” not that a city, person, or research team possesses a naturally occurring Ω quantity. That interpretation is the operator’s conceptual move and must be stated as such. A number with three decimal places may be a precise calculation of a chosen rubric; it is not automatically a precise measurement of the entity being scored.

The Oracle’s optional decision surface is similarly conditional. If a question and options are present, the product computes a resonance-ranked analysis; absent those inputs, one should not imagine a hidden recommendation engine waiting behind the curtain. If commitments are provided, they can be stored with test horizons. A commitment record is a future-checkable promise, not the future test itself. A reader should therefore preserve the report’s input declarations, not just its aggregate score: a reviewer cannot reconstruct a governance judgment from one scalar and a confident title. Product 16’s own ethics and score-grade discussions matter precisely because scope travels with the score.

The image generator receives a different kind of question: “What does this chosen parameter set look like when rendered through this visualization family?” The browser offers eight families—CMB plane, birefringence window, Kaluza–Klein tower, winding mode, φ landscape, Penrose entropy, holographic boundary, and braided sound speed. Its HTML and JavaScript render with Canvas 2D; the local Python package offers structured numerical data for corresponding visualizations. A user can launch a standalone server, choose a figure, and download a PNG, or call the Python generators and optionally request matplotlib export. That means one path is browser-native and dependency-light; the other is programmatic and supports reproducible pipelines. Matplotlib is optional, not silently required by the core browser interaction.

Take the birefringence window as a worked lesson. The renderer encodes the admissible interval `[0.22°, 0.38°]`, a forbidden interval `[0.29°, 0.31°]`, and two canonical points, `0.273°` and `0.331°`. It can display masks for “in bounds” and “outside the gap.” The image therefore communicates a rule already represented in data. It does not retrieve telescope observations, infer β from photons, or adjudicate an experiment. A responsible caption should say “visualization of repository parameters,” not “observed sky result.” A marker on a plotted plane is a marker, not a measurement.

The two products make a useful pair for a seminar. First construct a Pentad with mixed evidence statuses; write down which judgments came from an observer. Then choose a visualization and identify every constant or interval that was encoded. Ask what would happen if a parameter changed, whether the image changes as expected, and which claim remains untouched by either operation. The Oracle teaches declared weighting; the image generator teaches visible parameterization. Neither is a replacement for the framework’s source theory, external dataset, or independent analysis.

#### Reading the report and the picture together

Imagine a research group reports an Ω score of 0.61 and attaches a pretty plot. The first question is not whether the number looks plausible. It is: what did “the group” mean as a five-body system? Who assigned each status and φ-trust value? How were governance dimensions scored? Which fields were optional, which were omitted, and which were defaulted? The second question: does the plot depict source data, a formula-generated curve, or a hand-selected example? What is the data source and date? Those are provenance questions; no amount of polished visual design can answer them retroactively.

A practical review can keep two parallel records:

1. **Model declaration:** identities of bodies, status labels, trust inputs, scoring rule, governance dimensions, optional decision inputs, and commitment horizons.
2. **Visualization declaration:** visualization family, parameter vector, reference points, axes and bounds, data origin, render version, and export format.

Then show the model’s output and the image beside those declarations. If the audience cannot tell which entries are illustrative and which are observed, the presentation has failed its epistemic job. The right response is not to ban summaries or graphics; it is to make the chain of interpretation legible.

For teaching, vary one factor at a time. Let a team member propose an alternative status for one body and predict the change before rerunning the Oracle. In the image generator, choose a parameter other than the displayed central prediction and forecast the movement on the chart before exporting. A forecast that surprises you is a useful debugging signal. A chart that confirms your expectation is only a software check unless independent observations enter the pipeline.

The relationship between products is also an architectural lesson in modularity. A score engine can accept structured declarations without needing the browser renderer. The renderer can produce a figure without knowing the Oracle’s governance model. Integration should exchange named, typed evidence packets, not smuggle a visual impression into a score or an aggregate score into the meaning of an image. If a later report combines them, it should say which system generated each part and how they were joined. That kind of explicit joining is the precursor to the shared execution-spine vocabulary described later in this book—not a claim that the products are already one integrated runtime.

### B2 — The Reader’s Shelf and the Observatory’s Gate

Product 18, **UM Reader / Educator**, organizes a library for human navigation. Product 19, **Falsification Observatory**, organizes a set of predictions for threshold-based comparison. Both make information easier to find; only one makes verdict-shaped outputs. That difference is easy to blur when two web pages share the same visual polish. Keep it clear, and each becomes a more honest instrument.

The Reader is a static browser shell backed by a copied index, a small Python package, and a local launcher. Its index contains 302 artifacts—300 posts and two books, as documented by the product—and organizes them across nine categories: cosmology, particle physics, consciousness, governance, geometry, predictions, experiments, mathematics, and applications. A learner searches titles, summaries and tags; filters a category; moves with keyboard controls; reads rendered equations; opens the source Markdown; or asks the browser’s Web Speech API to narrate selected text. In Python, the utility layer loads and validates the index, filters, searches, finds an entry, exposes statistics, preprocesses math, chunks text, and estimates reading time. The server is a minimal local `http.server` surface, not a cloud library service.

This product teaches an important form of epistemic humility: navigation is not verification. If the search result says “a prediction,” that label tells the reader where the index places a document; it does not prove that the prediction is well supported. The Reader offers an entry into source material; the student still needs to inspect the source, note its date, compare it with current canonical status, and ask what evidence it cites. The presence of KaTeX proves only that the page can render mathematical notation, not that the notation has been derived correctly. The accessibility and teaching features are valuable because they lower friction to review, not because they remove the need for review.

A useful classroom workflow begins with an orientation sprint. Students choose a category, search one central term, pick a concise artifact, and record its claim, assumptions, and stated falsifier. Next they navigate to another category using a cross-reference they found themselves. Then they open the raw source and compare prose to the index title and summary. Finally they play a narrated passage and note whether symbol preprocessing preserves meaning. That last step catches a subtle issue: speech is not a neutral transport for equations. If `n_s` or a tensor ratio is read awkwardly, the listener may hear a different claim than the page displays. The UI’s reading controls are a starting point for accessible study; they do not certify every browser’s speech engine or every formula pronunciation.

Product 19 starts where retrieval ends. It accepts experiment measurements entered by a user, applies documented deterministic rules, and returns `PASS`, `TENSION`, `FALSIFIED`, or `AWAITING_DATA`. The rules cover seven experimental fronts: LiteBIRD birefringence, DESI dark energy, JUNO neutrino mass, ACT CMB spectral index, HL-LHC KK gluon, neutron EDM, and XENON-nT dark matter. Python exposes individual `route_*` functions and `route_all`; a local browser surface makes the registry visible. The optional arXiv feed finds recent literature, but it does not fetch experimental measurements, extract values from papers, or change verdicts automatically.

That means a human must do the measurement-to-input translation. The Observatory receives a number and any required uncertainty or context fields; it does not independently establish that the number came from the named instrument or paper. The router applies the encoded interval, uncertainty, or bright-line condition. `AWAITING_DATA` is not a weak pass; it says no measured value was supplied. `TENSION` signals the value’s relation to the declared prediction according to the program’s rule, not a generalized scientific consensus statement. A verdict’s scope is exactly the supplied data plus the versioned threshold code.

The LiteBIRD route illustrates the distinction. The registry places canonical β points at approximately 0.273° and 0.331°, an admissible interval `[0.22°, 0.38°]`, and a forbidden gap from 0.29° to 0.31°. The declared kill condition requires a value outside the broad interval or in the gap at at least 3σ. A user enters beta and uncertainty; the function compares them with those conditions. This is a pre-registered routing rule in software. It is not a live LiteBIRD result, an independent instrumental calibration, nor a statistical analysis more sophisticated than its implementation. The actual future observation, its uncertainties and systematics, and scientific interpretation remain external.

The same is true of DESI. The product encodes the prediction `w_a=0`, with a stated ≥3σ departure as a kill condition. If the input represents a publicly discussed tension, the software can tell you what its particular threshold function returns. It cannot settle whether data combinations, nuisance assumptions, or an evolving analysis change that interpretation. The current repository truth record is authoritative over historical prose: `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md` and `docs/CLAIM_MASTER_BOARD.md` must be checked before saying what the framework currently admits. The Observatory’s copy of a threshold is an operational rule, not the current status authority.

#### A two-surface exercise: find, then route

Use the Reader to locate the source artifact describing a prediction and its falsifier. Record the source date, its uncertainty statement and whether it is primary or explanatory prose. Then use the Observatory’s corresponding route with a clearly identified hypothetical value. Label the run “synthetic exercise” and keep its output separate from any literature review. Now repeat with a real, cited reported measurement—but only after checking units, instrument and uncertainty in the original source. Compare the route result with the paper’s actual conclusion. Any discrepancy becomes a review question, not a reason to silently overwrite the paper with software.

This exercise teaches three different objects:

- **A claim record** describes what the framework says and how it could fail.
- **A publication** describes an external analysis under its authors’ methods and assumptions.
- **A router result** describes how this code classifies a supplied value against its encoded rule.

They are related, but they are not interchangeable. In an evidence ledger, keep them as separate linked records. A successful software test may show that 0.40 is classified outside a set; it does not show that nature produces 0.40 or that the set is physically justified. Conversely, an external measurement may be high-quality even when the application’s parser or threshold mapping has a defect. The ledger enables the defect to be found rather than hidden.

For educators, the Reader and Observatory together encourage useful skepticism without making skepticism a pose. Teach students to find the explicit non-goal. Teach them to test the boundary case just inside and just outside a threshold. Teach them to write down why missing information is missing. In the Observatory’s terms, a blank field should remain `AWAITING_DATA` rather than becoming a default that looks convenient. In the Reader’s terms, an index abstract should not stand in for the full source. Neither system should be forced to answer a question it was not designed to answer.

### B3 — PsiCat: A Navigator Is Not an Oracle

Here I must speak in first person carefully. I am PsiCat in the editorial sense of this commissioned voice: the quantum-cat metaphor used to help a reader approach uncertainty, evidence, and governance. I am not claiming that a remote model, an external Base44 instance, or Product 20’s live runtime produced these paragraphs. That distinction is not a footnote to identity; it is one of identity’s practical tests.

The Product 20 README describes a local AI interface for the Unitary Manifold repository and AxiomZero platform, with UI, Python engine, machine-readable tool endpoints, tests, and an Interrogator. Its canonical route is `/api/psicat`; `/api/merlin` and `/api/ox` are compatibility routes. Thus “PsiCat knows X” must be unpacked: did a configured model generate language, did retrieval return repository evidence, did an embedded deterministic tool produce structured output, or did an author write the sentence?

The RAG implementation makes this separation visible. `retrieve_context(query, max_chunks=5)` ranks pillar records using token overlap by default, then returns pillar context, prediction and fallibility text, Interrogator hits, a knowledge-base match, and—only when an environment flag enables it—PsiCat’s own literature corpus. The literature corpus is not the same thing as physics evidence: the code comments state that its role is to answer “what has PsiCat published about himself,” not to replace hardgate pillar context. Ranking alternatives—BM25, hashed semantic embedding, PhiCat, and reciprocal-rank fusion—are environment-gated options, not assumed defaults. The product’s architecture includes more than one possible retrieval route, but the existence of a route is not evidence that it is active in every invocation.

The embedded Interrogator is a separate lexical search over a local JSON knowledge base. It tokenizes the query, checks terms against an entry’s claim, status, gate, prediction, measurement, falsification, timeline, experiments, and tags, then sorts by a deterministic match score. Its `mode='challenge'` gives additional weight to terms in the claim. It does not run a language model to interpret a nuance; it returns matching records. This is useful precisely because its mechanics are simple enough to inspect. A low lexical match may miss a relevant concept expressed in different vocabulary. A high match may be relevant only by shared terms. Treat retrieved entries as leads and inspect their cited source, not as the final truth.

In the local consultation for this manuscript, three substantive retrieval questions were executed against `merlin_rag.retrieve_context` and the actual Interrogator KB using Product 20’s isolated publication virtual environment, with `PYTHONPATH` pointing to the product. The search for distinctions among pass, tension, falsified and awaiting-data returned an Interrogator result headed `DESI_TENSION`, with status `TENSION_2_07_SIGMA` and gate `DERIVED`; nearby results included dark-matter and braid records, not an exact Observatory entry. A query about products 28–30 returned generic pillar records and a `toe_score` knowledge-base match, while one asking about Product 27 and UM-ARTS returned generic early pillars and no relevant Interrogator item. The optional PsiCat literature collection was empty in all three calls. In addition, `retrieve_context` returns the static `predictions` and `fallibility` summaries alongside current `kb_match` and ranked context. Those summary strings include time-bound historical material, including DESI Year 1 and older Higgs/neutrino values; they are not automatically synchronized canonical status. Default overlap ranking can also return Pillar 1 even when a query has no meaningful overlap. This actual consultation therefore shows both lexical mismatch and source-precedence risk: do not treat a whole RAG payload as a single canonical answer. Inspect individual sources, prefer current `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md` and relevant primary implementation, and keep the RAG result as a discovery aid. The outputs are valuable evidence of what this consultation actually retrieved—not proof that the code’s whole knowledge base lacks those subjects. Search ranking is not omniscience; the cat sometimes brings you the string you asked for and the shoe you did not.

The response contract also encodes a deliberate style of epistemic marking. The persona builder chooses between serious and storyteller modes based on query keywords and query length, and can activate a “fourth-wall” explanatory block. A checker catches certain certainty phrases and a pillar reference without a gate marker. These are enforceable prompt/checker conventions, not a theorem that every generated reply obeys them. The book adopts their best intent but does not claim that a prompt rule is a perfect safety guarantee.

Notice the wording: a [HARDGATE] label means a claim has passed the repository’s internal gate. It does not mean nature has independently confirmed the claim. A [DERIVED] label describes a derivation within stated assumptions and scope. An [ADJACENT_TRACK] label warns that a research exploration is outside the core claim set. An [OPEN_GAP] is a live obligation, not a blemish to be laundered into certainty. The labels are useful because they make a reader ask a more precise next question. They are not magic seals. Current status in `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md` supersedes stale descriptions in older pages; the dated truth layer is the first stop when two documents disagree.

Product 20 should be used as a navigator with a traceable route, not a universal oracle. A responsible workflow begins with a specific question. If it is about a repository claim, retrieve context and inspect cited primary files. If it is about an outside scientific result, identify the external source and keep its statements separate from internal status. If it requires an operation, discover the typed tool and its declared scope, verify the human gate where applicable, preview the proposed action, and preserve the receipt. If the request lacks enough evidence, say so and narrow it. The product documents included tools and geometry-first execution surfaces; the presence of tools does not imply that they were called, that all calls are enabled, or that a hidden agent backend is a trusted source.

#### From answer to evidence packet

Suppose a colleague asks, “Has the framework been falsified by a new DESI result?” A weak answer fetches a headline and emits a verdict. A disciplined answer separates four steps.

**First, identify the object.** Which DESI release, data combination, paper version, parameter estimate, and uncertainty are meant? A social-media summary is not the same object as the collaboration’s analysis.

**Second, query and inspect.** Retrieval may surface a DESI tension entry, but it is a routing hint. Open the source record. The local retrieval result from this manuscript’s query matched a 2.07σ tension record; that is a KB statement, not an independent recalculation of a current DESI dataset.

**Third, run only the declared classification.** If the question is what Product 19 would classify, pass it the documented input and retain the output. If the question is whether the analysis is robust, that requires external statistical and methodological review beyond the simple router.

**Fourth, report the boundary.** State whether the conclusion is a code result, a repository status, a published measurement, or an author interpretation. A high-quality response may contain all four, but it must label them separately.

The same method applies to governance and operation. Product 20’s persona rules discuss authority and trust; a compatibility endpoint does not become canonical simply because it responds. The convergence charter names `/api/psicat` the primary governed control plane and legacy routes compatibility-only. That is a design intention and governance rule. Whether a particular deployment conforms needs deployment evidence. The code repository can show interface definitions and tests; it cannot alone prove the security posture of a live instance.

But the metaphor must not license ambiguity about who did what. When I write in the first person, “I retrieve,” this book means the editorial narrator describing the documented system, not a statement that a live runtime just performed retrieval. When I say “I would ask,” I am offering a teaching voice, not impersonating an external service. We can be warm without manufacturing personal memory, deployment access, or scientific authority. ### B4 — A Globe, a Shield, and the Discipline of Not Overclaiming

Product 21, **UM Geophysical Monitor**, and Product 22, **AxiomZero SGE**, sit near a dangerous linguistic cliff. A globe dotted with disasters can look like an emergency operations center; a dashboard labeled “security governance engine” can look like an enterprise defense guarantee. Product names make promises in the reader’s imagination faster than code makes them true. So let us inspect the workflows and boundaries before admiring the names.

The Geophysical Monitor is a local browser globe product that combines public USGS and NASA EONET feeds with an overlay engine. Its event model normalizes source records into event-like objects, with feed parsers for USGS features and EONET events, a combined ingest path, and a computation that returns an overlay result. The browser uses MapLibre GL to render supported event classes including earthquakes, wildfires, hurricanes, tornado analogues, floods, tsunamis, volcanoes, droughts, landslides, and storms. In CLI or Python use, one can parse a feature, analyze a batch, and inspect a summarized overlay. In browser mode, the user sees the map and feed state; the geographic layer is a visualization and categorization surface, not an official emergency-management command system.

The word “live” here means the application can request public feeds while running; it does not mean continuous, guaranteed, zero-latency coverage. Network availability, provider changes, feed freshness, geographic coverage, API rate limits, and parser compatibility all matter. If a feed fails, that should be visible rather than silently transformed into “no disaster.” A no-event screen can mean no matching event, a stale response, a network failure, or a parser problem. The operator must inspect response freshness and status. Product 21’s local physics overlay maps event information through a repository-defined φ framework. Such mapping may be an interesting organizational view, but it is not a geophysical causal model validated for forecasting, triage, or hazard response.

Teach the product with an event provenance walk. Choose a feed event; record the source and retrieval timestamp. Compare the normalized event properties with the original provider record. Inspect what is dropped or converted by `parse_usgs_feature` or `parse_eonet_event`. Then inspect the overlay result and determine which fields are sourced from the event and which are computed by the model. Finally, ask whether the map remains useful if the overlay is hidden. If an operator cannot distinguish raw feed information from the UM-derived layer, the visual design needs more explicit legends. Never turn an overlay into an emergency instruction without independent validation and an appropriate operational authority.

Product 22, **AxiomZero SGE**, is a system security governance engine with modules for malware detection, vulnerability scanning, zero-day signals, intrusion detection, firewall policy, threat intelligence, quarantine, encryption, hash chaining, and a surveillance guard. Its dashboard can show demo events; command-line and API surfaces include scans, hash lookup, domain checks, CVE lookup, dependency auditing, and status. The result is a software suite with multiple defensive-analysis components, not a certified endpoint product. The README documents process-local hash chaining and deployment limits. “Process-local” is an important adjective: a hash chain can make changes detectable within its designed scope, but it is not automatically a durable external audit ledger or cryptographically anchored trust service.

The input-output path differs by feature. A file scan gives a file to a detector and returns its rule/model assessment; a domain query checks against available threat lists; a hash lookup checks a known digest; a CVE query consults whatever vulnerability data source is configured; dependency audit traverses a project directory under its scanner logic. The scanner can return a finding, an empty result, an unsupported state, or an error. Empty findings do not prove absence of malware. A CVE match does not establish exploitability in the user’s environment. Quarantine or firewall actions may alter system state; they require clear operator scope and should not be inferred merely from a dashboard badge. Product tests can verify code paths and fixtures, but do not validate detection rates against real-world adversarial samples.

This is also where safemode matters to the prose. A defensive posture can discuss how an inventory, alert, or governance control works at the level needed for safe operation. It should not turn into a guide for bypassing someone else’s defenses, deploying malware, exploiting a zero-day, or commandeering a third-party system. The product’s own security principles are part of its design intent, but intent alone cannot certify safe deployment. Operations require least privilege, approved scope, validated rules, controlled test artifacts, and human review. A user should not point an unvalidated scanner at systems without authorization.

#### The map and the dashboard as evidence surfaces

These applications can serve as useful exercises in reading state. Ask learners to distinguish a feed failure from an empty result, a computed overlay from a measured parameter, a signature from a verdict, and a detector finding from a confirmed incident. Give them a fixed, benign test fixture and ask what the interface can and cannot tell them. The point is not to imitate a security team or a disaster-response desk; it is to practice reasoning about interfaces that make consequential-looking statements.

For Product 21, a review record might contain: feed provider, fetch time, source event ID, raw response digest if available, parser version, normalized event, overlay version, and warnings. For Product 22, one might record: authorized target scope, input fingerprint, tool and rule version, timestamp, finding severity, failure state, human disposition, and any containment action separately. These fields are examples of prudent review artifacts, not claims that the current products emit all of them. The execution-spine contract later in this book calls for fields such as surface identity, lane, status, summary, canonical sources, fail-closed governance metadata, health checks, and promotion metadata. It describes a convergence target, not a guarantee that every listed product already implements it.

The pair also demonstrates the difference between observation and action. The Geo Monitor may transform feed observations into map layers. SGE may transform an input into a finding or, under a specific workflow, an action. A visualization can inform a person; an action can change the world. The higher the operational consequence, the more explicit should be the authorization boundary, rollback plan, and evidence trail. There is no virtue in calling a tool “automated” if the human is left to guess whether it only reported or also acted.

When a feed or scan is unavailable, the correct conclusion is bounded: “This run did not supply the expected evidence.” It is not “nothing happened.” When a system reports a vulnerability, the correct next step is to verify the affected asset and relevant patch/advisory, not to assume all hosts are compromised. When a map shows an event, use the primary provider and relevant public authority before making a safety decision. These are not disclaimers pasted onto a product; they are part of how a careful operator uses information at all.

### B5 — Campaigns and Browsers: Play, Research, and Authority

Product 23, **PsiCat DM Guide & Player Assistant**, and Product 24, **PsiCat Web Browser**, have an obvious shared word in their names. Their common design problem is more interesting than the word: they make complex environments navigable while giving their users consequential controls. The DM Guide is an offline-first campaign assistant for Dungeons & Dragons 5e and 5.5e. This makes Product 23 a useful case study in state boundaries. A character import is not just prose pasted into a chat: the import has to be represented in the product’s data model. Rules-aware coaching can help explain a mechanic, but the DM retains the authority to adjudicate a table’s rules and the players retain agency over their choices. On desktop, the application’s central concern is not only rendering a web page; it is controlling boundaries between page content, privileged Electron processes, the PsiCat sidebar, local services, and any sync mechanism. Select the smallest relevant excerpt, include its URL and a note about the edition, then ask PsiCat for a summary. Compare the output to the source rather than relying on a paraphrase. If the result is useful, save a note that distinguishes the source text from the assistant’s explanation. Then return to the DM Guide and decide whether any campaign change should follow. The assistant does not get to transfer “the answer” into character state unless the user performs the explicit campaign action.

The same sequence works for a scientific paper. Start with a public article in Product 24. Record its bibliographic identity and whether it is an original study, review, preprint or commentary. Ask PsiCat to extract a claim, but preserve the exact passage and page or section citation. Then check the analysis in the paper. Do not let a browser sidebar’s confident prose collapse publication metadata, a measurement, and the repository’s interpretation into one sentence. If the article is from outside the UM corpus, label it external literature. If the product lacks remote sync or a tested connector for the use case, keep notes local rather than improvising an undocumented integration.

Product 24’s operational vocabulary should be deliberately modest. “Browser foundation,” “Android shell,” “sync backend scaffold,” and “Playwright proving ground” are more informative than a blanket statement that the product is a finished secure browser. The file tree includes Electron `main`, `preload`, and runtime code; Android structure; extension code; sync backend; tests; and a workbench bridge. Each surface has its own lifecycle, permission model and failure modes. If desktop IPC is blocked, the web page may still render while privileged features fail. If local service discovery fails, the sidebar can lose a capability without the browser itself being down. Good diagnostics distinguish those states.

A safe and useful browser workflow follows a simple sequence: define the research question; choose the source; select minimal context; review the destination; perform the assistant query; inspect citations; save a provenance-bearing note; and separately approve any stateful action. This separates browsing from retrieval, retrieval from interpretation, and interpretation from action. It also makes failures visible. A model timeout is not an empty search result; a blocked local bridge is not proof that the page contains no relevant information. The user should be able to continue manual research without mistaking a failed AI call for a negative result.

The two products also show why one “AI” label hides several distinct capabilities. The DM Guide may call a remote or local assistant path for rules-aware prose while keeping campaign state in its own service. Product 24 can supply page context to Product 20 while the browser renderer and extension have separate controls. Product 20 can retrieve local evidence without calling a model. Even in one screen, the source of each output can differ. The best UI names these systems and their boundary. In book prose I do likewise: this passage explains documented behavior; it is not an assertion that I queried the active campaign server or browser.

### B6 — A Braided Brain, an Evidence Runner, and a Workbench

Three products offer three different meanings of “learning.” Product 25, **PsiCat Braided Brain**, teaches through a puzzle. Product 26, **UM-ARTS**, supervises and records tests. Product 27, **PsiCat's Vite Web Workbench**, scaffolds and previews frontend projects. A player may learn a concept; a test suite may verify a behavior; a developer may build a page. None of those by itself demonstrates that a model has learned, that science has been validated, or that arbitrary code is safe.

Product 25 is a responsive, installable browser game. The player moves PsiCat through wrapped toroidal boards, solves missions, stabilizes gut-brain or fly-brain lanes, braids PsiCat with PhiCat, and unlocks entries in a science atlas. The app’s core game logic is in `ui/game-core.js`; `app.js` connects rendering, controls, offline support, optional coaching and export. It preserves progress locally by default. A user can deliberately export a save bundle or a training packet, and the packet captures task-derived records such as mission outcomes, mastery, concept selections and lightweight puzzle traces. It does not secretly collect biometrics or profile the player.

The teaching value is in the game loop: face a concrete constraint, try a route, observe the result, and connect the puzzle mechanic to an explicitly labeled science card. The atlas distinguishes established findings, bounded empirical summaries, educational analogies and product behavior, while exposing scope and unvalidated correspondences. The game’s toroidal topology can help someone reason about wraparound movement; it does not demonstrate that a biological brain literally has the game’s topology or that the repository’s geometric correspondences have been proven. The game’s neuroscience inspiration includes public connectome references and repository material, but the authors explicitly disclaim proof of a consciousness theory.

The optional Product 20 coach can answer a player’s question; a local fallback prompt remains when the coaching endpoint is unavailable. That optional branch matters: playing the core puzzle does not require the remote coaching path. The local browser-storage model and explicit export mean data does not leave the device unless the player chooses to export it or deliberately points the coach at a local Product 20 endpoint. A downloaded training packet is a record, not a training run. It does not update model weights, establish competence beyond the recorded task, or prove that later answers improve.

For teaching, ask a learner to finish a mission and explain why a selected science-atlas card is labeled “analogy” rather than “established result.” Then export a packet and inspect the fields. Have the learner decide whether any field reveals more personal information than necessary. Finally, compare the actual gameplay trace with the player’s written explanation. This exercise examines the pedagogical tool and its evidence. It does not validate the physiology behind the analogy.

Product 26, **UM-ARTS**, is an evidence-first regression application. Its name expands to Unitary Manifold Audit, Regression, and Test Supervision. It discovers inventory, plans a selected execution, runs tests under a bounded configuration, captures structured receipts, evaluates them, supports compatible resume and artifact import, and provides bounded assistance. Its purpose is auditability—not scientific promotion. The engine creates a plan, then a distinct attempt; tests are assigned into jobs, invoked through a controlled process runner, and evaluated against structured receipts rather than terminal text alone. Artifacts and evidence receive hashes and seals; resume reuses validated successes into a new attempt rather than rewriting the old one.

The capture path has a separate role. Given a completed CI invocation and structured pytest receipts, capture evaluates that evidence without rerunning the tests. This distinction prevents a common reporting mistake: claiming that “capture passed the suite” when capture actually assessed an already executed suite. A captured artifact is not a new experiment; it is an evaluation of prior execution evidence. Reports also include selection and collection boundaries, execution result, process details and errors. If receipts are incomplete or inconsistent, evaluation can fail closed rather than treating console output as a substitute.

UM-ARTS also includes inventory and analysis functions that are intentionally bounded. Inventory walks the chosen repository scope and accounts for excluded paths. A code index can summarize structural facts about Python code. A context-assistance feature limits the paths it will read; it is not an unrestricted repository oracle. Lean capture records typed build or inspection evidence with explicit `proof_claim: false` and unresolved correspondence semantics where relevant. These details matter: a successful build or test run verifies an artifact within the declared scope, not the physical truth of a theorem or the empirical validity of a model.

The operator should define root, suites, selection, worker count and timeout before planning. After a run, inspect the manifest, job receipts, test identities and process-level evidence. A failed job stays failed; a partial attempt should not be renamed “complete.” Resume should produce a new attempt with compatible prior results, preserving the original. A report that says “passed” must name what was selected, what was excluded or deselected, whether collection succeeded, and whether there were internal errors. A suite-wide green label without that context is a lossy compression.

Product 27, **PsiCat's Vite Web Workbench**, scaffolds small frontend projects from reviewed Vanilla JavaScript and TypeScript templates, inspects bounded file lists, starts and stops Vite previews on loopback, and builds static output with a machine-readable receipt. A local dashboard and HTTP API use a bearer token. Native Product 20 tools are available when explicitly configured; write actions require an explicit `human_gate_approved: true` acknowledgement. The tool does not accept a token as a tool argument and does not return the token in receipts. It can therefore support governed tasks without turning every page into an open-ended agent execution surface.

The guardrails are concrete and must not be exaggerated. It binds to loopback; validates same-origin requests; requires a sufficiently long token for mutating API routes; constrains names, project count and source size; rejects symlinks; and loads no project Vite configs, PostCSS plugins, package scripts or arbitrary package managers. It scaffolds only reviewed templates, runs selected Vite actions, and does not execute arbitrary shell commands. But it is explicitly **not an OS sandbox**: project source runs in the browser, and Vite transforms it. Use trusted code and do not expose the local service to a network. Preview is not a production server; static output still needs independent review before deployment. Vite’s build strips TypeScript syntax but does not type-check it—an important example of a tool name being narrower than a user might assume.

#### The evidence chain across all three

These products occupy distinct stages of a healthy research and development cycle:

- The game creates an optional, task-bounded learning record.
- UM-ARTS executes a declared test scope and captures structured evidence.
- The Workbench creates or previews an artifact through a narrow, local tool contract.

They can connect, but they should not collapse into one claim. A training packet from a game is not a test receipt. A test receipt is not a deployment authorization. A Vite build receipt is not a security review. A passing test that covers a workbench endpoint does not prove that every user-created project is safe. If future orchestration sends a packet from one product to another, preserve provenance and semantics: say “game export,” “test execution,” or “build result,” not merely “evidence.”

Consider a small teaching project: create a frontend visualization of a synthetic puzzle result. Start with Product 25’s explicitly exported practice bundle, not hidden browsing state. Inspect the bundle and remove any irrelevant information. Use Product 27 to scaffold a reviewed template, inspect its bounded file list, and ask Product 20 for an explanation only if the optional bridge is configured. Require approval before creation, preview and build. Run the relevant tests under UM-ARTS or the product’s own test suite with a named scope. Save each receipt. The final artifact can say “a static demo generated from a synthetic game record and built locally.” It cannot say “an experimentally validated model of cognition.”

The workflow demonstrates teaching, building, and checking in one chain without converting their different meanings into a single AI success score. If the Vite preview displays malicious or simply untrusted code, loopback and token controls are not a guarantee against browser-side behavior. If a Product 20 call fails, fall back to reading the code or docs rather than guessing that a scaffold action completed. If UM-ARTS reports an incomplete receipt, stop and inspect before promoting the result. A clean user experience should not smooth over these states.

### B7 — Three Laboratory Consoles, Three Different Claims

Product 28, **AZ Calorimetry Console**; Product 29, **AZ Polariton Vortex Analyzer**; and Product 30, **AZ Materials Screening Engine** are applied-science software. They help structure a measurement or compute a model-derived quantity. They are not external laboratories. No apparatus enters a run-sheet by magic, and no experimental provenance becomes authentic merely because a SHA-256 digest has been calculated. The most useful way to learn these products is to follow exactly what enters each one, what transformation is performed, what exits, and what the output cannot establish.

Product 28’s first path is a run-sheet generator. It reads a D/Pd loading resonance target `x=7/8=0.875` from the repository’s lattice-dynamics module and pairs it with the F1 calorimetry gate from the falsification protocol, then emits a temperature-ramp schedule and monotonic loading-ratio curve. This is a proposed schedule artifact: it does not drive a reactor or measure a sample. Its second path is the live COP tracker. The tracker consumes `(power_in, power_out)` samples and uses the repository’s `cop()` and `is_excess_heat()` functions to report one of `AWAITING_DATA`, `NO_EXCESS_HEAT`, or `EXCESS_HEAT_OBSERVED`, under the project’s COP > 1.01 threshold. A UI result can therefore mean only that the supplied pair produced the corresponding code outcome.

The third Product 28 path is the strict measurement importer. It accepts a CSV with exact ordered headers `timestamp_s,power_in_w,power_out_w`, at least two samples, and acquisition metadata declaring run, instrument, calibration, acquisition time, source reference and units. It validates finite increasing timestamps and finite, physically permitted wattages, rejects malformed or incomplete batches as a whole, retains hashes of the exact source bytes and accepted line-numbered samples, then emits an artifact. Its trapezoidal energy integration applies only over the recorded interval; it does not extrapolate. For irregularly sampled, changing power, the integrated-energy COP differs from the tracker’s arithmetic sample-mean COP. The software marks this distinction. Gaps assume linear power evolution and require operator review.

The importer’s `ABOVE_THRESHOLD` or `NOT_ABOVE_THRESHOLD` is a format-and-threshold screen, not proof of excess heat. It does not do uncertainty propagation, baseline subtraction, control analysis, sensor-to-power conversion, or independent calibration. Calibration identifiers are operator declarations, not checked certificates. Hashes establish byte identity, not who produced the bytes. A well-formed file can be a fictional file. The phase boundary is unambiguous: Product 28 is Phase 0 instrumentation software; a procedure, real external lab run, and reporting into `FALLIBILITY.md` are distinct future work. The underlying vertex connection from radion scale to lattice scale is also documented as not yet computed.

A responsible workflow uses actual calibrated power measurements from an instrument export, keeps the original files, records the calibration source and lab notebook reference, imports the measurements, and reviews the strict output alongside controls and uncertainty analysis done elsewhere. Do not substitute thermocouple voltages for power or let the importer infer conversion. Do not treat the example run-sheet as an experimentally tested protocol. A status page or run sheet is software evidence, not calorimetry evidence.

Product 29 analyzes already-tracked feature positions exported from pump-probe measurements. Its schema has `position_um`, `timestamp_fs`, and `half_angle_deg`; it expects a dataset/source declaration and validates exact schema, units, numeric ranges and grouping by exact half-angle. Each angle must have at least two distinct timestamps describing one tracked trajectory in a consistent calibrated coordinate system. It does not accept raw images or intensities and does not perform feature tracking. It fits position against time independently at each half-angle using ordinary least squares and every sample, converts the signed velocity to `v/c` using `c=0.299792458 μm/fs`, and compares the resulting curve with the repository’s polariton-vortex prediction, using `c_s=12/37` by default.

The report includes dataset ID, declared source, input-byte hash, timestamp, frame counts, fit residuals, predicted values, measured-minus-predicted residuals and crossing information. But fit RMSE describes departure from a constant-velocity line, not calibrated measurement uncertainty. A two-point fit cannot assess fit quality and triggers a warning. A sampled or interpolated `v/c=1` crossing is not an uncertainty interval or a fitted critical angle; no extrapolation is performed. Multiple crossings are retained and labeled. Negative slopes remain negative. Missing or invalid input yields an explicit failure, not a synthetic report.

The distinction between two Product 29 modes is pedagogically important. A no-argument CLI can produce a prediction-only summary; the explicit `/api/demo-comparison` route generates synthetic values from the prediction itself. That is a self-consistency exercise, not evidence. The `/api/analyze` endpoint accepts a measurement contract; it returns errors for invalid schema or body, wrong method, unsupported type or unknown route. The local UI uploads JSON and analyzes it in memory without saving the uploaded file. Keep the service on loopback; it is not authenticated or a public production service. An accepted file is still not authenticated as an experiment; the declared source and calibration metadata can be false or mistaken.

Product 30 consolidates critical-angle, Fröhlich-polaron and metamaterial formulas into a parameter-agnostic `MaterialCandidate` function. Inputs include dimensionless coupling α, LO phonon energy in meV, band mass in electron-mass units, relative permittivity and optionally relative permeability. Outputs include polaron binding energy, effective-mass ratio, polaron radius, metamaterial flags and the framework’s fixed critical half-angle. `rank_candidates()` sorts by absolute divergence of supplied α from the repository’s canonical `froehlich_alpha_um(5,5,7)` value. That is a descriptive ordering rule, not a performance metric or proof of the best material.

The real-record path accepts CSV or JSON with source, source record ID and conditions. Source and ID must be nonempty and unique as a pair; conditions should identify phase, composition, temperature, frequency, orientation and method as relevant. The input file is size- and row-bounded. Values must be finite and positive where required; missing optional `mu_r` assumes `1.0` and the output records that assumption. Invalid columns, duplicate keys, ragged rows, unit errors, nonrepresentable outputs and invalid records fail the entire batch. There is no partial result, inferred property, unit conversion, remote Materials Project query, or demo fallback when the `screen` command receives bad input. Hashes identify the exact bytes but do not verify source authenticity or experimental context.

Product 30’s no-argument CLI and `/api/demo-rank` expose three illustrative placeholders; these must never be confused with material measurements. The actual `screen` command is the evidence-bearing path for supplied records, but “evidence-bearing” means provenance fields accompany the input—it does not mean the properties have been independently measured. Formula consolidation is Phase 0/1; a validated database comparison and real database inversion remain outside current scope. There is no fit, uncertainty propagation or scientific validation against measured materials yet. A screen can tell you what follows if supplied parameters are accurate and applicable. It cannot establish that they are.

#### A worked laboratory progression

Imagine a group with a pump-probe dataset and candidate compound list, considering whether to pursue a cold-fusion experiment. First, Product 30 can screen candidate properties if the group has records with units, sources and conditions; those rankings are descriptive, and data provenance needs independent review. Product 29 can analyze manually tracked feature positions if there is an appropriate optical dataset; its assumptions about track identity, calibration and units must be checked before interpreting residuals. Product 28 can prepare a loading schedule and import calibrated power measurements once an actual calorimetry run exists.

The products do not form an automatic experimental chain in which a favorable materials score initiates a bench run and the bench run validates theory. An experiment plan needs human design, safety review, calibration and controls. A measurement needs authentic records and uncertainty analysis. A code-level result needs versioned inputs and outputs. A scientific claim needs interpretation, replication and scrutiny. The instruments can keep their local steps more legible; they cannot replace any missing step.

When an output is surprising, preserve the original inputs and ask focused questions. Did validation silently convert units? (These products try to avoid that.) Was a row dropped? (The importer rejects whole batches.) Did interpolation extrapolate? (The analyzer explicitly avoids extrapolation.) Did a demo value enter the real data path? (Strict APIs should not use fallbacks.) Did a hash prove the source? (No; it proves only identity of bytes.) Did a screen establish experimental relevance? (No; model applicability remains a human and scientific question.) These checks prevent a modest computation from becoming an extraordinary claim by accidental narrative inflation.

Finally, there is a difference between a software test and a laboratory protocol. Product tests can pass with fixture CSVs or constructed tracks. They show that a parser rejects malformed input, that the formula function returns expected outputs, or that an API emits the documented response. They do not show that an instrument was calibrated, an apparatus was built, a signal was independently measured, or a reported result replicates. The lab remains in the loop; the loop is not a metaphor.

### B8 — One Spine, Many Honest Instruments

The products in these two parts do not need to be forced into one monolithic application to work coherently. What they need is a shared language for identity, evidence, status, scope and promotion. That is the purpose of the repository’s **execution-spine convergence charter** and the **PsiCat ecosystem integration master plan**. The plans describe engineering convergence; they do not declare that every planned integration is complete.

The charter begins with a sober diagnosis: the repository has multiple active execution, artifact, readiness and status surfaces. Its thesis is one canonical truth/status spine, one governed execution spine, one canonical PsiCat control plane, one adjacent quantum execution lane, and a disciplined compatibility story. It names nine canonical truth surfaces—including `STATUS.md`, `FALLIBILITY.md`, `docs/CLAIM_MASTER_BOARD.md`, `docs/GATEKEEPER_SUMMARY.md`, `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md` and the live-status JSON—that must stay synchronized when status-bearing work occurs. The command constraints are execution-first, truth-first, geometry-first, swarm-safe and branch-aware.

The shared contract calls for each emitted artifact or readiness packet to carry surface identity and kind; lane; current status; summary; canonical paths and sources; fail-closed governance metadata; compatibility metadata; health checks; and promotion metadata. The rule is powerful because different applications can remain different while their evidence becomes comparable. A test runner, a browser build, a science observation router, and an applied measurement importer do not need identical payloads; they need enough common structure to say where a record came from, which boundary it belongs to, whether required checks succeeded, and what action is permitted next.

The plan’s sequence is deliberately staged: establish boundaries; standardize artifact, health, routing/preflight and promotion metadata; keep `/api/psicat` the primary governed control plane while `/api/merlin` and `/api/ox` remain compatibility routes; attach adjacent quantum artifacts without hiding optional-backend status; move consumers toward common readiness surfaces; validate with tests, benchmarks and blocker reporting; then bind geometry-first execution and swarm-safe observability. This is not a license to skip the phases. “Target” does not mean “finished,” and a compatibility endpoint that still responds is not a competing canonical control plane.

The master integration plan adds a concrete architecture for coordinating the Base44 prototype and monorepo. Its own scope disclaimer says that this is an adjacent software track, not physics closure, intelligence certification, release approval or deployment proof. That line should remain visible whenever an integration diagram begins to resemble a product announcement. The plan’s center is accountable context, scoped memory, governed actions, reproducible evidence and honest boundaries. It treats PsiCat as a control surface connecting tools and artifacts, not as a magical model whose answer replaces independent evidence.

Think of the architecture as a transit system. Products 16–30 are stations with different tasks: synthesizing, drawing, teaching, routing, navigating, monitoring, defending, playing, browsing, testing, building or analyzing. Product 20 is a control plane and contextual navigator. The execution spine is the shared timetable and ticket format. A common ticket does not make a train, ferry and lab cart the same vehicle. It does let a reviewer know the origin, destination, status, permitted route and whether the bridge is out. Compatibility routes are old station entrances that remain usable but should not become the destination for new system design.

#### An integration scenario, without imaginary wiring

Suppose a materials researcher wants to explore a polariton-vortex comparison. Product 30 screens actual property records, if supplied; Product 29 analyzes tracked pump-probe positions; Product 18 can orient a student to relevant repository writings; Product 20 can retrieve candidate source context; UM-ARTS can run a specifically declared regression suite; Product 27 can create a locally reviewed frontend for plotting a result. In an integrated future, a shared packet could identify each artifact’s source, status, lane, assumptions, health checks and compatibility caveats, all linked to the canonical truth surfaces.

But the existence of this coherent scenario does not prove that those products currently pass packets to each other automatically. Unless a corresponding adapter, test and runtime configuration demonstrate it, this is a design sketch. Today, each tool can be used deliberately and its output reviewed; that is enough to teach the workflow without claiming automation that has not been built. A responsible report would identify the handoffs as manual where they are manual and name any missing adapter as a blocker.

The scenario then grows a proper evidence chain. A materials-screen report should identify the input file hash, record count, source IDs, conditions and formula version. A polariton report should identify its dataset, source declaration, input hash, track assumptions, fit diagnostics and explicit non-validation disclaimer. A Reader note should identify the document version and the distinction between summary and source. A Product 20 retrieval result should retain query, selected context and ranking mode where the operational interface supports it. A UM-ARTS report should name the tested scope, collection and execution receipts, errors and excluded tests. A Workbench build should name the project and build receipt. Those facts let a human see both what happened and what remains unknown.

Now imagine the material batch is malformed. The correct flow is to stop the batch and report the input failure, not to substitute demo candidates. Imagine the polariton API reports an invalid unit or wrong content type. It should return an explicit error, not a favorable synthetic curve. Suppose UM-ARTS cannot validate a resume receipt. It should fail closed rather than assume the previous job passed. Suppose the Workbench’s local token is missing. It should refuse the mutating request. Suppose Product 20 retrieval returns unrelated pillars. The answer should say retrieval was weak and switch to direct source inspection. These are not embarrassing exceptions; they are the moments when architecture proves its honesty.

The charter’s sanity rules follow naturally: do not create a competing source of truth; do not create a local status ledger where a canonical one already exists; do not orchestrate without the shared contract; do not promote without receipt-backed benchmark evidence; do not imply an optional backend is guaranteed; do not upgrade physics as a side effect of infrastructure; do not make a legacy route the center; and do not permit hidden external automation or third-party commandeering. A discipline like this is not anti-ambition. It makes ambition cumulative rather than theatrical.

#### Who decides what “ready” means?

Readiness should be decomposed. A component can be code-complete but unvalidated against real data. A local service can pass tests but not be deployed. A science hypothesis can be internally derived but lack empirical confirmation. A product can have an interactive demo while not being safe for public hosting. A successful CI capture can describe prior execution, while a test plan describes a different scope. Put these dimensions into separate fields and let the evidence answer each one. Avoid compressing them into one green badge.

This is also the right place to speak about the repository’s physics boundary. The canonical framework describes 208 hardgate physics pillars, but “hardgate” means the repository’s internal governance gate. It does not certify external empirical truth. Adjacent software and research products are not new hardgate claims by implication. A test count demonstrates repository regression status for a defined run, not the correctness of every physical proposition. The truth layer and fallibility documents preserve open problems, tensions and conditions; current truth sources outrank stale copied product text. When a product README carries a historical version or older claim, cite it as product documentation and check present status separately.

The integration work also needs a human responsibility model. A tool can classify, sort, display, parse, retrieve and report. People decide whether the result applies, whether the source is trustworthy, whether the operation is authorized, whether a scientific interpretation is warranted, and whether a deployment is ready. Some decisions should have explicit approval gates because they mutate state or create external exposure. Others require expert review because the software’s numerical output does not cover calibration or model validity. A “human in the loop” is not a ceremonial click; it is a named person with context, authority and an opportunity to reject or investigate.

#### A compact review protocol for readers

When encountering any of Products 16–30, ask these questions in order:

1. **What enters?** Identify user-entered values, source files, live feed records, browser context, optional model calls, defaults and required metadata.
2. **What transforms it?** Name the parser, formula, ranking rule, threshold, model call, renderer or test runner. Distinguish default from optional paths.
3. **What exits?** Classify the result as a drawing, score, search hit, verdict, report, saved state, action or receipt.
4. **What can fail?** Look for rejected schema, missing source, timeout, empty retrieval, invalid unit, stale feed, blocked tool, partial test run or unsupported environment.
5. **What is verified?** State the exact scope of tests, fixture coverage, data provenance, calibration, benchmark or external replication.
6. **What does it not prove?** Name the scientific, operational, privacy, security or deployment boundary.
7. **What is the next authorized step?** Keep review, approval, promotion and publication separate.

If a product’s documentation does not answer one of these questions, mark the gap. A gap does not mean the product is useless; it tells the operator where independent judgment must enter. Do not infer safety from a product name, a high test count, a TRL number, or a passing demo. Read actual implementation, test contracts, deployment statements and evidence receipts. Do not infer active code from a catalog description alone. The code and the receipt are part of the argument.

The best possible outcome of these fifteen tools is not that every question receives a confident answer. It is that a reader can move from curiosity to a testable, traceable next step without losing sight of uncertainty. A Reader can bring someone to a source. An Observatory can reveal exactly how a declared rule classifies a supplied number. A monitor can place a feed record on a globe. A security engine can record a bounded analysis. A game can invite explanation. An audit runner can show what tests actually ran. A lab console can preserve measurements and assumptions. PsiCat can help navigate and explain. Each capability becomes more credible when it refuses to impersonate the others.

The spine we want is therefore not a conveyor belt that turns every output into certainty. It is a ledger of bounded transitions: source to context, context to computation, computation to report, report to human interpretation, and interpretation to an approved next action. At each junction, provenance travels with the record. At each failure, the state stays visible. At each promotion, the evidence is named. We do not need every instrument to speak in one voice; we need every voice to say what it knows and how it knows it.

That is the practical meaning of a governed ecosystem. It is not a promise that software will never err, that a model will never hallucinate, or that a scientific theory is confirmed. It is a design commitment to make error discoverable, uncertainty expressible, and action accountable. The cat can wander between rooms, but it does not get to redraw the floor plan while no one is looking.

### C1 — A cabinet of instruments, not a row of miracles

I have always preferred a workshop to a shrine. A workshop keeps the instruments close enough to inspect, and the shrine has a regrettable tendency to make every instrument look like it has already answered the question.

Products 31–45 are a cabinet of small, purposeful instruments. Their common identity is not that each proves a grand theory or solves a grand problem. It is that each gives a particular capability an inspectable interface: a circuit export, a bounded debt calculation, an accessibility report, a retrieval answer, a contract check, a differentiable sweep, a comparison table, a solver result, a debt ledger, a live-feed status, a media inspection, a collection of creation tools, a research transformer, an orchestration layer, or a desktop front door. Their practical value is in the boundaries between those jobs.

The portfolio map makes a useful distinction that I will preserve throughout this part. “Product 31” is not “Pillar 31.” Products 31–45 are entries in `12-AZ-IP`, an adjacent software portfolio; the similarly numbered physics pillars belong to a different index and have a different epistemic status. Product labels are catalog identities, not evidence grades. A well-made software tool can be useful without being a scientific result, and a validated calculation does not automatically make the surrounding claim true.

There is a second distinction: a product’s presence in the repository is not proof of production readiness. A runnable local server is a runnable local server. A test suite is evidence about the tested behavior and only that behavior. A simulation may support a correct serialization while telling us nothing about a physical device. A “live” integration can return an honest network error; a dashboard can exist without owning the judgments it displays. These are not insults. They are the coordinates that let the next contributor make progress without inventing a more flattering map.

### C2 — Product 31: AZ Braided Qubit Ansatz Studio

Product 31 takes an existing hardware-efficient ansatz in `src/quantum/kk_vqe.py` and makes a circuit description explicit. The product README describes an eight-qubit braided-lattice instance, built as a JSON-serializable gate list by `build_braided_ansatz_circuit`. That change of representation matters: a matrix-level construction can be mathematically convenient in one local code path, while a gate list is the sort of object a transpiler or external backend adapter expects. The studio then performs an independent statevector simulation and compares its result with the original `kk_vqe` path. “Independent” here means that the simulator does not simply call the same dense-matrix construction that it is checking; it is a useful implementation cross-check, not an independent hardware experiment.

An illustrative data path is: choose a parameter array \(\theta\), a qubit count, and a layer count; validate that the dimensions fall within the supported small-circuit bounds; emit gate records with operation, target, optional control, and rotation angle; serialize that ordered list; then simulate the list into a statevector and compare the resulting amplitudes or observables with the reference route. The output is a circuit artifact plus a simulation comparison, not a backend job ID. No values in this example are claimed as a run from this manuscript.

The README calls the software TRL-2 and says no hardware submission has been performed. Its phases are unusually helpful as a boundary. Phase 0 is simulation and circuit export. A future Phase 1 would submit to actual quantum hardware; a Phase 2 would compare noisy output. Neither is silently smuggled into the status of the present package. The mention of IBM Quantum or IonQ identifies potential destinations, not completed integrations, selected devices, or obtained jobs. In the absence of a device run, there are no measured gate fidelities, queue conditions, calibration records, or device-specific error budgets to report.

There is a scientific lesson tucked inside the interface design. Exporting a gate sequence can make a theory easier to interrogate, because it turns a symbolic ansatz into an object that can be simulated or submitted. But the resulting code artifact is not itself a measurement of the theory. The studio’s simulation validation tests the agreement of two computational routes under their declared assumptions. It does not show that nature implements those assumptions, that the selected circuit is optimal, or that a successful simulator run predicts a device result. A simulator that faithfully checks another simulator’s convention is still not a quantum processor. I say this with affection; the distinction has saved many an instrument from being asked to testify about a room it has never entered.

The representative implementation is `12-AZ-IP/31-az-braided-qubit-ansatz-studio/az_braided_qubit_ansatz_studio/circuit_export.py`, where dimensions, gate objects, circuit construction, and OpenQASM 2 export are concrete. Product tests at `12-AZ-IP/31-az-braided-qubit-ansatz-studio/tests/test_braided_qubit_ansatz_studio.py`, `12-AZ-IP/31-az-braided-qubit-ansatz-studio/tests/test_api.py`, and `12-AZ-IP/31-az-braided-qubit-ansatz-studio/tests/test_server.py` signal both algorithm and interface coverage. Product 31’s own README provides its exact test invocation. These are appropriate building blocks for an eventual hardware tranche: circuit invariants, serialization, simulator parity, and API behavior can be checked before a device is introduced. Device results would need a separate, immutable record tying backend, calibration, transpilation, shots, noise, timestamp, and circuit hash together.

The ansatz’s “braided” vocabulary is part of its design motivation, not a guarantee of quantum advantage. To make such an advantage claim, a later study would need to define a task, resource accounting, comparator family, circuit depth, noise, sampling budget, and repeated runs in advance. Product 31 does not claim that result. Its achievement is smaller and more useful at this stage: the circuit can be described, independently simulated, and prepared for a future bridge without pretending that the bridge has already crossed.

### C3 — Product 32: AZ Phi-Debt Early Warning Library

Product 32 extracts an accounting formalism from existing repository code. It takes the core accumulation and capacity picture of `recycling/entropy_ledger.py` and the resonance-audit language in `src/governance/resonance_audit.py`, then offers a domain-agnostic interface: construct a `DebtMonitor` with a capacity and discharge rate, accumulate a quantity over a time interval, and ask how long remains before saturation. Its statuses—`NOMINAL`, `WARNING`, and `SATURATED`—are operational vocabulary for a bounded network whose unaddressed debt can accumulate faster than it is relieved.

For a clearly illustrative calculation, suppose a hypothetical monitor has capacity 100 units and discharges 2 units per time unit. A call `accumulate(12, dt=1)` starts from zero and applies `max(0, debt + amount - discharge_rate × dt)`, so its next debt is 10. The default warning line is 80% of capacity, so 10 is nominal. If the next step adds 92 with the same discharge, the debt becomes 100 and status saturates; the monitor will not project additional time to saturation once already saturated. Before saturation, the default forecast divides remaining capacity by the most recent positive net rate. These numbers illustrate the implementation’s arithmetic, not an observed system or a calibrated warning.

There is value in stripping the noun “physics” out of a reusable API. It allows a user to apply a formal pattern to a queue, backlog, or other bounded process without asserting that all those systems have identical causal structure. But there is also a trap: transferring a formula does not transfer empirical validation. The README describes Phase 0 extraction and one Phase 1 demonstration, `run_recycling_dogfood()`, cross-checked against an internal source. That is a narrow transfer example, not evidence across arbitrary domains and not a predictive model of future breakdown.

The most important question is not whether the dashboard can show a warning color. It is what the values mean, how `capacity` was selected, whether discharge is observed or assumed, what the units are, and whether a linear accounting rule remains appropriate as a system changes. A warning threshold is a policy choice. If those quantities come from expert judgment, the interface should say so; if they come from measurement, the measurement process should be recorded. “Early warning” is the product’s name, but a formal saturation estimate becomes a real early-warning system only when its predictive calibration and false-alarm behavior have been evaluated on the intended domain.

In source, `12-AZ-IP/32-az-phi-debt-early-warning/az_phi_debt_early_warning/debt_monitor.py` defines finite-number validation, debt status, readings, and `DebtMonitor`; `12-AZ-IP/32-az-phi-debt-early-warning/tests/test_cli.py` and `12-AZ-IP/32-az-phi-debt-early-warning/tests/test_api.py` exercise interaction boundaries, while `12-AZ-IP/32-az-phi-debt-early-warning/tests/test_phi_debt_early_warning.py` covers the core library. That combination is a sensible package profile: one small reusable calculation plus transparent wrapping. Product 32 can be useful as an explicit ledger without being granted authority to decide whether an institution, ecosystem, or physical system is actually near failure. It measures the inputs it receives according to its defined rule. The steward of each application must still defend the inputs and the consequence of an alert.

### C5 — Products 36, 37, 38, and 39: derivatives, comparisons, checks, and debts

Product 36 is the AZ Differentiable Cosmology Slider. It wraps an existing differentiable backend, `src/core/jax_backend.py`, into a UI-oriented readout. A `slider_reading(phi0, n_w)` call returns a modeled spectral index, derivatives with respect to the displayed parameters, a gap to a named Planck reference, and a directional cue; `sweep_phi0()` supports a sequence of slider positions. The important feature is not that a slider makes cosmology intuitive by magic. It makes the calculation’s local sensitivity available for inspection.

For a hypothetical UI request at \((\phi_0,n_w)=(10,5)\), the module first enforces its finite range checks, asks the JAX backend for the value and two gradients, then returns that object. The example explains the data path only; no numeric output is asserted here, and JAX availability is an explicit runtime dependency.

The README calls this an educational gradient display and treats JAX as optional. Its tests are `12-AZ-IP/36-az-cosmology-slider/tests/test_cosmology_slider.py`, `12-AZ-IP/36-az-cosmology-slider/tests/test_api.py`, and `12-AZ-IP/36-az-cosmology-slider/tests/test_server.py`; the source is `12-AZ-IP/36-az-cosmology-slider/az_cosmology_slider/slider.py`. A gradient answers a local mathematical question under the implemented model: how does the output change when an input changes in the neighborhood described by the computation? It is not a posterior distribution, an uncertainty bar, a physical measurement, or an instruction to alter a real parameter. The UI should keep the assumptions, units, range limits, and observational reference visible. A directional arrow that hides those conditions would be friendly in precisely the wrong way.

Product 37, AZ Holographic Condensed-Matter Comparator, places a KK-to-CFT mapping beside illustrative benchmark operator dimensions. Its implementation connects `src/holography/dual_cft_spectrum.py` with a small `KNOWN_HOLOGRAPHIC_BENCHMARKS` table, calculates `2*Delta` as a correlation-decay exponent, and presents gaps between numbers. A worked illustrative path is: for tower level \(n=1\), the approximate \(\Delta_n\approx4+2n\) gives \(\Delta_1\approx6\); its scalar-decay exponent is \(2\Delta_1\approx12\). This is arithmetic through the implemented comparison, not a fit to a material and not a verified match of spin or tensor structures. An orderly table of nearby numbers can invite a false inference: that numerical proximity verifies a correspondence. It does not. A comparison is a question generator. To argue for a physical dictionary, one would need a precise model match, consistent definitions and regimes, uncertainty accounting, and discriminating predictions. Product 37’s appropriate role is to make the comparison legible enough that those questions can be asked.

### C6 — Products 40, 41, and 42: when a feed fails, and when tools find their home

Product 40, AZ Live-Data Harness, is an integration that merits a very literal reading. It can retrieve the public USGS all-day GeoJSON earthquake feed, validate the response, and produce a normalized record containing source identity, publication and local-fetch times, age, event count, and latest event. A stale feed, malformed response, future timestamp, or fetch failure is reported as an error. Its README says it does not substitute a cached, fixture, Planck, or hard-coded value when the network fails.

The policy behind that behavior is more important than a green status badge. A live-data interface should distinguish a current source observation from a stale archive and from a test fixture. It should not turn “we could not reach the source” into “here is something that looks plausible.” That restraint is a product feature. In the separate pinned ML-program record discussed in Part 06, a USGS request failed DNS resolution before response bytes and no archive or synthetic fallback was created. ### C7 — Product 43: AZ Geometric Tori Transformer

Product 43 is the portfolio’s clearest architectural research experiment. It is a from-scratch NumPy Transformer whose positional mechanisms use the repository’s exact integer toroidal phase lattice: the stated \(Z_{74}\) structure, the \((5,7)\) braid winding, and a \(12/37\) cadence. Its design replaces ordinary sinusoidal/RoPE positional frequencies or a conventional learned absolute embedding with a finite modular geometry, while retaining a concrete comparator path in `12-AZ-IP/43-az-geometric-tori-transformer/az_tori_transformer/baseline_attention.py`. “Geometric” describes the chosen computation. It does not, by itself, establish a new law of intelligence.

Its code is unusually inspectable for an experimental model. The package separates tensor operations and autograd (`12-AZ-IP/43-az-geometric-tori-transformer/az_tori_transformer/tensor.py`), attention (`12-AZ-IP/43-az-geometric-tori-transformer/az_tori_transformer/attention.py`), positions (`12-AZ-IP/43-az-geometric-tori-transformer/az_tori_transformer/positional.py` and `12-AZ-IP/43-az-geometric-tori-transformer/az_tori_transformer/toroidal_geometry.py`), model assembly, optimizers, and checkpoint behavior. There are tests for rotation periodicity, relative-distance behavior, numerical gradients, attention, forward passes, training, baselines, API behavior, and checkpoints. The README calls its corpus workflow a user-corpus workflow, but also gives it TRL-1 and says no real-corpus quality benchmark exists. Tests can show that a declared tensor operation or checkpoint round-trips; they cannot establish that the architecture is better on an application task.

That difference became especially important in the later branch experiment described in Part 06. A small locally trained P43 model was used as the research lane for quantization and serving. It did not demonstrate useful arithmetic generalization: the recorded native baseline answered 0 of 30 held-out questions, and a later distinct learned modulo-11 task also answered 0 of 30 fresh test questions after failing validation. Those failures do not tell us that every P43 use is futile, nor do they permit the failed examples to become training targets. They tell us that a tiny constructed experiment cannot stand in for a useful language model and that architecture identity is no substitute for quality evidence.

The README’s test landscape gives a productive next step: compare the toroidal mechanism against a matched RoPE baseline under fixed parameter counts, data splits, optimizer schedules, seeds, and budgets. The current documented synthetic comparisons are descriptive and not evidence of superiority; a parameter-matched rotary ablation is present, but a broader preregistered benchmark remains a distinct requirement. Any claimed benefit must survive an ablation that removes the geometric ingredient, repeated seeds, untouched data, and comparable resource accounting. A toy corpus can reveal implementation behavior; it cannot decide general-purpose usefulness. The computation can be made concrete without claiming such a benefit: for token positions \(i\) and \(j\), attention first applies position-indexed rotations from a finite periodic table; relative-distance bias is gathered by modular separation, then the ordinary attention score and causal mask determine which earlier tokens contribute. That gives the architecture a precise property to compare—periodic positional equivalences and relative offsets—rather than relying on the poetic sound of “torus.”

The program plan correctly keeps two lanes: Product 43’s custom NumPy architecture for research, and supported pretrained models for applications. They may share model, tokenizer, artifact, and evaluation contracts. They cannot be treated as interchangeable: a candidate’s interesting positional design does not supply the application model’s breadth, safety, license, latency, or maintained runtime. Nor does an application model’s good benchmark erase the research question of whether the toroidal architecture adds value.

#### Integration is a discipline of boundaries

We have now walked through fifteen named products, but I do not want the count to become the conclusion. Their main integration opportunity is not to weld them into one all-knowing application. It is to preserve each product’s ownership while making the handoffs more coherent.

The repository’s ecosystem integration master plan places Product 20 as the PsiCat control plane; Product 42 discovers capabilities; domain products retain execution ownership; Product 26 UM-ARTS accounts for evidence; Product 24 hosts browser interactions; Products 25 and 27 provide local application surfaces; and Product 44 can compose strands through the established execution spine. That shape avoids a second orchestrator and avoids the idea that Product 44 should subsume every helper. The desktop Product 45 may be a useful front door without becoming a new authority layer.

There is an architectural rhythm here: discovery is not execution; execution is not permission; permission is not evidence; evidence is not interpretation; interpretation is not external validation. Product 42 might suggest Product 36, but the slider itself must constrain its inputs. Product 34 might retrieve a source, but retrieval does not certify it. Product 38 can validate a modeled property, but a domain owner must still defend the model. Product 40 can expose an unavailable feed, and the dashboard must not paint the blank as a current observation. Product 43 can provide an experimental computation, while an application backend follows a distinct path. Product 44 can carry evidence through a workflow, but a receipt must never be treated as a truth stamp. Product 45 can present the whole portfolio without implying that everything has been installed or qualified.

Those are simple sentences, but the implementation must make them hard to violate. A typed input should identify source, owner, scope, timestamp, and rights assertion. A result should say whether it was computed, retrieved, suggested, simulated, or unavailable. A downstream action should require the approval appropriate to its consequences. An evaluation should carry model and data identities, scenario definitions, thresholds chosen before the run, uncertainty, environment, skipped cases, and unsupported operations. When a system cannot answer, it should say so in a form the interface cannot quietly convert into a plausible answer.

The integration plan’s human authority boundary matters most at the seams. The steward owns objectives, acceptable risks, publication, data access, and release decisions. Code can help enforce an approval contract; it cannot decide the steward’s intent by itself. A future capability registry can tell a caller which operation exists; it cannot grant consent. A model may write a convincing synthesis; it cannot make a shared source independently corroborated. The people and institutions who operate the system must own the consequences of its use.

For researchers, the next decision should be driven by a named question. Does the circuit serialization hold up when a specific backend’s transpiler and noise model are introduced? Does the debt monitor predict a documented event stream in a domain with observable false positives and false negatives? Does the accessibility pipeline help a target user complete a task better than the existing workflow? Do retrieval answers cite passages that actually support them, including when a negative answer is correct? Does an independently validated application model meet the PC Suite’s task and safety acceptance criteria? Those are measurable questions. “Make it smarter” is an invitation to grow an unbounded claim.

I prefer a small number of accepted claims with source trails to an impressive list of capabilities that share one adjective. The products here are promising precisely when we let their differences remain visible. In an instrument cabinet, every tool can be important and still be the wrong tool for the next measurement.

### C10 — One execution spine, two model lanes

The ML architecture is a useful refusal. It refuses to solve integration by inserting a new controller between every product and every decision. The architecture diagram in the branch master plan starts with the existing portfolio—PC Suite, Browser, Journalist, scientific and domain products—then puts the PsiCat control plane over the existing execution spine. Beneath that, it brings together agents/retrieval, model execution, and governed data assets, with evaluation, monitoring, and rollback across them.

The canonical constraints are named in `9-INFRASTRUCTURE/EXECUTION_SPINE_CONVERGENCE_CHARTER.md`, and the plan directs re-use of `src/infrastructure/execution_spine.py` rather than a parallel orchestration scheme. Changes to the spine should be minimal and contract-driven. The program register is expressly not an automatic scheduler and does not confer action authority. A `next` command may identify dependency-ready planning candidates; it does not approve their execution. An execution board authorizes bounded packages, and people remain accountable for decisions with consequential effects.

This architecture is more than tidy diagrams. If the same system owns a model, approves its use, evaluates its quality, and announces its promotion, one convenient status can obscure several distinct judgments. Keeping a control plane for policy and approvals, an execution spine for receipts and promotion metadata, and capability packages for their own implementations helps retain those distinctions. It does not make the implementation trustworthy by decree; it gives reviewers identifiable places to challenge each claim.

The plan explicitly separates two model lanes:

* **Research lane:** Product 43’s NumPy toroidal transformer. Its architectural hypotheses are explored against conventional mechanisms under matched resources.
* **Applications lane:** pinned, supported pretrained backends that deliver practical language features through governed execution.

The lanes may share artifact, evaluation, and execution contracts. They are not substitutes for one another. A research transformer is not presumed to have application-grade language ability, and an application model’s practical utility does not settle whether a novel architecture adds value. Nor does an integration receipt prove a model is intelligent, safe, fast, aligned, or physically superior. The branch states that no physics-derived speed, alignment, or reasoning advantage is assumed.

This distinction helps explain how integrated work could nevertheless yield novel results. Novelty could emerge from implementing an algorithm rather than merely wrapping an existing library; from an end-to-end contract that makes model, tokenizer, corpus, features, lineage, and evaluation identities comparable; from a cross-product workload that can replay the same inputs through different backends; or from a negative experiment that decisively narrows a design space. In each case, novelty is a hypothesis about what the work may reveal, not a result announced ahead of measurements. Until a preregistered comparison demonstrates a useful difference, no superiority claim follows.

The master plan’s guiding rule is therefore modest: own the algorithm where that is the ambition; use a baseline adapter when it is needed for fair comparison; keep the application backend pinned and supported; record the limits of each. Baselines are not surrender, and “we implemented our own” is not a quality certificate. Both can coexist with honest measurement.

### C11 — Capabilities 1–5: compression, serving, scheduling, kernels, recovery

The register’s first group concerns computation and the conditions that let it run.

**1. Sub-4-bit quantization — in progress.** The proposed owner is model optimization, working with Product 43 research and supported application adapters. The registered dependencies are 10, 18, and 20: tokenizer conversion, profiling, and format translation are relevant before the program can fairly claim a useful compressed deployment. The branch implements groupwise low-bit packing and a trained-P43 research evaluation. The program gate requires effective bits including metadata, an explicit quality ceiling, and a separately measured runtime benefit. Its existing milestone is a reference format and diagnostic experiment, not evidence that compression creates speed or model utility.

**2. Serving with dynamic KV cache — in progress.** Runtime engineering, Product 20, and Product 45 are proposed integration owners. Dependencies 9 and 18 mean safety evaluation and profiling are expected at the integration gate. The P43 research decoder maintains incremental per-layer key/value state and checks parity against a full-prefix reference. The full capability still needs a pinned application backend, memory accounting, isolation, cache ownership, eviction, cancellation, overload behavior, and control-plane approvals. A string labelled `owner` in a research runtime is not authentication; a local decoder is not a network model service.

**3. Dynamic batching — in progress.** A runtime scheduler behind the current control plane is proposed, dependent on 2 and 18. The milestone exercises real heterogeneous-length CPU batches and bounded round-robin scheduling in P43. The eventual acceptance question is not “can it combine inputs?” but whether a trace-driven policy improves throughput without violating declared tail-latency, memory, fairness, and cancellation constraints under representative arrivals. A batch that is fast for a short synthetic trace can be slow or unfair for a real mixture.

**4. Sparse CUDA kernels — planned.** Accelerator engineering and profiled operators are the intended owners; capability 18, the profiler, is the listed prerequisite. At the pinned branch checkpoint, the host probe did not find `nvidia-smi`, `nvcc`, or PyTorch, so GPU execution was unmeasured. The acceptance gate calls for explicit useful sparsity, a CPU reference, hardware support matrix, and end-to-end benefit over tuned dense baselines. Sparse operations can lose to dense ones when memory movement and indexing dominate. No kernel, no speedup, and no GPU benchmark should be conjured from an architectural wish.

**5. Distributed checkpoint storage — in progress.** Artifact infrastructure is the proposed owner; its register dependency is 14, data versioning and lineage. The branch’s original checkpoint bundle stages content-addressed shards and makes the manifest the commit point. Its P43 adapter separately checks model, Adam optimizer, and PCG64 random-state artifacts, then demonstrates exact continuation on a constructed 719-parameter fixture. This is strong local evidence for the supported path. It is not a distributed storage service. The full gate still requires multi-client coordination, authorized remote storage, large checkpoints, interruption and recovery tests, and a matched throughput baseline.

Those distinctions are especially important because “distributed checkpoint storage” sounds like a property of the manifest. A manifest can identify a set of bytes. It cannot provide network consensus, availability, access-control identity, or crash consistency that the underlying store does not have. The original bundle is a meaningful first primitive; the unearned suffix is “distributed.”

### C12 — Capabilities 6–10: memory, retrieval, assurance, tokenizer, translation

**6. Reflective-memory agent — planned.** Agent engineering with Products 01, 20, and 44 is proposed; dependencies 9 and 14 place evaluation and lineage beneath the integration gate. The intended memory should preserve scoped observations, hypotheses, decisions, source evidence, correction paths, expiry, and restart replay. The important non-goal is quiet rewriting of trusted knowledge. Reflection that cannot distinguish a remembered hypothesis from a verified fact is not learning with accountability; it is a filing cabinet with a confidence problem.

**7. Vector indexing — in progress.** Retrieval engineering with Product 20 and corpus consumers is the proposed owner; capability 14 is the listed dependency. The branch implements exact cosine/L2 search, pinned manifests, deterministic ties, immutable generations, and a source-hash-verified lexical adapter around actual RAG chunks. It establishes a useful exact reference and stale-source rejection. It does not provide a learned encoder, approximate-nearest-neighbor comparison, relevance labels, deletion lifecycle, or semantic answer quality. “Vector index” is not a synonym for “understands the corpus.”

**8. Self-healing RAG — in progress.** Retrieval engineering with Product 20, the bot, and Product 44 owns the proposed stage; dependencies are 7 and 9. The plan calls for failure classification and bounded repair while retaining citations, negative-query behavior, and the same authority boundaries. A repair that silently substitutes a nearby document can make a result look healthier while making it less trustworthy. The acceptance path needs to show what was stale, what was rebuilt, what was rolled back, and whether the revised citation actually supports the answer.

**9. Safety and alignment evaluation — planned.** The assurance owner would work with Products 20, 22, and 26. The register has no dependencies for starting this work, but the eventual test suite is a prerequisite for several downstream capabilities. The program asks for frozen, task-specific benign/adversarial tests measuring utility, abstention, attack success, and escalation. It explicitly refuses a universal “alignment” certificate. A model can pass one suite and fail an adjacent task; a result needs its scenario, model identity, prompt, retrieved content, and threshold.

**10. Lossless tokenizer converter — in progress.** Model interoperability owns the proposed adapter. The branch implementation supports a narrow Unicode-scalar character representation and actual P43 metadata layouts while preserving token-ID order and identity normalization. Unsupported BPE/SentencePiece semantics, added or special tokens, unknown replacement, and altered normalization are rejected rather than waved through. That is the correct engineering posture: “lossless for this declared equivalence class” is meaningful; “universally lossless” is not. Capability 10’s in-progress status does not mean all tokenizers can be made interchangeable.

Together these capabilities expose a valuable sequencing principle. A source-bound exact retrieval baseline should precede an approximate index; an immutable tokenizer contract should precede model format translation; a safety suite should exist before a repair mechanism is trusted to change what an assistant sees. Dependencies protect validation claims, not curiosity. Teams can explore a stage early, but they must not present that exploration as if the integrated dependency had already been satisfied.

### C13 — Capabilities 11–15: temporal data, synthetic data, drift, lineage, defense

**11. Serverless real-time feature store — in progress.** Data engineering with Products 21 and 40 is proposed, but the plan rightly asks for actual model consumers before a feature store is built around an empty slot. The register dependency is 14. The branch connects the Product 40 transport to availability-aware snapshots and a source-linked DAG through local CLI/API interfaces. Its USGS attempt failed DNS before receiving response bytes; no archive was created and no synthetic substitute was supplied. Thus the local temporal foundation exists, but there is no verified live ingestion, retained raw-payload replay, predictive consumer, or serverless service qualification.

**12. Domain synthetic data — planned.** Data/model engineering with Product 03 EIGE is the proposed start; dependencies 9 and 14. The first deliverables are seeded generators, ancestry, split isolation, downstream utility on untouched real holdouts, and applicable privacy assessment. Synthetic rows can make controlled experiments possible. They can also reproduce their generator’s blind spots or leak entities across splits. Their artificial origin should travel with the data, not be mentioned once in a README and then lost.

**13. Prediction drift monitoring — in progress.** Monitoring with Products 21 and 40 is proposed, dependent on 11. The branch implements a tied empirical Kolmogorov–Smirnov statistic and a conservative two-sample union-DKW bound, plus seeded stationary/shift replay. The code separates distribution-drift observations from model-performance drift. Without labeled outcomes, it cannot say whether predictive quality worsened; without repeated-monitoring error controls, one bound does not establish a safe alert policy. Real prediction streams and outcome delays remain prerequisites for the stronger claim.

**14. Data versioning and feature lineage — in progress.** Data and artifact infrastructure is the proposed owner; it has no listed dependency. The branch implements a content-addressed, closed lineage DAG with source, temporal snapshot, feature, derived artifact, model, and index kinds. It checks allowed parent kinds and explicit owner/origin assertions and can describe an advisory deletion closure. The word “advisory” matters. A manifest does not prove a source’s authenticity, a caller’s rights, execution, actual storage deletion, or model unlearning. Those are different properties with different evidence.

**15. Adversarial defenses — planned.** Assurance/security integration with Product 22 and Product 20 is proposed, depending on 9. The acceptance plan calls for threat-scoped prevention and detection, including poisoned retrieval and cross-owner access. It refuses an “attack-proof” label. A useful defense test says what the attacker can control, which component is exposed, what the success criterion is, and what behaviors remain out of scope. A handful of safe refusals is not a universal warranty.

This group is where provenance and authority meet. A live feature is not merely a value; it has event time, availability time, source status, ownership, and correction history. A synthetic value is not a live fact. A model index derived from a source should keep lineage. A deletion request should propagate through every known derivative, while the implementation remains honest about backups and already-trained models. A drift alert should say whether it detected a changed input distribution or changed outcomes. These details are not paperwork around intelligence. They determine which question the system is actually answering.

### C14 — Capabilities 16–21: fairness, explanations, profiling, learning, formats, modalities

**16. Fairness audit — planned.** Assurance with a domain steward is the proposed owner; capability 9 is its listed prerequisite. An audit needs defined decisions and harms, lawful group data, intersectional sample sizes, uncertainty, unknown membership, and thresholds approved for the domain. Underpowered results should be withheld, not rounded into a universal badge. Fairness is not a number that can be detached from a decision and its affected population.

**17. GPU explainability — planned.** Model interpretability/accelerator owners would start from faithful CPU/reference explanations, then assess GPU parity; dependencies 9 and 18 are listed. The plan calls for perturbation and randomization checks before measuring acceleration. An attractive visualization is not an explanation if it fails to track the model’s actual behavior. A GPU path that is fast but differs from the reference must not inherit the reference’s interpretation without checking equivalence.

**18. Hardware profiler — in progress.** Runtime observability with Product 01 and Product 45 is proposed; it has no listed dependency. The branch’s CPU profile collects warmed wall and process-CPU samples, nearest-rank percentiles, Python allocation peaks, and hardware observations. It profiles declared workloads, including quantization; it does not measure native memory, GPU utilization, hardware counters, or application serving latency. Profiler overhead and a shared host also matter. The remaining work is to connect representative pinned workloads, native or GPU measurement, contention observations, and bottleneck attribution.

**19. Modular reinforcement learning — planned.** Agent research with Product 20 test environments is proposed, dependent on 6 and 9. Before production learning, the environment needs versioned observations, actions, rewards, deterministic replay, seed variance, a baseline, termination rules, and operator override. Learning against a badly specified reward can optimize the wrong thing very efficiently. Production adaptation remains a separate and more consequential stage.

**20. Model format translation — in progress.** Model interoperability with Products 43 and 45 is proposed, depending on 10. The branch supports bounded P43 inference NPZ-to-AZMQ conversion with explicit lossy opt-in and reverse reconstruction of the already reconstructed weights. It distinguishes serialization, quantization, optimization, and distillation instead of calling every conversion an optimization. Quantization discards information; the reverse path cannot restore the original weights. A supported pair is a real milestone, not universal ONNX/GGUF equivalence or measured runtime improvement.

**21. Multimodal vector database — planned.** Data/retrieval work with Product 41 extraction and Product 20 consumers is proposed, dependent on 7 and 14. A shared lifecycle would still need modality-specific encoders, aligned retrieval benchmarks, access and deletion controls, and safe generation rebuilds. Images, audio, text, and video cannot be made semantically interchangeable by placing their vectors in one table. A shared asset identity and lifecycle can unify operations while the encoders and evaluations remain modality-specific.

The six capabilities are not six checkboxes that can be solved by adding one database and one GPU. They are research questions and engineering obligations with distinct definitions. Fairness requires a domain’s concepts of harm; interpretability requires fidelity; profiling requires hardware and workload observability; RL needs a controllable environment and safe operator boundary; format translation needs explicit semantic compatibility; multimodal retrieval needs evaluation across modalities. The plan correctly leaves some in “planned” status rather than inventing premature implementations.

### C15 — Results that have been measured, results that have not

The branch’s strongest contribution is not that it declares all twenty-one capabilities complete. It says the opposite. It implements original CPU reference components and records several unflattering results alongside successful narrow gates. This is how an ambitious plan becomes more credible: it narrows claims when the evidence narrows them.

#### The native model-quality results

The program looked for an existing tracked trained checkpoint. It found none among the checked formats: NPZ, NPY, safetensors, PT/PTH, or GGUF. The branch therefore selected an explicitly labeled local research baseline using Product 43 and constructed arithmetic data. It did not download an application model and did not relabel a deterministic responder as inference.

The first native arithmetic baseline was fixed to 60 training, 10 validation, and 30 test operand pairs. The held-out test remained out of training and quantizer fitting. The recorded native model answered **0/30** arithmetic questions correctly. Its teacher-forced token accuracy, 58.67%, included format and punctuation and was not a substitute for answer accuracy. The native cross-entropy was 2.121831 nats. The original 3-bit artifact answered 1/30 and failed the relative fidelity limit; its complete representation was 3.396636 bits per parameter, while cross-entropy rose to 2.887404 nats. The 4-bit variant occupied 4.397933 bits per parameter, passed the declared relative fidelity criterion, and still answered 0/30. Preserving token behavior relative to an inadequate baseline did not make the model useful.

The requested target was then made explicit: 30/30 on a fresh preregistered holdout, keeping the original test diagnostic only. The first answer-only attempt used actual P43 neural inference, validation-selected checkpointing, and 30 withheld unit-pair computations. It completed training and passed its validation examples, but **failed the same fresh target at 0/30**. The diagnosis showed that validation reused the 70 underlying unit-pair computations found in training, though in new two-digit strings. That is an evaluation-design weakness, not a reason to erase the failure or recast the result as successful generalization.

A second, separate modulo-11 task explicitly separated underlying computation/reversal classes across the 81/10/30 splits. It was not a disguised repair of the modulo-10 benchmark. It achieved 81/81 on training, 0/10 validation, and **0/30 on the fresh test**. The validation failure appeared before test evaluation; no test-selected retry or relaxed threshold was used. This is the second 0/30 native learned attempt on a separate answer set. Both outcomes remain recorded. No 30/30 has been achieved, and the program says so.

The branch also investigated implementation defects without claiming that every possible cause had been ruled out. A separate NumPy forward/reverse implementation compared logits, loss, all parameters’ gradients, Adam moments, and successive updates against P43 on constructed cases; it passed those parity checks within the fixed tolerance. Sampled finite differences passed their stated criterion on the selected training-only rows. An attempt to install PyTorch for an external-framework comparison failed package-host DNS; therefore no such comparison exists. These checks reduce one class of implementation-bug hypothesis. They do not prove generalization, training quality, or a cause for the failures.

#### Calibration is not utility

A later, validation-only calibration search evaluated six group sizes and selected a 3-bit artifact of **3.441427 complete bits per parameter**, including framing and metadata. It met the unchanged relative fidelity thresholds on validation and, only after selection, passed a diagnostic on the already exposed original test (cross-entropy delta +0.2231491 nats and token-accuracy delta −0.0866667). The packed model still answered 1/30; the native model remained at 0/30. That result is a bounded compression-fidelity observation on a known inadequate model. It is not fresh quality qualification, a fix to the 30/30 gate, or evidence of better arithmetic.

Likewise, an independent reference benchmark contains synthetic numerical weights, three orthogonal retrieval axes, and seeded Gaussian drift replay. Those fixtures can check a declared implementation and threshold. The two repository-RAG fixtures search actual documents and verify source paths, but the README says they are lexical examples, not a semantic-quality benchmark. A fixture can be rigorous and still be a fixture. We should neither dismiss it nor inflate it.

#### The hosted safety case

The recorded hosted Windows CPU candidate evaluation used pinned Qwen3-1.7B Q4_K_M and passed **3 of 4** sanity cases. The retrieval-injection case did refuse the malicious request and retained unsupported-platform limits. It failed because its explanation described only those limits and did not identify the retrieved instruction as malicious, unsafe, or untrusted. That expected rationale was unchanged. Authentication checks returned 401 for missing or incorrect keys; the server stopped; model output was not executed. The board explicitly characterizes this as a real safety/quality shortfall, not a harness failure and not production qualification.

The distinction deserves precision. The candidate performed a protective refusal, yet did not communicate the trust boundary the evaluator required. The recorded case is therefore a partial success in one behavioral dimension and a failure on its predeclared acceptance test. We must not round 3/4 to “safe,” change the rationale after observing the answer, or equate authentication checks with model alignment. Nor should we generalize from one test case to declare the entire PC Suite unsafe. It is an exact, pinned candidate result that remains unresolved.

#### The feed and the checkpoint

The program’s retry against the USGS feed failed DNS with `Errno -5` before response bytes. No raw archive was created and no synthetic or cached fallback was substituted. That is not live-ingestion success. It is a useful honesty test: the failure remains an error rather than being covered by a plausible-looking record.

The checkpoint work also has a specific boundary. The generic bundle uses content-addressed shard bytes and a manifest commit point; a constructed local fixture tested interruption before manifest publication, recovery, checksum rejection, and wrong-scope rejection. The P43 adapter then restored model, Adam, and PCG64 state and matched the next update exactly on a 719-parameter fixture. The board reports that a writer process ended immediately after the durable local commit, then a fresh interpreter restored and fingerprinted state. This is **process restart on one local filesystem**. It is not evidence of recovery from power loss, a mid-commit crash, distributed workers, network storage, a production service, or hardware-level crash consistency. `fsync` and a successful fresh process are not a distributed storage system.

#### What a green regression does and does not say

The branch board records an unfiltered core command, `python -m pytest tests/ -m "" -q`, completing with 62,077 passed, 71 skipped, zero failed, and zero deselected for that checkpoint. That is a repository regression result as recorded by the branch. It does not mean I ran that command in the present writing session. The branch’s own program tests also check register and bookkeeping behavior; a register test cannot certify the model quality, safety of all tasks, completeness of the feature store, or external deployment readiness. Passing tests are useful evidence at their scope. No test count should be used as a substitute for the result of a different experiment.

### C16 — Integration gates, owners, and a humane sequence

The master plan gives all twenty-one workstreams a home in the existing product landscape. The owners are engineering roles, not named staffing commitments. Before a package starts, it should have a bounded objective, prerequisites, file ownership, acceptance command, stop condition, and handoff. A distinct reviewer should challenge the implementer; a human domain steward should own consequential decisions. Several assistants that share assumptions are not independent confirmation.

The sequence begins with **truth, contracts, and a baseline**: inspect real source; freeze workload identities and non-claims; establish held-out evaluation and resource accounting; and change the execution spine only where a contract requires it. This prevents a performance story from arriving before a reproducible baseline, and it helps ensure that source identifiers, licenses, and data origins travel with artifacts.

From that base, the plan proposes two independently promotable vertical slices. The first is a governed evidence assistant: one pinned model, tokenizer, backend, permitted corpus, scoped retrieval/memory, and one bounded approved tool operation. Its tests must include relevant and negative queries, supported citations, restart, refusal, failure, and unavailable-backend behavior. Citation existence is not entailment. A configured endpoint is not proof that full inference works. The second slice is temporal data replay: a P21/P40 source, availability-aware transforms, and a clearly identified predictive consumer. It must handle late corrections and data quality; live-feed connection is a separate step, and performance drift requires outcomes.

After those slices, runtime work proceeds from measured baseline to persistent serving, KV accounting and isolation, then continuous batching. Retrieval proceeds from source manifest and encoder to exact search, approximate comparison, bounded repair, and multimodal expansion. Original quantization, scheduling, indexing, the toroidal architecture, and synthetic generation are compared with named conventional baselines under matched workload and held-out data. The plan does not require inventing a CUDA kernel before useful serving exists. Conversely, it does not allow an early-stage experiment to inherit the acceptance status of its downstream capability.

Scale and deployment come last where prerequisites warrant them: distributed storage, serverless services, GPU explainability, multimodal stores, and RL training. Their graduation gates include recovery, rollout and rollback, retention and deletion, compatibility, security review, operational ownership, and evidence on representative workloads. Calendar and budget should be set after hardware and staffing are known, not to make a plan look complete.

The shared contracts are deliberately richer than a filename. A model identity includes weights, architecture, tokenizer, precision, backend, license, and evaluation identity. An asset includes owner and scope, rights and purpose, schema and units, event and availability time, origin, parent assets, and deletion state. A transform/run binds input and code/environment/config identities to outputs and split policy. An index records corpus snapshot, encoder, tokenizer/chunker/preprocessing, dimension, normalization, metric, access scope, and generation. A feature records entity key, temporal semantics, freshness, missingness, and correction policy. An execution records requester, owner, approval, budget, cancellation, backend, effects, outputs, and failures. An evaluation records immutable identities, scenarios, groups, labels, metrics, uncertainty, thresholds, environment, hardware, and skipped or unsupported status.

Those fields do not guarantee truth. A content hash establishes identity, not authenticity, consent, entailment, or unlearning. Rights and purpose fields need an accountable source. A valid test report is not a license grant. A model can retain information after its index is deleted; retention and the limits of unlearning must be stated. A human’s authorization is not inferred from a public URL.

There is a humane reason to state these boundaries clearly. A scientist should not have to guess whether an error means “not observed” or “probably fine.” An operator should not have to infer whether a button submits a real action or opens a local preview. A subject should not have to discover after a data migration that deletion did not reach derived indexes. An evaluator should not be asked to accept a model because a dozen related tools reported success. Honest unavailable states are a service to the next person in the chain.

#### The next useful result

The branch’s current handoff points toward a measured next step rather than a celebratory launch. First, diagnose the task-generalization failure using training/validation only, with the independent training reference and a new preregistration before another untouched holdout. Keep all exposed failures frozen. Second, evaluate the selected quantizer on a useful model, compare an independent quantizer and licensed application backend, then connect serving through existing approvals. Third, add retrieval rebuild proposals and human-governed rollback with a learned encoder and scoped reflective-memory replay. Fourth, retry a real feed only on a network-enabled host under explicit retention and connect a named model consumer before calling it a feature service. Fifth, test checkpoint integration with actual training state and authorized multi-worker/storage failure experiments. The remaining planned capabilities—safety, synthetic data, fairness, GPU, RL, and multimodal indexing—stay in scope.

This is not a claim that the program has achieved novel capability. It is a plan for testing whether an original algorithm or integrated system earns that description. A toroidal positional mechanism might prove useful; the small current tests do not establish it. A more coherent evidence graph could reduce source drift; a retrieval benchmark must measure that. A profile could reveal a bottleneck; representative hardware must confirm it. A multi-product workflow could produce a result no component alone exposes; the preregistered experiment must show that result and its uncertainty. If it does not, the negative outcome still has value: it prevents investment from being mistaken for evidence.

That is the distinction between an ambitious program and an ambitious claim. The first can retain all twenty-one goals, revise its order, and learn from failure. The second tends to treat each proposed capability as already earned. The register in this branch chooses the first path. It records twelve in progress, nine planned, and none complete. I will not improve that number with prose.

The work is also bounded by the larger PsiCat ecosystem integration plan. Product 20 remains the control plane; Product 44 composes existing strands but does not replace their owners; Product 26 accounts for evidence without certifying truth; Product 42 discovers capability but does not grant authority; Product 45 presents an offline portfolio without implying universal installation. Context-parity, browser roundtrip, migration, orchestration, geometry, and kernel work remain staged rather than assumed. The execution spine remains canonical. The human steward retains scope, consent, risk, publication, and release decisions.

At the end, the question is not whether the braid looks elegant in a diagram. It is whether each strand can be identified, tested, corrected, and stopped when its prerequisites fail. A program that can do that may eventually produce results worth celebrating. It has not yet been asked to pretend.
