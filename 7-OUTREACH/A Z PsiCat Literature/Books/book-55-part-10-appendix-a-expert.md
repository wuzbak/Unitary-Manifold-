## A.2 Methodology and the Confidence-Tiering Standard

This book inherits and formalizes the three-tier evidentiary standard introduced in the AxiomZero Journalist line and used consistently across the six source investigations this book synthesizes. The standard exists because a book of this scope will inevitably combine claims of very different evidentiary weight, and collapsing that difference — treating a grand jury indictment and an anonymously sourced allegation as equally solid — is itself a form of the narrative distortion this book exists to expose.

**VERIFIED.** The claim is independently confirmed by primary documentary evidence this book's authors directly examined or that is a matter of adjudicated public record: a court judgment, a regulatory consent order, a filed corporate disclosure, a leaked document set authenticated by a recognized investigative consortium (such as ICIJ), or a government's own published audit or review finding.

**DOCUMENTED.** The claim is established in the cited public record — investigative reporting from outlets with established editorial and fact-checking standards, government reports (GAO, CRS, Inspector General), NGO or academic research with disclosed methodology — but has not been independently re-verified by this book's authors against a primary source.

**UNVERIFIED / ALLEGED.** The claim is reported in the public record but remains contested, single-sourced, denied by a named party, or awaiting judicial or investigative resolution. This book includes such claims only where the fact of the allegation itself is independently documented (that is, this book is not asserting the allegation is true, only that it has been made, by whom, and on what asserted basis), and always marks them as such rather than allowing tier-blending by omission.

A fourth category, used sparingly and always explicitly: **DISPUTED.** The underlying fact pattern is agreed, but its legal, causal, or moral significance is contested by credible parties on more than one side — for example, the ruling of suicide in a custodial death that outside forensic reviewers have publicly questioned on technical grounds. This book states the dispute and its basis rather than adjudicating it, because doing so honestly is not the same as "both-sidesing" a question of whether a documented wrong occurred.

## A.3 Red-Team Self-Critique and Limitations Register

Every source investigation synthesized into this book carried its own honest-gaps section. This book inherits that discipline rather than smoothing it away, because a synthesis that erased its sources' self-doubt would be less rigorous than the sources themselves.

**On scope.** This book is a synthesis of existing, previously published investigative work plus consolidation and cross-referencing by its authors. It is not a work of original investigative reporting with new human sources, new document acquisition, or new on-the-record interviews. Its evidentiary floor is therefore the evidentiary floor of its six source works plus whatever this book's own cross-referencing has added or corrected.

**On the Unit 8200 material specifically.** This book's treatment of Unit 8200's commercial surveillance pipeline (Part One, Part Two) documents a structural personnel and acquisition pattern. It does not, and should not be read to, establish an ongoing state-directed intelligence operation inside the named commercial entities; no public record located by this book's source investigations supports that stronger claim, and the authors have deliberately declined to imply it.

**On the Carbyne/Barak/Maxwell material specifically.** As stated plainly in Part Eight, Chapter 25, the documented link ends at verified investment and verified event attendance. Any reader encountering a stronger claim about this specific link elsewhere should treat this book's stated boundary as the operative one for material appearing under the AxiomZero/PsiCat name.

**On causal claims generally.** This book is deliberately conservative about asserting causation where only correlation and documented proximity are available — particularly in the lobbying, PAC, and political-influence material in Part Six, where proving a specific quid pro quo is extremely difficult in any single documented instance, and this book does not claim to have done so.

**On completeness.** The offshore shell-company architecture described in A.4 and A.5 of this book's companion Empirical Appendix is explicitly partial. The 2008 Epstein Non-Prosecution Agreement's full scope, the complete technical record of Azure workloads referenced in Part Two, and the full extent of several entities' subsidiary structures remain non-public as of this book's writing. Where this book says "the full extent has not been publicly disclosed," that sentence is a methodological finding, not a rhetorical hedge — it marks exactly where further investigative work, by this project or others, would need to begin.

**On update currency.** Because several of the matters discussed in this book are under active investigation, litigation, or congressional inquiry, no version of this book can be considered a final account. New Epstein Files releases, DOJ actions, GAO audit results, or corporate disclosures may update specific findings after publication. Readers should treat the confidence tiers assigned here as current to this book's writing date, not as permanently fixed.

## A.4 Glossary for the Non-Specialist Reader

**Beneficial ownership** — the real, natural person who ultimately owns or controls an asset or entity, as distinct from the nominal or legal owner of record (which may be a shell company, trust, or nominee).

**Non-Prosecution Agreement (NPA)** — a formal agreement in which a prosecutor agrees not to pursue charges against a party, typically in exchange for cooperation, restitution, or other conditions; distinct from a plea agreement, which involves a formal guilty plea and court sentencing.

**Deferred Prosecution Agreement (DPA)** — similar to an NPA but filed with a court and subject to judicial oversight, commonly used in corporate criminal matters including several of the bank settlements discussed in Part Three.

**Revolving door** — the recurring movement of individuals between government or military positions and the private-sector industries those positions regulate, contract with, or oversee, discussed throughout this book as a structural mechanism rather than an individual ethical failing.

**Disclaimer of opinion (audit)** — the most adverse finding an external auditor can issue, indicating the auditor could not obtain sufficient reliable evidence to form any opinion on the accuracy of the audited financial statements — the Pentagon's recurring audit outcome discussed in Part Seven.

**Registered agent** — a firm, typically located in a low-disclosure jurisdiction, that provides corporate formation and nominal registration services, forming the supply side of the offshore shell-company industry discussed in this book's Empirical Appendix.

**Five-layer offshore architecture** — the recurring structural pattern, documented across this book's financial chapters, in which a beneficial owner is separated from an asset by a domestic law firm, a foreign law firm, a registered agent, a trust, and a holding company, each layer adding a jurisdiction an investigator must separately penetrate.

**Guilt by association** — the fallacious inference that an individual is culpable for wrongdoing because of a shared category membership (employer, nationality, religion, alma mater) with a wrongdoer, rather than because of their own documented conduct; this book's explicit rejection of this reasoning is stated in Part Eight, Chapter 24.

**Too big to jail** — the informal description of a regulatory posture in which an institution is deemed too systemically important to prosecute criminally (as opposed to civilly settle with), out of concern that a criminal conviction could trigger a license revocation or market panic with broader economic consequences; discussed in Part Three in connection with HSBC.
