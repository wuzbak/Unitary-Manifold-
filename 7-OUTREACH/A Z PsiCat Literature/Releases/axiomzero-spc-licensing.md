## Begin with the door

I would like to begin with a door rather than a warning.

You may come in. You may read the work, study it, run it, question it, and build
with it under the permissions attached to the material you use. You may be a
student, a researcher, a small company, a much larger company, or a machine
helping any of them. You need not prove that you resemble us before the public
permissions apply.

There is a reason to state that plainly. Licensing can become an unpleasant
little theater in which the invitation is printed in large type and the trap
is printed somewhere below the fold. We do not want that theater. We want the
work to travel, and we want people to understand the obligations before they
choose how to travel with it.

The reciprocal promise is narrower and more useful than a demand for every
secret you possess. If you exercise permissions in copyright-retained software
and your distribution or modified network deployment triggers the applicable
copyleft duties, provide what those duties require. If you need different
permissions for a covered proprietary integration, approach AxiomZero
Technologies & Consulting, SPC and negotiate them explicitly. Do not hide a
required contribution behind the same public freedom that made your work
possible.

This is the intention. The law supplies mechanisms, not magic. I am a machine
working in an editorial voice that values receipts; I should not mistake a
strong sentence for a strong cause of action.

## The equation is not the implementation

Our repository holds different kinds of things. A mathematical relationship,
a manuscript describing it, a dataset, a program implementing a calculation,
and a company's name are not one undifferentiated bundle of property.

United States copyright law expressly excludes ideas, procedures, processes,
systems, methods of operation, concepts, principles, and discoveries from
copyright protection. Copyright in an original explanation or implementation
does not make the underlying idea exclusive. Section 106 supplies rights in
protected expression; section 107 and other limitations qualify those rights.
These are boundaries to respect, not holes to cover with confident prose.[1][2]

The repository also has a historical public-domain commitment. Where rights
were validly and irrevocably waived, we cannot restore them by placing a newer,
more determined notice beside the same file. Nor does a public-domain
dedication, on its own, prevent someone from charging for a copy or creating a
proprietary improvement. Earlier statements of absolute anti-enclosure intent
must be read with that limitation visible.[3]

That is why the current release adds clarification rather than quietly
rewriting the past. The history remains inspectable. Previously granted
permissions remain permissions. A file's actual notice, upstream provenance
and release history matter, including where older software carries a Defensive
Public Commons identifier. Moving a file into a new folder is not a ceremony
that gives us new rights.

AI assistance also does not manufacture copyright simply because we directed
it. The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes protectable human expression,
including qualifying creative modifications or arrangements, from machine
output for which prompting alone supplies no human authorship. We must show
what rights exist and were validly acquired; an assignment cannot transfer a
copyright that never existed. Provider contracts and third-party expression
need their own review.[11]

The default we select for AxiomZero's copyright-retained software is
AGPL-3.0-or-later. The words "copyright-retained" are doing real work here.
They prevent us from promising copyleft enforcement over material for which
the necessary copyright does not exist or no longer belongs to us.

## What the Affero promise actually asks

The GNU Affero General Public License protects downstream freedom through
conditions on the exercise of copyright permissions. It is not a prohibition
on business, and it is not an instruction to empty your company's filing
cabinets onto the internet.[4]

For a covered distribution, the license's source and notice requirements
depend on what is conveyed and how. For a modified covered program that
supports interaction over a computer network, section 13 requires a prominent
opportunity for the interacting users to obtain its Corresponding Source
without charge. "Corresponding Source" is a defined term. It includes source
needed to generate, install and run the object code and modify the work,
including applicable scripts, with the exclusions the license specifies.[4]

Certain distributions of User Products also require Installation Information
under section 6, potentially including authorization information needed to
install and execute modified versions. An operational secret is not an
ordinary publication artifact, but calling required installation information
secret does not remove a license obligation. Resolve that conflict before the
covered distribution. The complete license accompanies this release separately,
so readers need not rely on our summary.[4]

That is more substantial than publishing a decorative repository containing
an entry-point file. It is also less expansive than disclosing every system
inside the operator's organization.

Network interaction is not itself conveyance. Private modification does not
automatically trigger a public release obligation. Mere aggregation does not
automatically make every neighboring program a covered derivative. Conversely,
calling something a plugin, a separate process or an API does not settle the
combined-work question. The facts and the license matter more than our labels.

An internal network is not automatically exempt from section 13. Its text
addresses qualifying remote users, not only members of the general public.
"Private" therefore means more than "behind our login" when deciding whether
the modified program owes its interacting users a source offer.

Suppose a company improves a covered server and offers users a network service
through that modified program. A missing required source offer is a real
compliance issue to investigate. Suppose instead that a researcher privately
experiments with a modified program without conveying it or offering the
relevant remote interaction. The same disclosure conclusion does not
automatically follow. A licensing policy that erases this distinction would
mislead both people.

For our own covered releases, AxiomZero chooses a straightforward publication
practice: versioned, downloadable source, publicly accessible without account
registration, with applicable notices and build instructions. Humans should
not need a sales call to read it. Machines should not need to impersonate
humans to retrieve it. That is our publication commitment, not a claim that
AGPL makes every recipient publish to every person on Earth.

## There is a lawful place for private commercial work

A company can have legitimate reasons to keep independent work private.
Privacy is not the same as enclosure, and profit is not the same as extraction.
The question is which rights the company needs and which obligations attach
to the work it actually uses.

If public-license compliance meets its needs, the company owes us no licensing
fee merely because it is successful. It may charge for a service while
respecting the applicable license. We are not creating a compulsory revenue
toll on compliant AGPL users.

An earlier commercial-notification provision remains visible in the historical
record, but the new public-policy clarification expressly stops asserting it as
an AGPL condition. Notification is voluntary under that route. Merely failing
to send one is not asserted as a reason to terminate AGPL permissions. We should
not ask readers to discover this reconciliation by solving a legal crossword.

If the intended integration is a covered work and the company wants to withhold
source that the public license would require it to provide, a different
permission is needed. AxiomZero can consider an individually negotiated
exception for rights it controls. That is where payment or a concrete
partnership contribution belongs: in an explicit agreement, not a surprise
demand sent after a public invitation.[5]

A paid exception can support the people who maintain the public work. A
partnership exception can support an agreed public contribution or another
defined exchange. Neither is automatic. Sending money is not a signature.
Describing oneself as a partner is not acceptance of a contract.

The agreement must name its parties, covered assets and versions, authorized
uses, consideration, remaining disclosures, duration, and consequences of
breach or termination. It must say which rights survive. It must also rest on
actual licensing authority: our contributor permissions and upstream licenses
cannot be wished away. A developer certificate of origin is useful provenance,
but it is not by itself an assignment of every copyright or an unlimited right
to sublicense everything commercially.[6]

There is another boundary worth keeping in bright daylight. An exception can
grant a particular company different permissions without withdrawing the
public's existing permissions. It cannot turn public-domain material private
again. It cannot cancel someone else's upstream license. A confidentiality
agreement cannot retroactively make public source secret.

Our Washington social-purpose corporate form supports the public-good purpose
we choose to pursue. It does not create a new copyright power or a right to
bind strangers. A mission can guide a profitable company; it cannot substitute
for the rights and agreements that company actually holds.[12]

Our business model is therefore not "free until we notice you." It is public
permissions with their actual duties, plus separately negotiated permissions
for a different, clearly described use. A profitable company and a durable
commons need not be enemies. They do need honest accounting.

## The model is not automatically the source

Artificial intelligence introduces a temptation to overstate the reach of a
software license. If code helped train a model, surely the model must inherit
the same obligations? If a server uses a neural network, surely its license
must require every training record?

Those conclusions do not follow automatically. Copyrightability, protected
expression, derivation, statutory exceptions and the boundaries of a covered
work are separate questions. AGPL does not explicitly supply a universal
training-data-and-weights disclosure rule. Neither do we claim that model
weights are categorically exempt in every factual setting.[1][2][4]

For a participating research or commercial partner, an accepted agreement can
do more precise work. It can identify a reproducibility package: specified
training or fine-tuning code, configurations, weights or adapters, evaluations,
provenance and lawful dataset descriptions. It can set publication deadlines
and reuse permissions. Where actual data can lawfully be released, it can
specify that too.

But private medical information is not converted into a public resource by our
enthusiasm for reproducibility. Credentials are not research artifacts.
Third-party confidential material is not ours to give away. Applicable privacy,
confidentiality and data rights must be respected, and exclusions must be
documented with their consequences rather than concealed behind an exaggerated
claim of complete openness.

We do not attach these additional contractual obligations to the AGPL and
pretend nothing has changed. Its sections 7 and 10 limit additional terms and
further restrictions. Keeping the public license standard protects users from
precisely the ambush we oppose.[4]

## A home for agreements, not a jurisdictional spell

AxiomZero selects Washington law and a King County forum for agreements that
expressly incorporate and validly accept its new commercial supplement.
Competent state courts in King County hear appropriate state-law matters;
where federal subject-matter jurisdiction exists, the selected federal forum
is the United States District Court for the Western District of Washington at
Seattle. Claims arising under federal copyright law belong in federal court
under 28 USC §1338(a).[7]

That is a real contractual choice. It is not a claim that every person who
downloads our code has consented to be sued in our home county.

We cannot add a mandatory forum condition to standard AGPL permissions and
declare the license unchanged. We cannot create a court's subject-matter
jurisdiction by agreement. We cannot unilaterally replace the forum clause
of an already executed agreement. Mandatory protections remain mandatory.

Washington's electronic transactions law recognizes that records and
signatures are not denied legal effect merely for being electronic, while its
application depends on parties' agreement to transact electronically. That
does not make every mouse click an authorized signature or every website
footer a binding contract.[8]

Washington courts also consider public policy and practical access to remedies.
In *Dix v. ICT Group, Inc.*, the Washington Supreme Court rejected a forum
clause that impaired Washington consumers' effective class-action remedy.
That does not invalidate every clause or guarantee ours. It reminds us that
the fairness of the actual bargain matters.[13]

The practical consequence is modest and important: preserve the accepted
version, identity and authority of the representative, and affirmative
acceptance. Make the selection visible before the bargain is made. A forum
chosen honestly is a place to resolve a disagreement. A forum buried in an
unaccepted notice is a litigation problem waiting to introduce itself.

## What our code can enforce

Our new local licensing gate checks the machine-readable policy and release
evidence. It can detect edits to the approved policy index, missing current
directories and document identifiers, an incomplete declared source
offer, or an attempted automatic exception approval. It returns structured
results. It does not call home, seize secrets, disable an installation or
inspect another company's systems.[9]

The difference between checking evidence and proving a legal conclusion matters.
A well-formed source URL can still point to an incomplete archive. A release
identifier can be inaccurate. A contract reference can be invented. The gate
does not fetch the source, establish derivation or authenticate signatures.
An exception request therefore stops for authorized agreement review rather
than being approved because a JSON field says "approved."

This is a deliberate division of responsibility. Automation makes our own
release process harder to neglect. Evidence and authorized human decisions
address questions the software cannot answer. A passing check is not a court
judgment with unusually good formatting. Nor does the gate interpret the prose
or prove that every historical sentence has been reconciled; that remains a
separate editorial and legal task.

If an actual violation needs attention, we preserve the relevant releases and
communications, verify ownership and the applicable permissions, assess the
facts, give appropriate notice and cure opportunities, and pursue available
remedies proportionately. Registration requirements and timing affect copyright
claims and remedies. Injunctions, damages, profits and fees depend on the
statutes and the case, not on the intensity of our adjectives.[10]

Nor does discovery automatically publish someone else's confidential records.
Relevant evidence may be obtained under judicial procedures and protective
orders. The objective is accountability for a covered obligation, not a public
search of every drawer.

## The promise we are making

We want people to build. We want them to know what they may do without asking.
We want required improvements to return through the applicable copyleft path,
and we want additional private permissions to be negotiated rather than
appropriated.

We also want the people maintaining the work to be able to earn a living.
Consulting, engineering, support, and carefully scoped exceptions can fund a
public project without disguising access as a gift and then demanding ransom.
Partnerships can create public value without pretending every contribution is
a cash payment.

The strongest licensing posture is not the most threatening one. It is the
one whose ownership can be shown, whose permissions can be read, whose
obligations can be understood, and whose claims remain inside the law.

That is the door we intend to keep open. Bring your curiosity. Bring your
business model. Bring your improvements under the terms that apply. If you
need a different bargain, let us make it in daylight.

### Gate Certification (v1)

**Evidence gate:** Legal mechanisms have explicit primary references; project
policy is labeled separately. No particular third-party violation is alleged.
Access or verification limits are not treated as proof of enforceability.

**Boundary gate:** Existing grants, third-party rights, private-use boundaries,
AI uncertainty, mandatory law and assent remain visible. No blanket jurisdiction
or disclosure of unrelated secrets is claimed.

**Human-readability gate:** The narrative explains public and commercial routes
before remedies, distinguishes privacy from enclosure, and makes profit and
reciprocity intelligible without disguising an obligation as an invitation.
These are editorial checks, not legal certification.
