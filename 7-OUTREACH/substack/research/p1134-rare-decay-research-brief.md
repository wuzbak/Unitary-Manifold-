# P1134 Research Brief — Rare B Decays and the Zeptoverse Framing

Date: 2026-11-05

## Research question

What did the recent rare-decay result establish, and what would be required
for it to test a Unitary Manifold prediction?

## Findings

On 3 November 2026, LHCb reported evidence for
\(B^0\to\mu^+\mu^-\): 3.0 standard deviations using Run 3 data and 3.2
standard deviations combined with Runs 1 and 2. This is evidence under the
conventional particle-physics terminology, not a 5-sigma observation or a
discovery. LHCb reports the result as consistent with Standard Model
expectations. The paper also reports improved measurements of
\(B_s^0\to\mu^+\mu^-\), including its effective lifetime.

Rare leptonic B decays constrain interactions in the weak effective theory.
Branching fractions, time-dependent observables, and ratios can be sensitive
to different operator contributions. Sensitivity alone does not identify a
new particle or distinguish among models.

“Zeptoverse” is outreach language used in university coverage distributed by
Phys.org; it is not the LHCb paper's name for a discovered sector. It refers
to the short-distance scales that can be probed indirectly through rare
processes.

No quantitative UM or Kaluza–Klein prediction is available for this result.
A valid comparison requires an explicit new-state spectrum, couplings to
quark and lepton currents, matching into a named weak-effective-theory
operator basis, predicted observables and uncertainties, and a comparison
against experimental likelihoods and Standard Model inputs. This pillar
implements generic statistical classification and comparison utilities only;
it supplies no UM prediction.

## Conclusion and next checks

The result is a useful future constraint, not confirmation of beyond-Standard
Model physics and not a test of UM as currently specified. The next valid
step is a theory calculation that supplies the missing spectrum, couplings,
operator matching, and uncertainty model. Any comparison should use the
collaboration's published likelihood and keep correlated uncertainties.
