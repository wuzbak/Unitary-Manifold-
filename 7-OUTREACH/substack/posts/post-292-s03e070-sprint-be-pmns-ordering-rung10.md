## Neutrino mass ordering: normal or inverted?

Neutrinos come in three flavours (electron, muon, tau), each with an associated mass eigenstate. The masses are tiny — far smaller than any charged particle — but they are not zero, as confirmed by oscillation experiments. What is not yet confirmed experimentally is whether the mass hierarchy is "normal" (ν₁ lightest, ν₃ heaviest) or "inverted" (ν₃ lightest, ν₁ and ν₂ nearly degenerate at the top).

This is the JUNO experiment's primary scientific goal. JUNO (Jiangmen Underground Neutrino Observatory, operating in China) measures the oscillation pattern of antineutrinos from nuclear reactors to determine the ordering. The Unitary Manifold makes a pre-registered prediction: normal ordering, based on the geometry of the c_L (left-chirality coupling) ladder in the 5D warp factor.

**PMNS_ORDERING_PROXY_OPEN → CLOSED**: The ordering proxy was formalized. The geometric argument: the c_L warp suppression factors, which determine the relative masses of the three generations of left-handed neutrinos, generate a mass ordering that is normal (lightest first) for the parameter space consistent with all other constraints. This is now a pre-registered, falsifiable prediction: if JUNO Year 2 returns inverted ordering, the framework is in serious trouble.

**KK_TOWER_ISW_OPEN → CLOSED**: The integrated Sachs-Wolfe (ISW) effect is a CMB temperature anisotropy generated by photons falling into and climbing out of potential wells as dark energy accelerates the expansion. The KK tower of massive graviton modes affects the ISW signal slightly. Sprint BE showed this effect is computable and small — it does not generate a new observable tension or resolve the amplitude gap. Closed as expected.

## Rung 10: the F-theory generation problem

Rung 10 is the F-theory extension rung — the program to embed the Unitary Manifold into F-theory, a twelve-dimensional framework in which the extra dimensions form a Calabi-Yau fourfold (CY₄). F-theory is a powerful unification framework developed since the 1990s, and embedding the Unitary Manifold into it would constitute a significant structural achievement.

The generation count problem in F-theory is formidable. In 7D compactifications, we count zero-modes of the Dirac operator on a T²/Z₂ orbifold. In F-theory, the relevant object is a "matter curve" — a complex curve inside the CY₄ where matter fields are localized. The number of generations equals the genus (the number of handles) of the matter curve.

**FTHEORY_RUNG10_SPECTRAL_COVER_GLOBAL**: The spectral cover construction gives the most systematic way to build F-theory compactifications with controlled matter curves. Sprint BE established the global spectral cover for the Rung 10 candidate geometry: a specific CY₄ base with an SU(5) gauge group from a stack of D7-branes. The matter curve is identified.

**FTHEORY_RUNG10_MATTER_CURVE_CY4**: The matter curve genus was computed on the reference CY₄. The result is relevant: the genus is large (order 10³ or more), which means a naive APS index would give far too many generations. The resolution requires understanding which zero-modes are projected out by the orbifold boundary conditions and flux.

Sprint BE established the framework for this computation but did not close it. The remaining blocker is the G₄ flux (a four-form flux on the CY₄ that is required for tadpole cancellation and can reduce the apparent generation count). The G₄ flux problem became the central theme of Sprints BF and BG.

## What is G₄ flux and why does it matter?

In F-theory on a CY₄, the geometry alone does not determine the physics. You also need to specify a four-form flux G₄ — a field configuration that pervades the compact dimensions. This flux:

1. Cancels a tadpole (a potential inconsistency that would make the equations of motion unsatisfiable)
2. Breaks supersymmetry in a controlled way
3. Lifts many of the zero-modes that would otherwise give unwanted massless particles
4. Constrains the chirality of matter fields

Getting G₄ right is the difference between an F-theory compactification that looks like the Standard Model and one that looks like something completely different. Sprint BE established the Rung 10 framework; Sprint BF would attack the G₄ problem directly.

## A note on the Rung structure

The Rung system is a transparency architecture. Each Rung is a numbered certificate that summarizes the state of a major outstanding problem. Rungs have statuses:

- **CLOSED**: The problem is solved with machine-checked proof
- **PARTIAL**: Progress made but unresolved aspects remain
- **ARCHITECTURE_LIMIT**: The problem is irreducible within the current framework (and is pre-registered as such)
- **OPEN**: Under active investigation

Rung 10's status at the end of Sprint BE: **PARTIAL** (spectral cover established, matter curve genus computed, G₄ problem identified as remaining blocker). This is an improvement from OPEN, but not yet CLOSED or ARCHITECTURE_LIMIT.
