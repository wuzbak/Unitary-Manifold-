# S04E008 — Sprint CA: The Coupled UV Lane (α_s and Higgs Together)

**Historical note:** this article documents how Sprint CA described the shared UV bottleneck in v35.7. The current repository reassessment narrows the evidentiary scope of these inherited residual figures: the `0.93` and `0.95` continuation scales are carried forward as historical assigned inputs, not new independently derived contractions. See `docs/TRUTH_LAYER.md#what-cannot-be-counted-as-progress`.

The useful idea in this lane is still real even after that narrowing: if two problems depend on the same missing ultraviolet object, they should not be narrated as independent victories. In this repository, the α_s lane and the Higgs lane are linked by exactly that kind of shared burden.

## What changed

Sprint CA kept those two lanes coupled on purpose.

- The repository treated α_s and Higgs as one shared-constraint problem rather than two separate chances to tell a success story.
- The lane preserved the historical continuation factors attached to the prior packet:
  - α_s assigned scale: **0.93**
  - Higgs assigned scale: **0.95**
- The packet exported a single joint diagnostic, `joint_bottleneck_pressure`, so a reader could see whether the common obstacle had actually disappeared.
- The current implementation still reports that the shared UV object is required and that the continuation outcome remains `CARRY_FORWARD_OPEN`.

That is the real editorial value of this lane: not that it solved the problem, but that it made selective storytelling harder.

## What did not change

- No shared UV object was derived.
- No hardgate label flipped to closed.
- `ALPHA_S_TYPE_B_FLOOR` and `HIGGS_MASS_ARCHITECTURE_LIMIT_WINDOW` remained open.
- The packet did not establish new scientific progress simply by inheriting historical residual numbers.

This last point matters most after the September 2026 reassessment. A carried-forward scale factor can be a traceability marker without being new evidence.

## Falsification implications

There are two different ways this lane can fail.

The first is scientific: a genuine joint UV completion could fail to reproduce either α_s or the Higgs mass under the repository's stated rules. The second is editorial: someone could quietly speak as if one side were solved while the shared object was still missing. Sprint CA's coupled-lane design exists to block that second failure mode.

So the proper takeaway is modest. The repository did not earn closure here. It made the burden structure more explicit and preserved the rule that a coupled problem stays coupled until the common object is actually in hand.

## Residual unknowns

- The UV derivation that would jointly constrain α_s and Higgs remains missing.
- The retained continuation factors do not by themselves prove contraction.
- Stability, matching, and moduli-fixing burdens remain part of any real closure path.
- The lane is still useful because it prevents isolated metric wins from being misreported as total progress.
