# S04E023 — Merlin Proof-First Sprint: Tightening the Kawamura Residual Without Pretending It Is Closed

This sprint is about one thing: reducing the final Kawamura-independence functional-analysis residual while refusing to narrate scaffolding as closure.

That distinction sounds pedantic until you watch how quickly projects lose track of it. Formal artifacts, traceability kernels, and burden-carry-forward logic are all useful, but they are not the same object as referee-grade discharge of the residual itself. The residual remains open, and keeping that sentence visible is half the integrity work.

So the method was intentionally narrow. One active burden, one machine-readable closure ledger, one dual-loop review structure, and one verdict discipline that allows only three outputs: closure earned, burden reduced, or still open.

Merlin’s role was bounded to pressure-testing the argument rather than narrating progress: generate alternative derivation attempts, search for counterexamples, surface hidden assumptions, reconcile provenance across proof surfaces, and block unearned closure language. In short, Merlin was allowed to challenge the claim, not award it.

What survived cross-audit is modest and useful. The final residual now sits in a dedicated burden ledger, dual-loop review enforces verdict-class convergence between human/Copilot and Merlin tracks, and new Lean4 bookkeeping invariants lock in four guardrails: residual open status stays explicit, traceability is not closure, cross-loop verdict agreement is required, and external-import boundaries remain visible.

That is real rigor work. It is not the closure proof.

The decisive unresolved item is unchanged: referee-grade Kawamura-independence functional-analysis closure remains open. This sprint reduced burden while making future self-deception harder.

That is exactly what it was supposed to do.
