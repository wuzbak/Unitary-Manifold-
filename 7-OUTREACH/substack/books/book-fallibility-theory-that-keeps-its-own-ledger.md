## Chapter 1 — Why a Failure Ledger Matters

Most speculative frameworks do not keep a first-class public record of their failure modes. They scatter caveats into appendices, bury them in footnotes, or delay them until peer review forces the issue.

The Unitary Manifold does the opposite. It maintains a canonical failure ledger in the repository itself, updates it in lockstep with every major sprint, and treats falsifiers as part of the theory rather than an inconvenience attached to it.

That discipline changes the meaning of progress.

A sprint is not successful because the number of pages grew. A sprint is successful if one of three things happens:

1. a real gap closes,
2. a false path is ruled out honestly,
3. a vague complaint becomes a precise architecture boundary.

Sprint BL is a good example of the third category.

## Chapter 2 — The Difference Between an Open Problem and an Architecture Limit

An **open problem** is a question that may still close inside the current framework.

An **architecture limit** is a question that the current framework has run out of structural room to answer.

Those are not the same.

An architecture limit is not an insult. It is a location. It tells us where the current mathematical machine stops being able to earn stronger claims.

The repository currently uses that distinction in a hard way:

- the **CMB amplitude** gap is treated as a confirmed irreducible architecture limit inside the audited EFT stack,
- **Jarlskog Layer-2** was explicitly forced into an architecture-limit verdict when the in-EFT cap audit failed to reduce the residual materially,
- the **flavor-family residuals** were reorganized in Sprint BL into one mapped UV flavor/moduli boundary rather than left as disconnected complaints,
- the **α_s Type-B floor** remains outside the tightened compactification window even after route hardening and external-ingestion discipline upgrades.

That is not pessimism. That is mapmaking.

The most serious internal mismatch remains the CMB acoustic-peak amplitude suppression.

This matters because it changes the quality of every future claim. Nobody gets to wave this away with “future work” unless the future work actually changes the architecture.
